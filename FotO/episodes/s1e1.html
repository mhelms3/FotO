Season 1, Episode 1: Rules are rules

There are no murders in Osprey Cove, or at least none that I can recall and none that anyone has told of. Until now, that is.

The morning after the storm Johedia found a dying man sprawled at the edge of town. Beaten by the storm, and with infected crossbow bolt wounds, and a strange sickness that left rust-red splotches all over his body, the man gasped out an unsettling warning before he died: “Iron Harbor... rust... ship... all dead... before... too late... ”

Or so Elder Ordiwi has told us. Us. The small gathering of people I had not met. A gnome, a goblin of all things, and a tall man, and a halfling who appeared already in his cups. Not that I am judging. Perhaps he had been up all night. Or something.

So here we are making awkward introductions. No, that is not quite true. I was nervous but the others seemed various degrees of indifferent, eager, or drunk. Ordwi has determined we should travel east to Iron Harbor, both to offer help as needed; the elders agreed that something dreadful has happened in our sister (and only neighboring) village. Perhaps it was just a pretext for us to extend overtures of peace and end the years long feud that Ha kept us apart, but Ordwi seemed quite concerned.

So we have headed out the Old Coast Road to Iron Harbor. Us. Headed out. Something unimaginable yesterday. And with strangers. Zunk-the indifferent one. A goblin, armed with knives, and not the kind meant for skinning fish. They seem to think this a game, and adventure to be claimed, so why not tag along. Nothing more interesting to do, they offer with a slight smile. But nice enough, I guess. I haven’t ever met a goblin before, or even seen one, so what do I know?

Hans is the gnome, and even if I did not know him for a stranger to Osprey, there is no way he would have been marked as one of us. Clothes as neat as can be, hands never to have seen a net or gaff or, well anything other than a pen, I should think. Or maybe a wand-he is a mage of some sort from a mainland school he seemed disappointed we did not know. But full of desire to be helpful and he was most earnest. And also most talkative, but it’s good. He makes up for my stammering.

There is also Cormac-a halfling drunk, but I am not sure whether “drunk” is a hobby or a profession to him. He and Oldwii know each other, and perhaps he is doing her a favor, or maybe, the other way around. He seems a man in need of a distraction.

The last is Tom Fidelis, a man of my height (tall, very tall for a woman) well armed and armored. A farmer he says, but on a sort of family quest to, well, to what is not entirely clear. But his attitude is one of irritation, as if all this is a chore like chopping the kindling or mending nets. Perhaps he has daddy issues. Not that I am judging; I have enough of my own.

By mid morning we had passed the farthest point from Osprey I have ever been. Only half a day to reach the end of my world; how strange. Hans kept up a constant stream of commentary on the plants, animals, clouds, rocks and whatever else caught his eye; his capacity to talk is endless. Cormac whistled tunelessly between swallows from his flask. I kept thinking about the oddness of it all, wondering if I would embarrass myself further in front of everyone, trying to imagine what Iron Harbor would be like, what we would see. That there was a rust grimed dead man was almost lost in my flighty stupid brain. I dared not open my mouth again-recalling the last idiotic comment I made before we set off: Well, he didn’t die of a fishing accident. Fish and fiddles-could I have been more useless? Of course the stranger didn’t die of an accident-he was plunked by bolts and his flesh red and splotchy! Hans was kind enough to only nod when I blurted this inanity out after inspecting the body. Yah Yah, but I shall take some samples in case they will be useful later, was all he said. But I am sure he thinks I am a moron. At the very least he thinks me callow.

We move along, Tom and I setting the pace. Luckily he does not seem interested in talking either.

After following only faint traces of the Old Coast Road, we approach Kindred’s Crossing, one of the few landmarks I know even if I have never been here. The road grows more obvious, and our boots clack on some of the remaining old paving stones. As the road winds through a small peninsula, we see a rocky beach with a small beat up rowboat overturned on the ground. Fearing someone might be injured underneath, I ran up to the boat and looked underneath.

Rule #1 of The Outside: No good deed goes unpunished. Underneath was not an injured person, or even nothing, but a nasty armored crabby-buggy thing with big pinchers. Clumsy oaf as I am, I bashed the already broken boat with my morningstar but at least managed to block the things blow with my shield, getting only a slight scratch. I heard a sizzle as something crackled by my ear, which turned out to be the gentlemanly Hans frying the things with some lighting. Huh. Didn’t see that coming. After the shock of actually being attacked, I came to my senses and thunked the thing hard, while Zunk stepped up with their clever knives and sliced it to bits, sending the thing into a spasm of twitching claws and legs, one of which stabbed the little goblin, turning it sickly. As if this was not at all quite enough a strange little creature burst from the grass and tried to prise something loose from the bags we had dropped. Stop-thief! yelled Hans firing another sizzling bolt as Tom stepped up and calmly sliced it in two with his pole arm. Farmer, right. I wonder if everyone in the outside is so lethal. Looking at the corpse I recognize it as a haniver; a sort of distant cousin to Zunk and much disliked for their tendency to steal, well anything.

I turn the rowboat over to make sure nobody is still inside, which seems daft now that I think of, but I feared some traveler might have been dragged under by the beast to be consumed at its leisure. But no, only an oil skin pouch with some coin (well, more than I would see in 3 months, if the truth be told) and a small vial holding a liquid that Hans declares would allow one to breath in water. What a fantastic thing for someone to be….just carrying around like that.

Stupid me-I forgot completely about the little gnome injured at my expense. Am I that dull witted as to let a few trinkets distract me from my wounded new friend? I take out my healing kit and try to rebalance their flow, but I know nothing about the nature of the poison; still I am able to accomplish something, and they start to look better. I hope my small boon makes up for me putting them at risk, and I give a little prayer of thanks to Milani.

Around the bend the remains of an old bridge now no longer crossing a channel into the bluff, salty water surging in and out, about 15 feet below us. The staves are long gone-only the posts remain. We grapple the post on the far side and tie the rope off on our side so that we may slither across with hands and feet. The only problem is Hans, who is obstreperous about crossing and loses his grip, managing to catch the rope at the last moment before he plunges into the foamy water. He comes up sputtering, but gamely tries it again, without incident.

We spy a stray cross bow bolt lodged in one of the bridge posts, and pull it out. The bolthead is slicked with something that Hans sniffs, stating he thinks it is the same thing found on the wounds of the murdered stranger-the bolts themselves certainly look similar. Hans wants to collect more samples, but I point out we are still somewhat far from Iron Harbor and we have just enough time to get there before full dark.

On the move again, as fast as is safe, we are beset again by another cowardly foe hidden in the grass; an arrow plants itself in Zunk’s shoulder and the little goblin pitches forward into the dirt as some sort of giant centipede slithers up the seaward rim of the trail (which is now running beside a sandy defile). I recall one of my first rituals and murmur some words as I throw some pieces of forge metal that turn into bolts and pierce the chittering thing threatening Ordwi. A companion appears. Cormac moves towards the pair, his steps in no way resembling a drunkard, but a dancer, but I fear he has lost his mind. He has no weapon or armor or shield. He evades a pincer with a deft twist and pummels the beast with blows from fist and foot and sends it to the dirt, as Tom steps up with his long blade and cuts the other in two.

The bowman has revealed itself and is standing on a small rise in the defile, about 5 feet from the trail rim, with a clear shot at any of us. Small in stature, dressed in black, full of malice. Tom half leaps to blackie, trips the little creature (halfling? Dwarf?) and reversing the blade, stakes it to the earth. Farmer my…ash.

Zunk is breathing but unresponsive and we debate resting in the small cave we found, currently occupied by dead creature the same as we just fought. A darrow, someone said, kind of an underground dwarf, which accounts for the unnaturally large eyes and the palor. Sunlight must hurt them to judge by the burns on the dead one in the cave.

It did not seem wise to spend the night here, so I offered to carry the little goblin till they can recover. Weighed less than 2 nets, I’ll wager, and I often carried three to and from the dock. I put them under my arm like a loaf of bread and we moved on at a good pace. Nobody seemed bothered by the sudden death of this afternoon, like it happened all the time? Is this what I can expect in The Outside?

Sometime later Zunk awoke, and I put them carefully down when it became clear they well enough recovered. I’m umm, sorry, I stammered. It was. Well. The best way all considering. Iron Harbor surely is a better place to spend the night then a cave with a dead derro.

Ahh, it’s alright-ya did what needed to be done. Quite comfortable, really. Like I was wee again, in swaddling.

I didn’t know what to say to that, so I just blinked.

Rule #2 of the Outside: Things often are not what they seem
