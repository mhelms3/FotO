Season 2, Episode 10: Familial Complications, Part 1

We headed to the townhall in morning to meet Sheriff Hemlock, Mayor Deverin, Brodert and Abstalar in the hopes we could figure out a way forward. There was no good answer. Everyone agreed Ripnugget was a threat but a visible response would panic the townies, so Mayor and Sheriff would agree only to fortify the guards at the lighthouse-hellfire flume and the catacombs. We would go investigate the burning horseshoe prints of the dread “Sandpoint Devil”-everyone thought this a prank of some sort but perhaps it was connected to the cult that seemed to be residing in the pit. We heard for the 40th time that “there are always darned cults kicking up trouble”. Oh and also that travelers had been disappearing from the road in the same vicinity of the farms that had been visited by whatever it was that left the hoof prints, which was all near the wishing well.

“What?” we all said

“Well, erm” offered Hemlock “didn’t I mention that earlier? Must have slipped my mind” I wondered if too many sausages interfered with common sense.

That was the best plan we had at the moment, and so the meeting broke up and we made to leave. We were about to make our way through the busy morning bustle when I heard Hans suck in his breath. “Ohh no ohh no ohh no, what is she doing here now? This cannot be happening. And then a squeak-there is no other word- and “ohmygodohmygodohmygod-the two people I least want to see. This is too much”

“What, Hans?” I really wasn’t paying attention. A large fancy carriage was turning around in the square making its way to us, taking no heed of the foot traffic. I was worried it would run over someone.

“I’m sorry Hans. You were saying?”

He looked at me with wide eyes and licked his lips “Jah, ah, I was saying I need to RUN. Now.” And he turned around and scurried back into the building. There was a puff of smoke and he was gone.

What the heck? The carriage definitely heading our way, and I could see quite a few people inside its bulk. I turned to ask if anyone knew what was going on, and noticed Zunk also was gone. I stomped my foot. What was going on?

The clatter of the iron-shod wheels died down as the black carriage settled heavily onto its springs in the center of the public square. The nearly run over townies were tense and silent as the door with the silver "S" swung open, and Titus Scarnazzi steped out, looking like that cat that ate the canary. He was clearly plotting something. I wanted to punch him, to be honest.

At least his grand entrance was immediately derailed by the chaos of the passengers behind him.

First out was a middle-aged goblin man, Gorfang as he was later named, practically bursting from a traveling coat that was three sizes too large and singed heavily at the cuffs. His large twitching ears twitch and unfocused joy as he gazed up at the architecture of the town hall marked him as a frantic, wide-eyed tinkerer. Under one arm, he tightly clutched a brass-bound leather case leaking the sharp, unmistakable odor of sulfur and cheap alchemical solvent. I am sure he and Hans, wherever he had gotten to, would get along merrily.

“Oh, the limestone compaction here is magnificent, Titus! Truly!” he squeaks enthusiastically. “And the thermal draft from the harbor? The Hellstorm Flume arrays will achieve optimal oxygenation within three days of digging! The math is flawless!”

A trim sharp eyed goblin woman emerged—lean, weathered, and wrapped in a sturdy, patched leather mantle. Her eyes weren't tracking the architecture but scanning the crowd like a hawk mapping a snake-pit, immediately followed by intense calculations. It sort of reminded me of how Zunk looked at things.

"Ah, Sheriff Hemlock, Mayor Deverin," Scarnazzi announced loudly enough to reach everyone in the gawping crowd. "Allow me to introduce the crown jewels of my new defense initiative. Master Alchemist Gorfang and his lovely wife, Venke. I have personally chartered them from the southern syndicates to oversee the subterranean excavations. They tell me their specialized expertise in blasting-powders will...accelerate our underground digging. Sandpoint will be made secure!"

Venke looks back at the smirking Titus, her face carefully neutral. "We are honored to work for the Scarnazzi interest, my Lord. We should proceed to our quarters to organize the blasting ledgers immediately. Introductions can wait until the work is secured."

Scarnazzi smirks, enjoying everyone’s confusion

“Yes, quite,” Scarnetti says, the smile not reaching his eyes “The laborers are eager to begin...”

Since I was being deliberately ignored, I stepped up and interrupted him, quite loudly.

“What? You just can’t march in here and order people around” I looked to the mayor, who was holding herself quite rigidly. “Can he?”

“Oh yes I can, girl. (the sneer breaks the icy politeness). Of course, since none of the so called town elders are acting to protect us, I have brought minds untainted by the... provincial stagnation of our local institutions to direct this grand and necessary infrastructure project”.

He stepped aside, gesturing broadly to the carriage door. From it came Dr. Horatio Thrum. (He has a ridiculously large badge identifying himself, with the imprint of the magic academy Hans attended). He’s overdressed, over-arch and seems like a real prick as he looks down his unfortunately large nose at the town and its assembled people. Behind him a prim and spare woman steps down with mathematical precision. She holds her silver-trimmed ledger like a weapon. Ugh.

“Allow me to introduce Dr. Horatio Thrum,” Scarnazzi intones announces, his eyes gleaming as they lock onto the town leadership. “Director of the newly chartered Sandpoint Geofluvial Consortium. And his brilliant colleague, whom he personally recruited last year from the prestigious Faculty of Applied Jurisprudence in Pezzack—Ms. Vendora Vax, our lead arcane and legal specialist.”

“Brodert,” Thrum says, clearly relishing the quesy look on Brodert’s face. “They told me you were still topside, but I confess, I expected to find you retired to an asylum by now. Still babbling about Thassilonian geometry and old light towers, I see?”

Brodert blinks, his jaw tightening as he clutches his chaotic bundle of notes closer to his chest. “Thrum. Your economic models are a farce, and your understanding of the local fault lines is—”

“My understanding is backed by imperial charter and functional physics, old man,” Thrum interrupts ruthlessly. “You are a backwater hedgemage,” he sneered, stepping directly into Brodert’s personal space, towering over him. “The Sandpoint Geofluvial Consortium isn't here to play at archeology. We are here to reshape this valley. I suggest you stay out of the sub-levels, Brodert. The math required for our excavations would only give you a headache.”

I whisper frantically to the mayor. “How can they do this? What about the town council? Who appointed him king?” This last quite loudly.

Vax cuts me off, sharply.

“Silence girl. Dr. Thrum's parameters are absolute,” Vax says, like a school marm to an unruly class. Absolute command. “I have personally reviewed the regional shipping charters, municipal boundaries, and subterranean rights dating back to the foundational cheliaxian grants. Legally and arcanely, the Scarnozzi interest has total jurisdiction over the Hellstorm Flume project. Any municipal interference—or rogue, unlicensed arcane activity within the municipal perimeter—will be met with immediate asset seizure and provincial injunctions.”

She turns towards the mayor and honestly, I want to punch her as much as Scarnozzi. “Having discerned you might use bureaucratic incompetence and inertia as an excuse for inaction, I present the required documentation is already prepared. Three identical copies of the geofluvial blueprints, the Pezzack-stamped safety certifications, and the Scarnozzzi indemnity bonds. Delivered to you now, on the courthouse steps. As by law provision 2.106 subsection etc, you have 24 hours to review the documents. If no formal injunction is issued by that exact second, the excavation begins by default of law—and any subsequent interference will be classified as economic sabotage.”

Vax shuts the leather book she has been consulting and the sound is more like the thunk of a butcher’s axe.

Mayor Kendra Deverin doesn't flinch. Holy cow. I am in awe and do not know if I am worthy of the respect and warmth she has shown me.

“My, my, Dr. Thrum,” Kendra says, her voice sounding smooth and entirely unbothered. “It is always a pleasure to see the southern academies taking such a sudden, passionate interest in our humble coastal plumbing. Sandpoint is truly honored.”

She turns her eyes directly to Vendora Vax.

“As for regional charters and subterranean rights, Ms. Vax—imperial grants or no, all non-municipal excavations within the Sandpoint perimeter must conform to local civic safety codes. I shall make my administrative determinations and appraise you of the results. Until then, any action on your part shall constitute a material breach of your claim”

Vendora Vax’s jaw tightens slightly. If she expected Deverin to be cowed, she was not as smart as she looked.

“Where is that gnome” the mayor muttered “he’s always underfoot precisely when he’s not needed”.

“Uhhh” I ventured “I think maybe he had to use the privy. Bad. Maybe all the sausage rolls upset his stomach”. It was the best I could do. Zunk could have lied much better if they were here, and Tom was his silent self. He seemed mildly bored.

I had had enough of Hansplaing, even if it was coming from a woman, so I got into Scarnazzi’s smirking face I wanted to punch so so badly. “What are you really after” I challenged. “I know this is not for the town’s sake-that’s not you. What’s going on in that calculating brain”.

“Pffft girl.” More smirking. “You are out of your depth. Leave this to the gownups and do something useful you can actually do. Have a baby. You can do that, can’t you?. Be a good girl”

He almost had me but overplayed his hand. Bullies want you to be scared and needy, and I was not going to give him the satisfaction. I didn’t take the bait.

“Hah” I tried a plucky chuckle but wasn’t sure it came off. “You’re just mad about how we beat you at the town council, and you’re so mad about it you’ll endanger the town.”

The mask slipped, just for a moment. Maybe I was the only one who saw it, but still.

In the awkward silence, Venke declared too loudly "Lord Scarnozzi, where will we be posted exactly? I should like to get to work forthwith." She unexpectedly gives me a look, but I have no idea what it means.

Titus responds, "Yes, yes. And well we should. I shall afford you the generous pleasure of my own manor as your working quarters. Come now. Our business here is at a conclusion, and we shall be off."
