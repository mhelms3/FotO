Season 1, Episode 5: Death is Easy. It Is Living That Is Hard.

In the morning, we took Elsie up on her offer of supplies, buying some potions to resist plague and the like, and I found a set of heavier armor that fit well. We decided a visit to the ship was in order first, to tell First Mate Janice that we could not find her captain, but we had not given up. We told her what we had learned of Metrimar and asked to search his quarters in case there was something there that could help, but she declined (rather impolitely at that), instead offering us the last pages of the Captain’s log. He had become suspicious of Metrimar, with the last straw being the portage of some terrible beast to the island. The log’s description was too fantastic to be believed; a 6 legged thing with a plant like head. The horrors of this man seems endless. We have to stop him.

Nothing had changed at the temple/fortress overnight. I suppose we should have told the men to give the bodies a suitable burial to send them on their way. Poor souls. I hope they find peace.

We made for the tunnel in the basement and followed it for a long time. It was a good distance and seemed from our best reckoning to be heading out under the bay to the island. Zunk crept in front, hopefully to warn us of any dangers, but there were none. We barely noticed when the raw stone walls merged into some built hallway.

Entering a larger space, we are confronted with a large rusty door, runes inscribed on its archway. Thallasonian, says Hans, who translated it as Sky Metal Workshop. There was a spiky horseshoe shape carved into the rusty metal. There was also a hallway leading out.

Zunk regarded the door and moved closer to detect any traps, then stopped. “This feels bad” they say “Like me bones are rusting from the inside. Jhys, my girl, step to me and see if you feel the same. You’re the only one among us who have been touched by the rust, it may be different for you”

It was not. It felt black and red and horrible and I quickly moved back.

The door did not have any visible lock, so Hans offers to push the door with magic. We positioned ourselves and Hans murmured some words. A disturbance in the air in front of the door, not wind, but more like the ripple of clear water and the door was pushed back. Resourceful Hans!

Rusty red-black tentacles shot from the door as it opened, making right for Hans, as if some squid thing was questing. In solidity, they were somewhere between smoke and water and one grabbed Hans around the wrist, and quickly retreated, vanishing as it went.

Hans looked at his wrist and shrugged. “Jah, jah, I am fine. Maybe it did not like my taste”

Beyond the door is wide hallway made of black marble, with murals on the east and west walls. Again, Hans recognizes what it means. They show Rune Lord Belmarius conquering people and other rune lords, supplicants at his feet praising his victory and begging for mercy. Scenes of bloody chaos in the distance. What a massive plonker!

Large double doors are at the other end, and about halfway through two berusted metal statues of bullish horn-headed beasts that turn slowly and step off their pedestals towards us. Zunk fires arrows, and they are as clever with them as they are with a dagger. The first arrow blows a piece off thing’s chest, revealing cogs and gears. I had heard of such magically animated constructions but never thought I would actually see one. It would be a wonder if it wasn’t trying to rip us apart.

Zunk’s second arrow pierces the gap created by the first arrow releasing a spray of metal bits and it falls stiffly to the ground, like a wound down toy. The other advances on Zunk and hits it hard, wounding them badly. Why must that brave little one keep doing things like this?

Tom steps up to aid our friend, tripping the thing so it’s flat on its belly and I hammer it hard. It crushes like an empty cannister, gears and springs flying everywhere. Holy smokes-that felt…good!

I heal Zunk and whisper “Do not expose yourself so, stay behind me until they are distracted. You are brave and clever, but also soft and small” I hoped they were not offended, but better for it to be mad at me then dead.

Nothing further threatened us in the hall, so we made to the doors at the far end, Zunk inspecting them carefully before opening them.

The room beyond was large, with a soaring domed ceiling, thirty feet tall. The floors were of marble floors and stairs lead up to a central dais, with the surrounding floor stained with old blood. Upon the dais rose a twisted apparatus of strange metals. Motifs of a seven-pointed star appear on the many doorways, and on all sides of the altar, as do images of spiky horseshoes of rusted iron. There is an air of grave and dangerous ceremony.

By the flickering light provided by the numerous brands smoking in their sconces, we see a dwarf with a hammer and 3 men with daggers, the horseshoe sign emblazed on their clothes. The dwarf, who I take to be Ragnulf intones. “Welcome, you are the first supplicants to grace Zar Azmak, we will kill you now in offering” and he steps toward us raising his hammer.

Okey dokey, if that’s the way you want it.

Tom puts himself in the fore, and his deadly blade cleaves the dwarf badly, but he is undeterred and smashes Tom in the face, rocking him backwards. I see a flare of energy as Tom’s protections buckle, but at least they kept him alive. Daggers fly from the 3 men, but most miss their mark; a couple clang off my armor. Zunk guts one of the dagger-men like a fish. I wish they wouldn’t put themselves in danger like that; he is surrounded now and with Ragnulf in front of me, there is naught I can do. They are at least holding their own and have bloodied one of the ones attacking him.

Hans conjures more tricks, and the men and the dwarf are slammed by invisible fists. One of them falls, and his comrade snarls. “You will all DIE. my lord only needs to touch you to rust you”. What is it with these people?

I don’t know if Tom can take another hit, but I am worried about the dwarf with the hammer. I backhand the dwarf with my flail and, Milani be praised, he goes down. I bestow Milani’s grace on Tom, and he kindly steps up and puts one of the dagger-men down. The other, now wisely afraid, runs for one of the doors. Fish and fiddlesticks! He’s going to call for allies! I hear Hans murmuring “Jah, I’m afraid I can’t let you do that” and an invisible fist smashes the fleeing man against the wall and he slumps to the floor.

I patch up Tom some more and regard Ragnulf. Luckily, he is not beyond saving; I recall the blacksmith and Vanda saying he was a good man, somehow swayed by the Thalassonian merchant, so I tend to his wounds, after of course, we tie him up. He may not deserve death, but neither are we stupid. I consider the others, my eye catching the symbol on their clothes. No, it seems they have chosen this path and so walk it they must.

Ragnulf’s eyes flutter open.
