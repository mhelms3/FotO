Season 2, Episode 6: In Defense of a Good Man

Visiting the pit was next, but we had to make sure everyone important knew what we found and so went to Brodert’s. He was most keen to take a group of rune plonkers down there now that we had made it safe. We omitted mention of course, of Woogie and his primitive tribe/civilized mob, depending. We trusted they would remain hidden and this would avoid further bloodshed and said no more.

Rule #4, Its ok to not tell someone something they don’t need to know, and which might harm them if they did. Like not telling an eight y/o boy about the can of kerosene, matches and rags, is not a lie.

Zunk asked about the Scarnazzi, and learned the four founding families of the town were, in addition to the Scarnazzi: House Kaijitsu, House Deverin and House Valdemar. Kendra Deverin is currently the mayor. There is some political wrangling among houses regarding the renewal of the tithe, and the Scarnazzis appear against it, while the church seems to represent voices on the other side, even if they have no official position. Still, that explains, as Brodert suggested earlier, the trouble making.

Then to Hemlock. We agreed an indirect approach was wise; it would raise uncomfortable questions about how we knew of the events surrounding the maybe death (again) of Ripnugget. Basically rule 4, again. So we would ask him of news and perhaps come round to an opening. Wanting to stay right footed, I suggested we bring him some lunch from Sandpoint Savories. The shop woman, Alma was glad to see us, especially as we were making another big order, and I used this as an opportunity to inquire about the pit-maybe some adventurers stopped here for provisions.

Alma instantly clammed up and would talk no further. I thought she might be concerned for our safety and tried to be reassuring, but I had stepped over some line. As they ushered me out, somewhat abruptly, Zunk reminded me of our first night in the Rusty D where they had mentioned some girl that seemed to be on the minds of the townsfolk; she had taken up with adventurers from Magnamar and had not been seen for awhile. Stupid me-I had blundered right into it and then didn’t have the good sense to shut up. No doubt daughter and the pit were connected via the adventurers, and it didn’t seem a good place to have been. I was sorry to upset the shop woman. But still, it was useful information.

Next on our list was a visit to the Curious Goblin to keep in the good graces of Chesty. He was quite happy to see us, really, and admitted he was not able to stay mad at Zunk. We made small talk, till Zunk remarked that we were curious about the pit, and no doubt a renowned adventurer like Chesty would have heard of it.

“Ah” chortled Chesty “Indeed I have, and I will tell you everything I know about the pit...”

Perfect!

“over dinner. Tonight. 5:00 at the Rusty Dragon. Will you be there, Zunk?’

“Aye, I’ll share a meal and a flagon with ya. My treat even. I’ll be there”.

“Will you?” mused Chesty. “Well, we will see, shan’t we? I look forward to it.”

We left he Goblin with one more errand-a trip to some curiosity shop called The Feathered Serpent to see if we could find useful items for our jaunt to what likely would be a horrible place. The proprietor, one Vauxhall Voon, was most enthusiastic salesperson. Tom bought a rope that he wrapped around his waist like a belt; fisherman are sometimes so, but I doubt they would go and buy a bit of scrap for this purpose, but what do I know?

We arrived back at the Dragon with about an hour to spare; Zunk had bought some soap at the curio shop and had in mind to bathe. It was cute he wanted to spruce up for Chesty, even though, sort of..ick. But Bethana accosted us as soon as we came in, all fluttery and tense. It seemed they (the town guard) had arrested Abstalar; he was accused of performing a dangerous necromantic ritual and recklessly endangering the town. We needed to speak on his behalf, now!

Fish and fiddlesticks-there was no way this would be over before 5:30; Zunk was going to stand Chesty up again-he would be crushed. It would be good to keep him on our side, and besides, he didn’t deserve this. But what could we do?

“C’mon mates” said Zunk “lets go to the Goblin-it’s on the way and we can make it in time. Let’s see if Chesty is interested in a little entertainment before dinner”.

Okey dokey!

Chesty was there, and happy although surprised to see us. A little wary, maybe? “So, you see” started Zunk, “We’e going to dine, but maybe a little later then we planned. We gotta go help Abstalar beat some scyvy rap someone’s trying to pin him with. Accused of endangering the town, and I was there and it ain’t the way it went down, and we all gotta say so. But well, you being a learned and wise town poet, well, I was wondering if you wanted to come along? (Butter wouldn’t melt in his mouth, my Da would say). The town could use your counsel, I am sure, and Abstalar your help. Up for a little adventure?”

Chesty’s face had been fixed the entire time right after “but maybe a little later”, mouth tight and down curving. But he suddenly laughed hard out loud, the most genuine sound he had uttered. “well Zunk, aren’t you an interesting one! It sounds grand. Lets do this!” We tore out the Goblin, sprinted to the townhall, Tom jamming his foot in the door before it closed in our face.

The guard was mildly offended, but on second look, he stepped aside and we were escorted into a box on the side. The whispers began. “That one with the dark eyes says he’s a farmer, but that ain’t no hoe, and it looks like he knows how to use it”. “That little one there, he fights with three blades, they say” “Oh..see the painted red rose-that’s the Priestess of Milani. I could convert for that”. ”They say that little one with the big nose took down a demon by sneezing fire”

Who comes up with this stuff?

The crowd went quiet as the Mayor opened the proceedings; she was irritated at the Scarnazzi Counselman and punctuated her remarks with looks at him sharp as Zunk’s knife. But she had no choice except to play by the book. Titus Scarnazzi looked like the cat that ate the canary. I recognized the scumbags from the temple along with smooth-talker (Cam Scarnazzi); the family resemblance to Titus was unmistakable. Son perhaps, or nephew.

Devlin read the charges; necromancy, reckless endangerment of the town, and assembling in a religious gathering greater than 3 without a permit. Hans nodded glumly at the last. “Tsk” he murmured “they at least could have gotten a permit.” Devlin had to gavel the crowd quiet several times as Scarnazzi thugs shouted “Defiler” and “Body snatcher” and the like.

Abstalar was called to make his statement, and he explained clearly and calmly the aim, the safety of the ritual, and the immense benefit of closing this sad chapter of the town’s history. He pointed out that in fact, nothing ill had occurred, despite the dangerous disruption. “You have these four to thank for that, but they were there because I asked them to be. I expected someone might cause trouble. And you (pointing at Cam) did exactly that-you are the one who endangered the town! I am trying to keep it safe by putting to rest a shade that darkened us. We summoned only a spirit that could not affect this realm. You were the problem.”

A few cries of “Necromancer” “Ghost-caller” and “Black Priest” rang out and Devlin again used her gavel. There was more than a little muttering, and it did not sound sympathetic. Devlin now looked at us, saying “Is it true what we have heard, that you were present at this event?” We nodded. “Then” she continued “We direct you to give witness. Start with your name, and tell us, in your own words, what has occurred”

Tom when first and I had a moment of panic, but he presented himself as Tom Sylvanus-a common enough name. He affirmed the truth of Abstalar’s account and wondered why the town did not trust their Priest in the manner of religious affairs, or to doubt Abstalar’s opinion on the threat it posed. What about all the births Abstalar had attended, the wounds healed, the souls laid to rest? Tom also stated he saw no threat until the attempted disruption by an angry mob and doubted the neutrality of the Scarnazzi claim. The hostility of the Scarnazzi to renewing the tithe was a clear motive to do mischief, and that, more so Abstalar’s intent, was the question to be posed, he offered.

More angry shouts, but this time, I heard a few whispers, recounting this or that good deed on Abstalar’s ledger. And a few comments on Scarnazzi machinations in the past.

Hans went next, again offering the same account. He questioned how recklessly disrupting a sacred ritual would lead to safety and that these actions were not those of someone concerned for the town. It was the most unwisest choice, he argued, and against the town safety code!

“Liar” blurted Cam “Nothing happened there because of us”

“Nonsense” countered Hans “We completed the ritual safely-you did nothing but endanger it without cause”

“Hah, so you admit the ritual was completed? Necromancer” he yelled. More echoes from the crowd: “exile the traitor priest” “he has soiled Desna-burn him!” but a lot of them came from the Scarnazzi scumbags.

“Well yes” replied Hans, quite unperturbed “That was never in dispute-try to keep up young man. And I note you have not offered a suitable explanation for your poor decision making, not to mention the potential code violation, jah. We’re you completely and inexplicably unaware of the danger you posed, or did you attempt it for some purpose?

Cam’s face reddened, and more shouts from the toughs, but further whispering, no longer quiet. “Well, he’s got them there” I heard. One man shouted “You’d better watch out, Cam, that un’s mind is as sharp as his nose”

I went next. I was too stupid to argue like Tom or Hans, but I knew what I knew. “I’m a priestess of Milani” I started “so I know something of these rituals. As the others have said, we called a shade only to talk, and so the town and Nualia could heal. What harm there? Abstalar intended only to do good, and we helped, so we are guilty too and I am not ashamed of it. “

I was interrupted by shouts of “vile moon-priest”, and “Rose-slut” and worse. “Shut up” I yelled directly back “I will say what I will say and you will hold your worm tongue until I am done. Why do men always think they can silence a woman by shouting loudest and calling her a whore?”

I saw a slight smile on the face of the Mayor and heard “You preach, sister” from the crowd. Not everyone here was a man. The elder Scarnazzi’s jaw was tight, and he stared balefully at Cam, who shuffled uneasily in his chair.

“Anyway, during these rituals, one cannot shield their earthy thoughts. I know for an absolute fact Abstalar acted only out of love of the town and its people. These people (and I pointed at Cam and his toadies) are the ones trying to divide you. Why do you let them?”

Having no clever finish, I sat down and hoped I did not harm our cause.

Zunk stood and introduced himself-his full name was impressively long. “I’m just a wandering goblin from the North, and I don’t know shit about your town. But I do know we did a job for Abstalar, digging up some old bones so he could do what you have heard-keep the town safe... I, uh, don’t use three knives, by the way. I am working on two. That’s how Promqeth defended the town, am I right”?

“Aye, that he did” someone shouted and from another corner “You tell ‘em, Billy Barrett”.

“Well” drawled Zunk “If it’s good enough for him, well, it’s good enough for me”

Nods all around. It was fascinating how Zunk had gotten their attention so quickly and completely. He seemed enlarged in stature even though he had not grown an inch and his voice deepened and filled the room, even though it had not changed in pitch or volume. But Zunk was filled with conviction, and everyone saw it. Zunk was persuasive, but never like this and I leaned back to the row behind me to ask Chesty what he thought. Only Chesty, among all, was not looking at Zunk. He sat with his eyes half closed, murmuring quietly but intensely. I was beginning to suspect that not all about his adventuring past was an empty boast.

Zunk continued “Well, like I said, I don’t know about this town and I got no dog in this fight as I say. But I know when someone is being played, and let me tell you friends, you ‘uns are most definitely being played. You’ve heard from Abstalar and my friends about how nothing was amiss until this lot stuck their noses in, yet here they are stirring the pot. It ain’t for me to say why, but I say it’s so; they be telling you that you ain’t seen what you’ve seen and don’t know what you know. Up to you to decide whether you’re gonna let that happen. But well, I don’t see why you would, if you’re asking me”

And he sat down. There was not sound, except for the snapping of a pencil in Counselman Scarnazzi’s hands. It was like a tree falling in the forest.

I leaned back again and whispered to Chesty “Well, that Zunk sure is something, eh”?

“Oh indeed, most persuasive, magically so if I may say, don’t you think”?

“Well, that was just Zunk being Zunk, you know?” I am not good at sarcasm, but I think Chesty took my meaning well enough.

“I am sure that had something to do with it too” replied Chesty, and at that point I whispered “Thank you-you are not so bad underneath, I think”

“Ah, does that mean you will join us for dinner?”

“Absolutely!”

“Excellent! I love a threesome-so much more interesting, right?”

“Don’t get ahead of yourself, Chesty, just take the win”. He smiled.

Mayor Devlin asked the room to be cleared so the assembly could make their decision and the five of us and Abstalar were led away to wait. I saw Cam skulk out-he was not looking happy. It did not take long for us to be called back in and I could see Devlin no longer looked troubled, but Scarnazzi looked like that canary had not gone down smoothly after all. Cheers erupted as the verdict was read - not guilty on the first two charges, the permit charge dropped in light of the disruption. “A fair verdict” whispered Hans “only it would have been more appropriate to confirm the lack of a permit, bu waive the fine, if you aske me. Rules are rules”

Scarnazzi moved rapidly to leave. He looked in our direction as he did, and I was surprised it was not venomous. Instead, he gave us a brief nod of respect; a player acknowledging, at least for now, they were outplayed. Nothing, however, indicated this was over.

Everyone was of course joyful, and it was clear that the mayor was particularly delighted at the outcome, both Abstalar’s exoneration and the dent in the Scarnazzi’s plans. “But” she cautioned, “words are weapons too, and you have wielded them exceptionally well to wound Titus and embarrass that cur who does the dirty work. You must be careful of drawing even more attention to yourselves. The town has not seen your like in nearly twenty years, and tongues are wagging. Keep your heads down”. Wait-what? Was she actually comparing us to Vistaro, Stan, Promqeth and Viktor?

Devlin was only partially satisfied we were going to the Pit, muttering that getting killed was not her idea of keeping one’s head down. It didn’t help when Hans pointed out being buried would fulfill that objective. Zunk eventually interrupted the discussion by pointing out that he and Chesty had a delayed dinner to attend.
