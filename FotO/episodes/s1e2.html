Season 1, Episode 2: How to make friends and influence people

We made Iron Harbor around nightfall, but managed to at least see the lay of it before full dark. Bigger than Osprey, it spreads out on a nicely sized bay with an island in the center with spires that seemed different in style from the rest of the buildings around the harbor, of the type you would see in Osprey. Not the same was the giant walled compound nestled in the bluff to the north; you could fit nearly ½ of Osprey in its footprint. A small baylet just on the outskirts held 3 grand looking buildings but either unfinished or in great decline. Two lacked roofs, and the smallest most complete looked mashed together with mismatched parts.

We were hailed by an imposing well armored figure as we approached. She named herself Vanda and looked completely in charge, standing bestride the street awaiting our reply with no unease. It was hard not to notice the huge sword strapped to her back. I might have seen the symbol of Gorum, but could not ne certain.

We whispered a bit between ourselves and eyed Ordwii, who turned and addressed Vanda, explaining the mysterious dead stranger, his dire warning, and the resolve of Osprey to travel to our sister village to put the past behind us and offer what aid we might.

I held my breath, not knowing how she would react; the silence between our two villages was long and deep and allowed plenty of time to build mistrust. But she was most generous, with heartfelt thanks, and agreeing it might be time for a healing. It could not have gone better! She explained the dead man was named Blanton, who had taken sick one day suddenly seemed to have left his mind and disappeared from the village. But the manner of his death was greatly worrisome to her and was something else she wished to discuss.

Vanda invited Ordwii to confer with them in the Temple of Gorum (ah, so I had seen rightly) with the council of Iron Harbor. They occupied the walled compound we had mistook for a fortress (well, Gorum has always gone in for brutalist architecture to embody their martial discipline, according to Hans anyway. If it’s a fancy way of saying they have grim ideas on what home should like, I agree). Ordwii suggested we find ourselves an Inn while she and Cormac (who had been mistaken for Ordwii’s manservant and decided not to contest the point) meet with the council. Given the amount to discuss it would take awhile. It would be a kind gesture to not burden them with us to feed and house. Besides, I have never stayed in a real inn before! As she strode away, I wondered what it would be like to have such magnificence.

Stupidly, I was so besotted I forgot to ask Vanda where the Inn would be. Everyone did! Hans was distracted by the architecture, grumpily commenting that the dry dock should be there and not there, while Zunk was seemingly more interested in that rock formation, or clump of thick grass or that shadowy doorframe. After sizing up Vanda, Tom was kicking listlessly at some pebbles,

Rule #3 of the Outside: Don’t depend on anyone to speak up if you fail to do so.

We found a pub at least, with a few dockers among the well-worn tables (well rickety to be honest-but it’s probably hard to get furniture here, so I am not judging). Hans and Zunk decided to start building good feelings with our sister villagers by ordering a round for all, using some of the coin providence provided us when we found the boat. To good cheer!

The bartender, warming to us and the coin we laid down, asked us how we came to be here and when we said Osprey, everything changed. “Osprey” he cried. “Nothing but a bunch of low life bird fuckers”

“Filthy sheep fuckers, get your poxy asses out of here; you smell like a boatload of rockfish left in the sun” yelled another., twisting around to look at Hans who was talking to some locals

“Yeah” chimed in a third. “take your fucking blood money and begone from our gentle, uh, homey…<looking around> uhh..just get the fuck out, is what I mean. Begone fish fuckers!”

“What is wrong with you” I yelled back. “and what is all this stuff about animal diddling anyway? How dare you-we’re just normal people like you, coming here to help you after one of your own died on our doorstep and you….” I was so mad I could barely speak. “You treat us like..You…and your beer sucks” is how it landed. I slammed my tankard down on the dirty ugly bar and stormed out, not even caring that I spilled ale all over for someone to clean up.

As we left I turned to Hans. “what did you say? Everything was going fine and then the barman went crazy and the people you were talking to started their filthy slights. What did you say?”. “Well, nothing” stammered Hans. “I just, complimented that woman on her facial hair. It had a particularly elegant curvature that..”

“WHAT”? (I had rudely cut him off, but it was…idiotic beyond belief..yes, something like that). “You actually said…” <counting to 10>. “uhh..you haven’t had a lady friend have you? Like a lady gnome.?.”

“Ah, no” says Hans “I can be most absorbed in my work to be honest. How did you know?

“Lucky guess, Hans. Anyway, next time you meet anyone here, or well, anyone anywhere, can we pick a different subject”?

“Ya ya of course of course” he replied. “I did not mean to offend. Village life is most peculiar to me and I thank you for well considered counsel. It is most kind”.

Well, so much for the bar, and it was now well into the night. What now?

Zunk points to a building close to the water that is casting light onto the street and offers that someone clearly is inside, which is better than the empty place we are and we head to it. No sign, but the small window shows shelves, none higher than my head, and a corner of a similarly sized counter. We enter and a small woman (ah, thus the low fixings) appears through a door and gives us one of those townie looks. I introduce myself (she is Elsie) and give her a hopefully not awkward smile. “I was just about to close, strangers”, which seems an invitation to leave. I just can’t believe everyone here is so,,mean…and I explain (while being a little light on detail) our recent arrival and need for shelter. I could not help but mention the nasty encounter in the bar, trying to make amends for bothering her and then thinking. Stupid stupid! Why are you pointing out we already are shunned. I shift hopefully from foot to foot.

She peers at me a moment, marginally less suspicious. “Ayup, that sounds like them folk. Always quick to judge and always finding the others wanting. You wouldn’t be the first been run out by those idiots. An’ them Gorumites ain’t gonna be no help either; they more or less keep folks from the murderin’ and rapin’ but don’t give much mind otherwise. Yeah, sure, I can let you bed down on the floor” She looks at me again, and my smile is less awkward“ You a big ‘un, but I ain’t getting the sense you gonna thump me with that” <pointing at the Morningstar hanging off my waist.

I thank her greatly, overwhelmed by the first kindness we have received, and I cannot let this go unreturned so offer to pay. “Aye, aye” she says “You’re a good one you. How about I throw in dinner”? Okay, so good cheer and honesty does work, even if delayed.

We talk as she cooks, interrupted by a constant stream of questions and comments from Hans about her preparation. It is irritating and endearing; father never cared about any of this, only whether dinner was on time. She did not know anything about the murdered citizen, only snorting that as a Gorum follower, who knows what he got into, Her best advice was to ask the hermit who squatted in the derelict mansion we saw just outside of town. Man of the name of Daril, knew stuff, she said. She also mentioned one of the boats in the harbor was from New Thassalonia, currently the dominion claiming the island upon which we live. Normally we see them once a year when they take their cut, which is painful but never too much so. It is not tax time and the ship was seemingly ill suited and too big for that routine purpose. The contingent of representatives and merchants (more than 3, less than 10-she wasn’t paying too much attention) was met by an escort from the Temple. They retired there a week or so ago and have not been seen since, “Whatever they are hatching up in there, it’ll mean more payout from us, that’s for sure” says Elsie.

“Well, you now have friends in Osprey, and we will be stronger if we work together! You’ll see-it’ll be fine.”.

“Huh. Eat your soup. I’ll look at that bolt in the morning and see if I can tell you a little bit more about the poison.” She clambers up some back stairs and leaves us to it.

I thought for sure I would have trouble resting in a strange place with strangers, but it was a long day full of bad and good surprises. And the possibility of doing good here calmed me, so I murmured a little prayer to Milani for good news from Ordwii tomorrow and went promptly to sleep.

Elsie came down after we had woke (I was cleaning the small kitchen) and sat herself down at a work bench strewn with vials and retorts and flasks of various kinds and began mixing powders. She scraped some residue off the crossbow bolt from the bridge, muttered to herself a lot, and combined it with her various agents.

“Hah…gotcha. Put you to sleep it does, sometimes for awhile if ya treat it wrong”

“Aye mum” says Zenk, “On that you are quite right”

“Ya, Ya, we know this” puts in Hans “Would symptoms include red blotches and rust?”

“Nope, can’t say that it would” she says. “Sorry, but what you are looking for aint what’s on this. Anyway, good tidings to ya. Come on back if it suits.”

We head to the temple gate to meet Ordwii as instructed, but there must be some sort of delay; she is not there. Nobody is there, in fact. The door is not manned, although the spikes and blades welded into the wall would discourage visitors even without guards. But still, where was Ordwii?

I want to find a way in; I’m worried about Ordwii. I’m not sure what could be going on, but stupid Rule #2 (things are not what the seem) comes to mind. The others are not convinced and are discouraged by the unclimbable wall and the locked door. Zunk wonders if we should head to the Thalassonian boat to “check their vibes” whatever that means. I’ve been on boats for my whole life and I don’t even know what a “vibe” is, but the others seem to because they all agree. Except for Tom, who shrugs and says “whatever”. Being outvoted, I don’t have much choice. “I’m coming back here in an hour” I say.

There’s not much happening on the ship when we arrive, and Zunk convinces one of the deck hands to summon the 1st Mate who’s in charge while the Capt is occupied in the temple. She’s a swarthy skinny woman with weathered skin, a constant squint, and a distinct lack of tact. “I’m the mate Janice, what you want?” she barks “I’ve already had enough foolishness this day so this better be good “. <it is less than 2 hours after daybreak>.

“Well, m’am” parries Zunk, “maybe you’re worried about your captain, then, and it’s accounting for your short temper”? Somewhat disarmed by the question, she snorts. “Now why d’ya think this, shorty”?

Zunk explains our situation and the parallels to theirs and is not unpersuasive. The mate is indeed bothered by there being no contact with the capt or the party for over a week but has no immediate action she can take given the captains’ orders to wait and keep the ship safe. But, she says, that hasn’t prevented her from scouting a little, and other things.

“I been spyin on them Gorum priests. The work in shifts one in the morning and one in the evening, walking the wall and the town. The big ‘un with the great sword usually comes out in the afternoon for a look see, but the last couple of days I ain’t seen much. But if you’re really wantin’ to go inside and find your friends, then I can help you if you promise to find my Capt and the party. Here, I, you know, borrowed a key from one of the guards and made a mold of it. Maybe it’ll get you in”.

We take the mold, thanking her profusely and I insist on checking the temple again in case Ordwii has shown, but it is as before. So we head back to Elsie’s; she might have the tools to turn that mold into a key to get us in. Hans says he can make the key if so.

I burst through the door, kicking over a badly placed…what is that anyway? Never mind. “Elsie, we need to make a key and we wonder if you have the right tools. It’s important”

“Aye, I might” she says, “but why?

Zunk spins a yarn about an uncle who lost an important key, but friends don’t lie to friends and I cut him off before it gets too far. “Look” I say . “This will unlock the door so we can try and find our friend, you have to help us!”

“Aye” she says “but you don’t need a key to get in, if that’s what you’re after. There’s another way if you don’t mind getting a little wet”

She explains that there is a well in the compound that connects to channel in the rock that is dry enough at low tide to provide entry. “You’re gonna have to squeeze a bit, my girl, but you should be Ok if you time it right. Ain’t no need to go barging in the front gate. But if you go, you got to do me a favor in return. “

See-honesty is the best policy, and the favor turns out to be a boon. Elsie is the town animal doctor too and has been taking care of a sick ram but she’s not been able to treat it since the Temple closed up. “Here” she says “shoot him up with this-he’ll be in the stockade with the other critters”.

I’m not one to be sneaking around, but it’s pretty clear we’ve been lied to, and there’s no good reason I can think for this to be so if Ordwii and Cormac were not somehow in danger, so the well it is. But Zunk insists on making the key so we have a plan “B”, which seems sound. Even after Hans completes the task, we have a lot of time before low tide. Not having anything else to do, we head to the old mansion to talk to the mysterious Daril.

We are greeted by Daril as we approach the smaller roofed building in the middle of the of the grounds. He’s dressed more like Hans than a typical townie, but the clothes are well worn. What looks like ink stains his fingers. Happily he’s nothing like the townies we’ve met and we’re not accused of boffing birds or sheep or whatever and we go into our usual explanation, which Hans has pared down so he can get to the point before anyone’s attention wanders. Daril, it turns out, is not from here either, originally coming some years ago to build a fancy house for some rich merchants to use as a summer lodge, only some upheaval in the local economy sent the family into ruin, and Daril was abandoned mid-construction.

“Something about an old Runelord meeting his end at the hands of some heroes, but it sounds too crazy to be true. A rat, a frog, a cat and a couple of humans taking down one like that? Don’t think so. But whatever happened, I got stuck here. Not much use for an architect in a place like this. Did the best I could to get one of these buildings sort of finished, but it’s a hard go.”

“Ya, you’re an architect?” cried Hans “I have a great interest in city planning. Graduated from the University of…”

Well, this threatened to derail the whole conversation, so I dragged it back to the matter at hand.

Daril proved to be less all-knowing than Elsie had indicated, having nothing useful to add to what we knew about the murdered Gorumite, or the fate of the merchants. He also might be a little, well, traumatized by his abandonment, claiming he hears ghosts around the grounds that otherwise don’t bother him. I’d probably be lonely too, if I were him, and in need of imaginary companions.

He did know something of the temple, however; spending some of his plentiful time on observing its architecture. He marked out a path on one of his maps, well-hidden he said, that climbs the bluff and ends around back close to the Temple roof.

“One of you might be able to jump the gap, or throw one you smaller ones on top and find a way in. But if you do, could you try and sketch out the inside for me? Temple architecture was a specialty of mine before I got side tracked and this is an unusual structure”

“Ya, Ya, I noticed that too!” cries Hans. “The proportions seem overly rigid for Gorum, particularly in regard to the diminished use of porticos, and the vernacular lacks a clear articulation of the grandiose chaos of battle typical of modern Gorum construction.”

“Ah, you have a keen eye my friend” returns Daril, “Here, look, maybe you’d be interested in my drawings of the spires on the island. Notice how dissimilar they are to the town construction? Nobody seems to know their origin, but I have a theory that..”

I drag Hans away before the entire afternoon is used up, promising we will try our best to map out the Temple. We need to sort out whether the roof or the well is the better bet and we don’t have much time. We move together to where we see the little path snake through the hills, and Zenk moves on to scout. He’s better at this than any of us, and he’s back before too long.

“Aye, the path ends about 20 feet from the back corner o’ the Temple, and I’m bettin’ we could get across. There’s a big sand pit up there, for whatever reason and a door down. But a couple o’ them priests were lounging about and they might have seen me. Couldn’t be sure, but ain’t no time to be going on hope alone. I’m for the well, if ye be askin.”

The rest of us are too, except for Tom, who just shrugs. “Whatever” he says. It is amazing he can pack so much boredom and disregard into such a small gesture.

The passage is as Elsie has described: well hidden, cramped and damp, but we make it to the well, looking up into a circle of darkening sky. Milani grant us we are not too late! Tom decides for some reason that now is the time for action, and with little warning starts climbing the well before we can make a plan. I can only stare as he gains the top, affixes a rope and disappears over the edge. Of all the times to be decisive! I grab Zunk and hold them as high as I can to get them to the top fast in case there is trouble, but the rope must be damp and he slips, banging me in the shoulder and we both topple over, making quite the racket.

Oh, fish and fiddlesticks!
