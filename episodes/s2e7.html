Season 2, Episode 7: Whistlefangs and the Fall of Ripnugget

The whispers and darting glances started the moment we walked into the Dragon, and Zunk asked to be set up in a private space. There the three of us sat while Tom and Hans (and Phrillys) went to their rooms.

We nibbled a variety of delicious dishes. They must have fired the cook from our first meal- the difference was extreme- and made small talk. I drank my wine slowly and tried to follow all the bawdy word play. Chesty filled in some details for us about House Scarnazzi;   they were particularly financially strained, and the head of the family, Titus (the Counselman), had clamped down tight on the family and its businesses to steer through the troubled times. No doubt us stepping in the middle will have consequences. Chesty also revealed that Alma Avertin's daughter was the sole survivor of a party that had explored The Pit, and the only person in recent memory to return from that place. As I suspected, she had taken a dark turn after that, and Alma was worried sick. I shall have to find some way to help.

Having broached the subject of the pit, Chesty reached over to a lute he had been carrying and strummed a few notes. “Here” he said “this is what I know” and commenced to sing a dirge like dark tune, in rhyming couplets, of countless wanderers braving that place, most never to return or if returning, doing so broken. The pit was a monstrously deep hole, inhabited by horrible beasts and a series of dangerous ladders and bridges gave entry to the numerous tunnels and shafts connected to the main bore hole. The final couplet was clear advice to stay away, to which Zunk sadly shook their head.

“Well, enough of these depressing tunes-I have another one I was working on while you were with the mayor. It’s called “In defense of Abstalar”. Do you think the other dinner guests would enjoy a song? I do. Come.”

It was glorious. Most of the guests cheered when any of our names were mentioned (even mine!) and jeered at the mention of Scarnazzi, and there was thunderous applause at the end. Chesty and Zunk were having a grand time, and there was an impromptu performance afoot. I was knackered from the day, and my head a little spiny, so I retired. I was later told some of the songs were quite bawdy, especially one about a somewhat dour half eleven warrior who doesn’t talk much. I might have to ask to hear thar one.

We took to the trail in the morning; the pit was a few hours away by foot along a coastal trail that then turned south and east, curving around a low flat topped rise that was the Devil’s Platter. It was only a few hundred feet high, but very steep-I could not see climbing it, although Zunk might. We looked for the path up that Bethana had described-its lower end was hidden in scrubby bush, and it would be hard to see if we did not know to seek it. We could see the faint discoloration of the dull red stone that marked the switch backs as we got closer. Some areas had eroded slightly and we could see occasional humps of scree and loose rock, but it was not a death defying climb. We ascended carefully and without mishap to the top and emerged on the flat earth of the summit. We found a few scattered items as we climbed, dross of other adventurers most likely, except for the goblin dogslicers that while well used, did not seem to have been there long,

Not a thing grew at the top, which seemed unusual as we were not especially high, so the pit was clearly visible some distance away; a hole punched down into the earth without any ledge or hint of its presence so that one could easily walk right off the edge in the dark. You would fall a long way; the hole was shrouded in mist that began at the edge of our vision, and it roiled a little bit in grey malevolence. I did not like the feel of it. The pit was quite circular and the edges smooth if not polished. It was hard to tell if it was just a strange natural formation, or some ancient runelord working, but either way it was not inviting.

There was a ladder on the north side, clearly made from foraged pieces, the rungs held to the frame with cord. Improvised and a little rickety, but I inspected it carefully and judged it would hold my weight. Zunk clambered down first, being the nimblest and quietest, and we assembled on the small earthen platform below once he reported it safe. The bottom of the pit was no closer-I thought I heard screaming from the mist, but it was probably my imagination. I was sure someone would snicker if I mentioned it, so I kept it to myself.

Another ladder leading down and a tunnel leading north. Zunk scouted and we joined him before a door, which Tom opens to reveal a good-sized chamber with walls adorned with skulls. Mostly beasts, but a few humanoid of various sizes, many cracked and gnawed, or pierced with holes. A grim and unwelcoming collection. There were two alcoves on the east and west walls, each with a giant stuffed spider, with marbles for eyes. The claws and jaws looked real though, and there was a stuffed scare-crow horse ahead of us, with wings made from sticks and scraps of cloth. Giant musty horror dolls for children, or dolls for giant children, is what it looked like to me, and not without threat.

Tom shrugged and stepped in, triggering some sort of trap; a cloud of greasy smoke came from the horse’s mouth enveloping all but Zunk-and I felt a little weird, “Hey Zunk” called Tom as the smoke cleared “maybe you want to look around here a little bit, seems like there’s, well, you know..”

Tom carefully retreated as we waited in the tunnel to let Zunk work. “Aye, yeah, it’s a good thing you got your long shanks out of here, there’s a whole bunch of stuff going on here, so give me a second.. Oops, shyte”. And then it got quiet. “Uh, mates, I’m kinda stuck, heh, so..” and then it got quiet again. We all looked at each other, ready to move in, but Hans, who was closest to the door, held out his hand. Wait-he mimed. We stood still for what seemed like forever while I imagined all the terrible things that might be happening, but I trusted Hans. Tom fidgeted quietly but made no move.

After a few minutes, Zunk sauntered in. “Whew, that was tricky, but I think its sorted. Looks like a bunch of goblins shacking up here, but they ain’t Ripnegget’s crew. Came out to see the trap I sprung-clever thing really. One of them spider dolls webbed me when I was trying to shut it down. Almost had it, but just as..well..anyway. Goblins-so we got on good enough that they didn’t skewer me right off, and then we got to talkin. Figured dropping my Gran’s name might be a good idea and it was but not in the way I thought. They ain’t no fans-seems ol’ Gran roasted their last chief and told the rest of them to mind their own business or else. Got a meet with the new boss, Tunch, and I might have convinced him to go in with us and take Ripnugget down, but he’s wanting to take a look at you first. So you know-act tough, but keep your powder dry. Jhys-scowl a little bit for me, eh? There ya go lass. Okay-ready friends? Let’s meet Tunch.”

We passed through the room-there were a couple of doors on the other side of scarecrow horse that we did not see, and into a large cavern with a bunch of goblins, and maybe some kind of large weaselish thing that drooled and snarled. There were a lot of goblins, and they seemingly were in two camps, staring each other down from opposite sides of the room. A burnt corpse sitting in an equally burnt large chain divided the room roughly in half, serving as the line between the two groups, who were snarling and whistling through their teeth at each other. Zunk looked a lot less relaxed as he took this in.

“Tunch, mate” he said gently, “what’s going on? Thought we had a deal to get rid of Ripnugget, but it’s looking a little tense here” He was speaking to one of the goblins on our left.

“Hey-who the fuck are you?” spat someone on the right “I’m Minargle and I speak for us, not that tuneless retard, Tunch. Ain’t nobody doing nothing less I I say so. That bastard Ripnugget would roast us like sausages-he likes ‘em well burnt (points to the corpse). Why should we listen to you and not gnaw on your bones for dinner?”

“Fuck you Minargle-you aint our boss you scraggly faced yellow eyed runt. If I wanna deal to take out that firey bastard, that’s what we gonna do”

This led to a long minute of whistling and hooting and goblin insults and hopping up and down and brandishing weapons.

“Whoa, hey” says Zunk holding up “lets all calm down here and keep our heads, okay?”

“Jah”, added Hans helpfully “literally and figuratively, jah?”

“Ripnugget’s my Gran, and we know he’s a right bastard and whatever he’s doing it aint no good. Your chief ain’ t the only one he done, and he’ll do a lot more if we don’t stop him. So if you lot (points toward Minargle) wanna stay here and let this lot take the glory and the loot, well, its all the same to me. We ain’t choosing no sides, except against Ripnugget. So go do what you need to do and we’ll do the same. Cool”?

The two bosses in waiting snarled at each other until finally Minargle spat “Fine. You assholes get yourselves killed. Not my problem. Less mouths to feed”.

“Fuck you, Minargle. Tunch will do this thing and then you will see he is real boss, and you will shut it”. More whistling and hooting. “So lets do this” yells Tunch “Through that door there. Ripnugget is somewhere back there. I show, and you go first. “ I couldn’t help but notice his confidence waned as he led us to the door (which was well hidden) “We are right behind you”, he added, kind of quietly.

Okey dokey, I guess. Hans suggested it might be the right time to cast a fire protection spell, and they all agreed I should be the one so granted. I protested, but Vistaro would tell me I can’t help them if I get burnt to a cinder, so, okey then. Tunch and his companions were so quiet we sort of forgot all about them.

Zunk cracked the door noiselessly so we were able to peek through the slit to see a large room with desks and books and scrolls strewn on big tables. A few split-faces were visible, and something in armor with its back towards us, pawing at the contents of one of the tables. I saw a flash of a skeletal hand, and bone where the face should be.

The presence of the splitfaces pretty much told how this was going to go, and we were all ready when Tom yanked the door open and strode towards the nearest splitface and cut him.

The armored skeleton-Ripnugget almost certainly, cast out his bony hands and leapt to mount the large flat snouted lizard that he had conjured forth, while another spit face moved to challenge Tom, trying to rake him with nasty claws. Tom caught him wrong footed and opened a wound from chest to kidney. Hans conjured a wall of water that he positioned between us and Ripnugget, which cleverly protected us from its fire. Another splitface burst through the wall of water to take a swing at Zunk, who was right beside Tom. Splitface’s claws raked Zunk across the arm leaving too bloody gashes. I touched the cold iron hammer in my pack and channeled its energy into bolts I hurled at the wounded spltface and took it down, as another one crashed trough the wall and went for Tom. It was chaos-spray and claws and bolts and steel all flying about. I watched a perfect droplet of spray fly through the air and plunk Phrylliis on the nose.

The wall as a good gambit, but the lizard was fast enough to round the far side and Ripnugget breathed fire to strike us all, scorching us thoroughly. Thankful now to be protected, I avoided serious harm. Tom took the lizard’s legs out from under it but Ripnugget managed to spring free.

Zunk was terribly burnt and ominously, still bleeding, but Ripnugget had miscalculated and made a major error. His fire had also taken out all of the splitfaces. I would heal Zunk while Tom and Hans occupied the others and then it was over. Ideally. With or without Tunch, and it was looking like “without” was the situation.

Except Han’s spell bounced off Ripnugget to no effect and when I reached for my energy, something blocked me and my boon to Zunk sputtered and died. I stamped my foot. Fish and Fiddlesticks-why is it NEVER easy. ? I had to tend Zunk with medical skills, not priestly healing. As if to confirm my suspicion- Zunk took a healing draught, which did nothing to judge by the frown on his face, and then he moved to the other side of the water wall. Sigh. At least he was out of reach of our foes.

Worried for Zunk, I bulled through the wall like it was a summer rainstorm, and tended his wounds, and then some more, first carefully and then more quickly, but the risk paid off. By the end, Zunk was fit enough if not fully well. As I was doing this, the lizard, apparently unaware of Tom’s disdain for those who fail to take the hint and lie low, tried to regain its feet and Tom staked it to the red stone floor. Only Ripnugget now, facing off against all of us.

Zunk took out his bow and fired an arrow through the wall, sticking Ripnugget hard and Tom tripped him, sending him into the water wall. The undead thing tried to stab Tom as Zunk pulled out a glowy arrow and nocked it. “I will see you all in hell” Ripnugget snarled as he flailed. “Good bye Gran” Zunk growled “Dad always said you were a right bastard, and someone should do you. So here ya go, Dad”

The blue tinged arrow flew from Zunk’s bow, piercing the water and burying itself in Ripnugget’s chest. A storm erupted in the wet wall, light flickering in the water like flashing fish and wind blowing chunks of water about everywhere. Blue light flickered over Ripnugget like the sparking of a deep sea creature and it went rigid-I could hear bones snapping from the tension. It pulled back its fleshless head as tremors cracked more bones and teeth, and whatever curse it uttered was absorbed by the churning water and blown away by the wind. Then it stopped moving, and the water crashed to the ground to drain through the cracks in the stone, leaving only a broken skeleton in armor. “I know he was your grandfather” says Tom “but, fuck that guy anyway”.

We spent some time collecting books and scrolls-there was too much to really make sense of, even for Hans, so we intended to give them to the Rune plonkers to study. Ripnugget had a dog slicer much like the one Zunk already carried, which he took as another family keepsake. There was a book (a “grimoire”, which a bit of Hansplaining clarified) and a ring. Beyond that, there was little interest except for the raised platforms on each side of the room, which Hans believed were teleportation devices. The bones on one of them suggested they might not be working too well anymore.

As we were making to leave, Tunch and the others rushed in. We had completely forgotten about them. “Hey, hey-you guys alright?” asked Tunch “we heard some stuff, and then quiet. Not know what was going on so we stayed outside. Not want Ripnugget to get past. But he looks dead! Minargle must now bow!. So where’s the loot? You-go tell others Tunch has defeated Ripnugget!”

“Ah, good on ya mate for securing the rear. Smart-sign of a tactician to be sure. Anyway, we didn’t find much, but everything here is yours. Some pretty nice chairs, and a good pile of bones over there. That metal on the floor looks valuable, so have at it.” Zunk says all of this without batting an eye and with seemingly complete honesty.

Chaos ensued. A fight broke out concerning some of what to the goblins were of value - unusual bones on the teleport pad that would make good ornaments, inkwells with ink which they smeared on faces as "warpaint", quills with pointy metal tips that were being used to scratch and poke, and Ripnugget's decorative leftovers which the goblins viewed with both fear and greed - with greed eventually winning out. Tunch claimed Ripnugget's bone crown and was peacocking around with it, cementinh his leadership role. One of the goblins was trying to figure out how to wear the breastplate, but it was clearly too heavy for them and they ended up pawing at the air like a turtle on its back while the other goblins kicked them, only to stub their toes on the armor. We departed from the cacophony of whistles, shouts, thunks and occasional groans.

We reached the chamber where we first met Tunch and the others. A couple of goblins were dragging the “throne” to a refuse pit, and they tipped the charred body in. I guess this was considered adequate ceremony for succession of the new ruler; Tunch was obviously content. There were a few goblin dogs running around, adding to the chaos, which we were advised to stay clear of; dogs carry mites and bugs that are very disagreeable to “pink fleshies” as they called us. Tunch protectively kept the dogs away from Sausage (the weasel thing), which snapped their jaws violently whenever a dog got too close. We gave the dogs and Sausage a wide berth, and Hans found the sanitation issues quite alarming (noting a large creature moving around in the refuse pit).  “Code violation for sure” he muttered as we passed.

Before we left, Tunch said “We make good team. Maybe you come back and help us with little problem of long shanks in floor below. Sandpoint devil worshipers kicked us out; is why we are here. You come back?” Tunch was at least able to read the room. “Okay-if you come back-then whistle like this…and we no attack. Okay?

“Yeah-we know how to whistle, goblin boy” says Phyllis. “You just pucker up and blow” One eye was on Tom, the other swiveled to regard Tunch.

Zunk signaled our agreement and made sure he could whistle the right tune. We stayed till dawn in a little magical hut Hans thoughtfully provided and left the goblins to do as they would. We left for Sandpoint at dawn.
