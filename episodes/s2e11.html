Season 2, Episode 11: Familial Complications, Part 2

Deverin comes up to me as the carriage clattered off. ”I am so glad you are here” she says, She is worried but trying to put a good face on it, and I flush a little bit at the thought she trusts me. Go do what you have to do and find Hans for Iomidae’s sake. We need his devious and pedantic brain to square off with that witch Vax.”

“Uh-what?” I wasn’t listening again, as I had spied something in the corner of my eye that was so instantly recognizable I jerked my head around towards the middle of the next block. To see a tall gangly man, full black beard and hair (with a touch here and there of gray, which I knew even though I could not see from here), staring at me and shouting.

“Dad?” I cried “Dad! What are you doing here? I mean, I am so glad you came, but..”

My Da, dressed as always, strode down the street till he reached me. "Jhyselein! Gods... look at you. Praise the stars, you're whole. I've been tracking your name up from the harbor for two hours, Jhyselein. Every merchant and guard I spoke to looked at me like I was carrying black powder when I said I was your father. Stop. Don't look at the sky—look at me. You're alive. You're really alive!"

I ran up and hugged him. “Yeah Da, I’m fine, really. I can’t believe you are here, and I know this might be a little confusing. It’s complicated, but good, sort of. It’s a nice town..”

"Alma at the Baker told me you've been playing the savior of this town, Jhyselein. She spoke of a trial at the courthouse and monsters under the streets.”

“Well look, Da, I haven’t been playing at anything. I’ve been helping people who need help”

“They are whispering about you in the taverns, daughter. Terrible, ugly things. They say you’re a radical, that you’re practicing taboo magic.”

“What, Da, you don’t actually believe that do you? There’s this Scarnazzi guy, you just missed him actually, that’s telling terrible things about me cause he lost the trial. I pledged to Milani, Da. Do you think I’d break my edicts?”

“No daughter I don’t. I know who I raised but you need to tell me right now: what is happening in this town, what have these strangers dragged you into, and who are these people you’re running with?  Who..is this boy? Who is he to you?"

Tom had come up behind me as I was talking, and he stepped up and said pleasantly “Hi, I’m Tom. I’m a friend of Jhys..eline. Jhyselein, your daughter. Sir”. There seemed to be some hidden communication going on, but then I got it and giggled.

“oh, Da. That’s Tom, one of my friends. You don’t actually think I’d go out with someone wearing more eye make up than me, do you? He’s uhh, a farmer actually.” Da looked at the big blade but said nothing.

“So Da, uhhm, I bet you’re tired and I have a room in the Rusty Dragon, so maybe you want to go and rest? I have some things I got to do, but I’ll be back this evening and I’ll explain everything.”

It was like I wasn’t even speaking.

“You want to know how I found you? It was an accident. A miracle. A strange woman walked into my workshop last month. She didn't want furniture; she wanted a custom lockbox carved with Milani's blooming rose. She looked me in the eye, said she was 'of the faith,' and asked if I was the father of the fierce woman preaching justice in Sandpoint. The moment she left, I dropped my chisels. It cost me every single extra copper we had in the lockbox to charter a coach and a vessel to get here. "

That certainly was miraculous, but I still had things to do and this wasn’t helping. Of all the times for him to show up. And that thought triggered the recollection of what Hans had said. Hmmm. Whatever, but it didn’t solve the problem of what to do about my Da.

Deverin stepped smoothly down, passing me a look I knew: I got this. Leave it to me.

It was a girl thing.

I edged over just fractionally to giver the mayor more space, and suddenly she was in front of me grasping my Da’s hand. “Pleasure to meet you. Jhyselein has told me much of you. You are even more striking in person. Did your daughter tell you of how well she has acquitted herself? No, she wouldn’t, but is quite a valued, uh, employee. She is on a special contract with the town, and has some things to which she must attend. In the meantime, let me show you around and of course you shall be my guest at our manor. Jhyselein shall join us later. Come, do you like architecture? Our town has quite a history you know”

It was so skillfully done she wondered if her Da had picked up on it. But even if he had, it wouldn’t matter. He was caught in a gentle but implacable whirlpool.

“Thanks for taking care of my Da, Mayor” I called. Da was looking around and back at me. I hoped being with the mayor would keep him off balance for a bit till I could return. “I’ll see you later, Da, and explain everything. I’m uh, glad you came.”

I watched my Da be lead away and shook my head, utterly flummoxed by the craziness of It all; Da here, in the middle of all of this. Just a second later, Zunk trotted up.

“Aye, what’s this? Ya look like you’ve seen a ghost Jhys. What’s going on”?

“Well, you would know if you, and he” I glared at Tom “ hadn’t wandered off doing god knows what. And that was my da, actually.”

“What? Fuckin hell” blurted Zunk. He seemed overly excited/distraught about my family reunion.

“The reason I bugged out was because there were people in that carriage I didn’t want to see, at least until I could figure out what the hell was going one. That goblin pair, those were…”

“Your parents?” I cried, not able to help myself. One thing suddenly made more sense (the look Venke gave me), but others did not. ”This can’t be a coincidence” I finished.

“Aye, that’s what I think too. Anyway, when I saw them, well, I just have thing where if I see something that don’t belong, I’m gonna lay low until I can get it sorted. So I figured maybe it’s better for me to stay hid till I could talk to them and get the low down and I followed them to make sure where they ended up. Tom was keeping an eye on me.”

I smiled sheepishly at Tom, who shrugged.

Zunk continued “We gotta swing by near the Scarnozzi estate so I can leave a message only Venke will understand. She uh, used to be head of the local thieves guild” Ah, that also explained some things. Zunk definitely favored their* Mom in a lot of ways. Hard to picture him as coming from a dad who liked to blow stuff up though-that seemed more Hans’ thing.

“It won’t take long, and then we can head to the farms and the well. You can tell me the rest of what happened on the road”

So that’s what we did. Cautiously approaching the Scarnazzi estate, Zunk left us and snuck further, no doubt leaving some sort of hidden message composed of sticks and dragon fly wngs, fur, snail spit and what have you. They returned to report that their mom would be at the Rusty Dragon at 10:30 if she found the message. We headed out of town for the farms and the well.

It was a gigantic waste of time. Well, we did meet some nice farmers and Tom had a lovely chat with them about the weather and the price of barley, but we didn’t get much in the way of information. The first folks we met (the aforementioned farmers) admitted they had heard some bad things had happened along the road, and told us the location of a couple farms where the “Sandpoint devil” had left it’s blackened hoof prints, but nothing else. They were happy to wait for us while we inquired at the farms, taking us up on our offer to see them to Sandpoint when we were done.

Visiting the farms offered the same thin gruel. Everyone seemed to think it was probably a prank by some kids, and had bigger things to worry over. Like the weather. Zunk clambered up the roof to inspect the prints, but it didn’t reveal much. One thing became clear though-the marks were not horseshoe prints but hoof prints. So it was hard to imagine a prank where a couple of kids carried an animal leg up a roof to somehow leave a burnt mark. Had to be a real live beast (or an animated dead one), but nothing to connect it to the pit or anything else. We rejoined the barley farmers and headed back, but not before Zunk took a few moments to climb down into the well in search of the flamey winged horse devil of Sandpoint. Nothing but mold and wet.

We decided to go to the RDI to clean up; it would have been rude to show up to the Mayor and Da stinking of barely and horse dung (Okay, it was gonna be a hard conversation that I was happy to delay to get my head in order). The dining room was crowded, we noticed, but strangely silent, except for a large center table occupied by a silken storm of sky blue-aquaramine-deep sea-sapphire clad gnome loudly commenting on the décor (tired rustic chic), food (imaginative but trying too hard), silverware (spotted) and all manner of other things. The gnome woman was such a focal point the 7 foot tall minotaur standing stone still behind her, was kind of an after thought.

This was (Obviously), The MOM of Hans, for there he was, slouched over the bar, head in his hands, empty bottles strewn before him. Phryllis was on her back head in a glass, eyes halk open“ Ah, Jaaah..Jh, Jhys..elein” Hans picked up his head slightly “Lemme introduce you to my..”

“Oh, so this is Jhyselein?” she cried, blue silk storm billowing “Hans, you rogue-she is a big one!”

What?

“Oh, I am so happy to meet you Jhyselein. Hans has told me so much about you I feel like we have been friends forever. (I’ve known Hans for two weeks). It’s so wonderful you two have found each other. Poor Hans, head always in a book, missing out on the affairs of the heart, or just some fun eh?” as she winked and elbowed me, aiming for the ribs, but only making it as far as my thigh.

I was caught between fury and astonishment. Why it is anyone’s business but mine who I chose to share a bed with, drove me mad. Why anyone would even think I would take up with Hans left me bewildered. (Hans, if you are reading this, which I cannot believe you would be, or anyone would be, to be honest, I mean no offense. It’s just that well, you know, you’re not exactly my type, which I am sure you would agree is painfully obvious).

“Hans” I cried sharply “What have you..”

“I can ash-ur you, Jyshhhhlein I said nothing.” Drunk as he was, he was still expecting this and held up his hands in half-hearted defense, head still on the bar. “it wasz them” as he pointed vaguely about the room. I noticed many around the room where whispering while looking and sometimes pointing, in our direction. “I tried, on my word as a mage of the Pryzjhyk Camady, but you haszh no idea how stubborn she is once she gets an idea in her head. What could I do, jah”?

“Try harder?” I yelled.

Mom seemed unperturbed by our discussion as well as Hans’ extreme drunkenness, swishing her hands and burbling “I have some things to attend to my dear, but I am most looking forward to chatting with you, You must tell me about how you and Hans got together. Tata. Bye Hans, you two don’t have too much fun!” and exited in a swirl of cobaltis-turquoise and periwinkle.

This elicited another round of whispering and pointing, and a sigh of relief as the blue storm cloud moved on, the minotaur silently behind, 3 paces.

Hans I growled

“I’m gonta go to my room and decouperate. Let me know when she’s gone” as he half-walked half crawled away. Phyllis rolled off the table with a thunk, staggering after.

“Oh sweet Jhyselein” drawled Tom, emoting for all he was worth, “Have I lost you to Hans”? My head snapped around. It was clearly a jest, but I was in no mood, and it was badly aimed, a fact no doubt apparent in my face. “Jhys” began Tom.

I was staring between Tom and Zunk at a spot on the wall. All I could see was red.

I cut Tom off before he could continue. “I’m going to go clean up and I will be down in 15 minutes and then we are leaving before I say something I will later regret” and turned around and stomped away. At the door I stopped, regarded the room and shouted to the whisperers “You ought to be ashamed of yourselves”.

We arrived at Deverin’s just as the meal was concluding, and Da seemed more relaxed. He had even taken some wine, and was a little flushed. But he was still clearly worried about me, and gave a small sigh as we walked in. Deverin arranged for us to be given food, and excused herself, leaving us to talk.

“Daughter” he sighed “Enough is enough. Tell me how you came to this, and why we should not leave on tomorrow’s boat, which is our only hope of passage back now that I have spent all our coin to make sure you were safe and we are without two coppers to rub together. “

“Oh Da. I am so happy to see you, and really, the coin is no problem. I’ve been well paid and found things along the way. I can scarce believe it, but that’s the one thing you don’t need to worry about. Nor me. I’m fine, thanks to my friends. I’ll tell you everything, but it’s going to seem strange”

He nodded.

“I am sure Owrdii told you about Iron Harbor and the rust plague and awful priests and their plans to summon some terrible thing, right?. Well, that’s partly why we’re here-we found something that seems harmful and brought it to the scholars, which lead us into investigating some troubles of the town that might be connected. But there’s a part Ordwii didn’t tell you, because we didn’t tell her: The Pride came to us, Da, all of them, and told us we needed to come here-it was our job to bring the artifact back.

I could scarcely tell of this without being short of breath with the enormity of it all, and paused, allowing Da to say:

“Jhys, this is not an explanation! I know whatever you did it was something you felt you needed to do, and whatever it is I will stand by you, daughter. But you do not need to lie to me. This is absurd, that you expect me to believe this”

“Da, it’s true, all of it! Vistaro gave me this staff, and Promqeth that dagger to Zunk. Do you think I would lie about something like this, to you? I know what it sounds like, but they were there!” I looked around helplessly, not sure what I could say. And hurt, because my own dad thought I was a liar or insane. Even though I might not believe me either.

My friends had my back, as usual. Zunk said gravely “Sir, in the time I have known your daughter, I have reckoned beyond all doubt that Jhys does not lie, even perhaps, when it would be easier. How is it you do not know this? And with regard to what she has said, it is the absolute truth. I have this (holding his dagger) from Promqeth himself”

Tom nodded “It is as they have said. I know-it sounds fantastic even to us, but well, sir, Zunk is right. Jhys does not lie, so even if I were not there to support her claim, you should not be doubting her”

The defense of my friends buoyed me, moving me beyond the hurt, or maybe not beyond, but at least to the side.

“Da, look, Maybe it wasn’t them and all of us have been deceived for some reason, but it doesn’t matter. The threat to this town is real, and we are here and might be able to stop it. I..can’t walk away from that, or my friends. “

"Jhys, Look around you! Look at the guards. This town isn't a sanctuary. We know what happens when powerful men start targeting families like ours—that's why we fled the old home! Don't make the same mistake.”

It went around like that for a while, me explaining why I couldn’t leave and my Da insisting it was someone else’s problem. He was afraid to let me make this choice; I wondered if he had similar arguments with Mom. Maybe that was the key.

“Do you think Mom would want me to stand by if it meant people would suffer? What would she do”?

“She would want to you be safe” he shot back “She would never take such a foolish risk”

My turn to shoot. “Well, I am a lot like her, but I am not Deebrah”

“Yes daughter, I am beginning to appreciate this. Look, Mayor Deverin speaks well of your work and I can see how you do not want to disappoint her. If I beg hard enough, I can delay the ship for two days. Suppose you take that time wind things down and hand whatever it is you are doing off to the Sheriff. It’s his town. Then we leave this powedkeg before someone lights a match”.

I smiled, but I could not commit. “I’ll try my best to figure this out, Da, and in a couple of days we can..”

“Good” he nodded “Two days, then we can pack our bags and return to the shop. God knows the orders are backed up and we’ll need he cash, even with what you’ve earned. Then the Sheriff can fight his own monsters." It was like he was willing this to happen by speaking, a habit all too common in men. It was distressing.

Zunk broke in, quietly. “Meaning no disrespect, but it’s Jhys’ choice. If she decides to go with ya, then I will be happy for you and her and sad for us. But if otherwise, then Tom and I will make certain her wishes are honored”

Again, I felt a warm glow of being protected. “Hush, Zunk, its fine, I am sure..”

“Do you threaten me?” my Da leapt up, turning in my direction. “He threatened me, Jhys. You call these your friends?

“Yes, Da, they are my friends”

“Fine. In two days, I get on the boat. I hope you are with me.”

“I hope so too Da, but sometimes you don’t really get a choice.
