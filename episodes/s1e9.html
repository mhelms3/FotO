Season 1, Episode 9: Finale, Part 1. Meitremar’s Ritual

I grant Zunk my final boon from Milani, as we all move to the stairs amidst a hail of arrows and spores, many of which seem directed at me, and most of which miss. One of the dero sparkles us with some spell that clouds my vision, making it hard for me to tend to Zunk’s wounds, but I managed after a couple of frustrations. I might of, erm, yelled “fuck” at one point but I can’t recall. (Well, no, that is not true. I absolutely did yell that out, but the thought of Zunk bleeding out was pretty stressful). Our enemies had no interest in pursuing us outside their circle of dark and so we made it up the stairs in one piece. There was no choice but to return to Elsie’s to rest.

It was good to be somewhere without blood and rust and dead bodies. Cormac was passing around his flask (even I took a small sip) and perhaps understandably, the talk turned to home and kin. I had no idea, but Tom’s father was the holy warrior who was part of the Pride of the Irritated Kitty! I was stunned-what what it must be like to be the son of such a great hero!? According to Tom-not so great at all. I could sense respect when he spoke of his father, who was nearly always somewhere “killing some lich in a dark cave” as he put it, but little affection. Is that the price of doing what needs to be done? A bitter son? I hope there is a better path for me, but I am not a hero. So it is a stupid girl worry.

And Hans-well it turns out he was born in Sanpoint! The very town where the heroes first came together, and left there out of boredom. He was amazed at all the things that happened there, and at first did not believe me when I told how The Pride stood down the dragon attack that nearly torched the village! Hans’ family also seemed less than ideal. His mother left when he was young to seek more adventure than Hans’ distracted tinkerer father could provide, and Hans was left mostly to himself while his sire spent most of his time in the attic, inventing arcane and dangerous things. Eventually his reach exceeded his grasp, and he and the attic was consumed in a giant ball of flame. Yikes. Poor Hans.

As I told my boring tale, it didn’t seem I had it as bad as I thought. So what if my Da was stern, and couldn’t really deal with having a daughter instead of a son? Was it the worst thing he could only see me as a fishwife* and not something else? At least he did not ignore me or almost blow up the entire house.

*somehow the term “fishwife” generated much confusion in the literal minded Hans. “What-he wanted to marry you off to a fish? That is barbaric!” It took more time than it should to explain that no, he only wished to marry me off to a fisherman. “Ja ja-that is different”. I blame the Iron Harbor townies for talking about sheep diddling, but hopefully this is now behind us.

We all felt better in the morning except for me. I had a throbbing pain in my arm, and when I inspected the area there was a weird blue welt, and something…moving? under the skin. Fiddlesticks-this could not be good. I could not clearly make out what was going on, but it felt cold and desolate, which is a weird way to describe a wound. Zunk peaking at it too, muttered “Oiy, looks like a bite, it does”. Holy smokes, yes, that was where the demon dog grabbed me in its jaws. Ooh-that is not good at all, I thought, recalling the zombie boggard. Don’t want to end up like that! I mean a zombie, not a boggard. No judgement intended about frog people. They were quite kind as it turns out. But demon dog rabies is not a sickness I wish to be afflicted with. Yuk!

I spent a little more time probing and prodding and figured I had at least a few days before the disease got really bad and then rummaged through some of the things we had found. Ah…I knew I recalled it correctly! Luckily, we had a scroll that would help me to feel less weak and prevent things from getting worse till I could attend my condition properly. One problem at a time. Elsie joined us and clucked at my wound. “Best take care of that, girl” she advised “or it well end badly. Here, I have prepared some more healing draughts for you, at least, but I cannot remedy what you have.” I nodded.

We took the potions in return for a few coins and set off. We could not risk Meitremar completing the ritual by dawdling further.

Once again, we entered the cavern behind the statue, and thought about our choices. Back to the dero (this time with a counter to dark at least. Hans encouraged me to delve into my rituals and I believe Milani has granted me a boon that might help)? Or we could go down the way with the trapped circle of stones. “Hey-over here” says Tom pointing east “I didn’t notice the first time, but this may get us around the crevasse to the other side, although” shrug “that might be the wrong way”. Well it seemed better than the flesh eating dero or the deadly circle, so we chose that direction.

The tunnel opened into another large cavern and we were regarded by two boggards. “Hey” one croaked “what you humans do here in our place? And why do you have clans stones and bones?” They regarded the trinkets the frog queen had given us, and it appeared for now they would not raise arms.

We explained our relationship with the boggards above and how we were granted the bits of bone and feathers we wore and asked if they might help us find the cultists, and were rewarded by croaks of anger when we described our enemy. The followers of Xar Azmak were no friends of the boggard either, it seems. “Come with us. You must tell this to Gnork-he will decide what to do with you. This way”. We went in the direction they pointed and the two boggards trailed behind, alert but not like as to start anything.

We entered into a space bigger than any we had seen; vast from the echoing of our bootsteps, and skirted the shore of a large black lake to an earthen and stick pier, upon which stood a simple hut adorned with bones and feathers and the like. Two boggards came forth, one more compact than we had seen before dressed in robes scrawled with unknown sigils. The other was burly and powerful looking, wearing strange bone and metal armor. The robed one spoke “Grrrmmm. Humans..and others..wearing the signs of the Black Lake clan-this is a strange day indeed” His words were quite well formed and his voice less froggy than the others. “Explain to us how you have come to these and the shore of our black waters. We have no love of humans here”.

Again we explained, and his gaze appeared thoughtful as we told our tale. “So you have defeated the evil robed humans above? That is good-they have been plaguing us for quite some time and cut us off from our Queen. How does she look? Is she well? She is great piece of frog ass, I am sure you coveted her” He appeared to grow agitated as he spoke and at the end his voice was quite booming “You did not mate with her? Bwaaark. Do not lie to me humans. I will smell if you are lying.” I took this one, it was familiar. Sigh. Just when I thought we were over this.

“No, we DID NOT” I stamped my foot. “Humans do not mate with an…uhmm..Humans mate with HUMANS, and goblins with..well. No. None of us tried to mate with her and she was not interested in us, that is for sure. But well, she was interested in Khhaarl or Gllouie-they were working it out. Not sure how it landed-we had to leave”

He was starting hard at us as I stammered through my protest. Suddenly he tensed and rocked back and “Bwarrk hahahaha Brawkkkk. Just fucking with you, humans. See Gorn-I told you. Humans have bad choice in mates, would never with us. Pay up!” The armor clad boggard sighed and tossed Gnork a bag.

Still sniggering, Gnork turned to us. “Still, your tale is most interesting, it seems something has riled her cool green blood. But those two? They could not build a proper nest if you put the mud and sticks in front of them” The boggard reference was lost on me, at least, but I think we all got the gist. Gnork was not wrong.

His stance shifted and his eyes got a little buggy. “But. You say the upper level is free from human scum? No offense. Uhh..listen. I gotta go grrggg..take care of some things. Hold the fort, Gorn. I’l be bwraack. Oh, humans, there is a door over there that leads beyond the crack in the earth. The other humans have been doing some strange things there for some time. You’d better hurry.”

There was a spring in his step as he made his way down the mud pier, and his chest and throat expanded in a big orange puff. Yup, seen plenty of that before at a certain time of year. Looks like it’s spring time for Gnork. I wished him well as we headed for the now obvious door.

Forewarned, we crept cautiously down the tunnel till it opened into a cavern light redly. The walls were becrusted with a ropey rusted fungus, strands intertwined in ways that were somehow disgusting to behold, with fungal stains coloring the tiled floor. There were piles of bones, decayed and ancient, but suffused with the same fungus on the floors and walls. A shifting red wall of twisting light cut the space in half. On the other side was a altar with several acolytes chanting around it, led by dwarf that must certainly be Meitremar. They all are so engaged in their ritual that they fail to notice us.

Zunk creeps up hoping to find some way to shut off the wall of light so that we may safely disrupt the ritual. As he moves slowly closer, the bones in several of the piles gather together and make to rise. Well, at least Zunk did not rush in this time.

Zunk twists his knife into the skeleton before him and Tom rushes another one, catching it before it can rise beyond its knees and dismembering it. The one in front of Zunk ignores the little thief, instead turning empty sockets in my direction. “Die cleric” it rattles and I am struck by a terrible pain that lays me low until the bright light of Milani courses through me and I straighten. Okey dokey then.

A fount of water crashes into the third skeleton sending it towards Zunk, who flicks their dagger out and ancient bones clatter to the ground like an unmade toy, as the bone-caster regards me balefully and snaps off another spell. Darkness. I can’t tell if the thing can see me smile, but I hope it can. “That was a dirty trick that got us last time, but not again” I yell as I raise my arms and cry “Darkness be gone!” And it was, and it was most satisfying. Thanks Hans!

Even better was Tom sweeping the bone-caster’s legs out from under it. The thing tries to cast another spell and Tom shrugs, mutters “read the room bone-bag” and cleaves it to the ground. The skeleton clacks as it works its ruined jaws “You will not stop my grandson; he will be victorious and Xar Azmak will be reborn!”. It explodes in a storm of bone chips, peppering Tom who manages to avoid the worst of it.

The red wall comes down and with a look over his shoulder, Meitremar completes his ritual with a shout. A rush of magical energy infuses the altar and is too much power for 3 of the acolytes, whose heads explode, while the last shudders and morphs into some undead thing advancing towards me. Upon the altar is a feral bony thing with an evil maw. It makes a sign and a green bolt flies forth striking me in the chest, burning me with acid. I wrap myself in Milani’s light and send a boon to my companions to help shield them from our enemies. We will NOT be taken so easily.

Meitremar seizes something dangling on a chain on his chest-it looks like a horn, and sure enough, he brings it to his lips issuing forth a sound like a metal sheet tearing. A cloud of jagged rusty shrapnel rages around the nasty dwarf, but luckily neither Tom nor Zunk are within its ambit. The zombie tries to brain me, but Zunk steps up and murmurs “Ahh, not so fast there, you’re gonna want to leave her alone” and sends the undead acolyte into true death. Ahh, Zunk, how sweet.

Zunk and Tom have squared off on the whatever it is and Meitremar, all of their allies gone. Okey dokey. Time to finish this.

Tom takes a tongue lashing (literally) from the inhuman thing, which smells like blood and rust with a hint of something else, which I immediately recognize as demon-stink. That would certainly be true to what we have learned, and fits with the bloody altar. He has no choice but to step into the metal storm but manages to trip the demon. The demon belches forth a sickening cloud, and its evil affects me greatly. My eyes are watering and I start to vomit. It rises and lands a massive blow on Tom, breaking his weapon besides.

Han’s spell goes off and the cloud flickers but remains in place “Jah-sorry” says he “it is too powerful”, as Zunk draws his bow, sending an arrow into the demon’s shoulder. Tom recovers slightly, and manages to wound Meitremar, who has used some of his gods power to heal himself. Blows are flying everywhere, claws and arrows and swords (Tom has drawn the blade given to us by the blacksmith), and both my friends are taking grievous wounds. Hans points his wand at the ground beneath the feet of our foes, but both keep their balance. I am still retching uncontrollably, but I manage to extend my defensive boon to cover my friends. A gout of water rushes towards Meitremar but slams against the altar instead. “Jah” says Hans “this is most annoying”

Between bouts of vomit, I gather my strength and fling Milani’s boon towards Tom, who is in dire straits (as is little Zunk, but one problem at a time). I cover Tom in blinding moonlight, undoing all the harm that has come to him. He straightens tall and strong, swinging his great sword at the architect of all this woe and runs the dwarf through. He dies choking on a bloody oath to his god of rust and ruin. Now only the demon…

..who howls in desperation, raking the little thief with its terrible claws. Zunk collapses. I manage to control the retching long enough to send the last of my strength towards my goblin friend, bringing them back to the battle. Tom and Zunk have the demon between them, but the evil thing is powerful in its anger, slashing Tom again and again. I close with the demon surrounding it fully, manage to give a quick stitch to Tom, and bash the demon, hoping to distract it from my friend. He’s pretty beat up. But the demon is sorely wounded too and not liking its position, tries to move away. Tom realizes it’s his best shot, lunges forward and spits the demon’s skill from behind, the great sword breaking demon teeth as it goes through, releasing rivulets of acid that smokes the demon’s flesh, dissolving it into a green blobby mess, bits of horn and tooth and claw in a sickly viscous pool. Hmm..I would have thought the demon would be immune to its own ichor, but there you have it. Rule #2.

I can’t believe it! I knew we could do it (both can be true)! We have won the day! I retch for the 50th time.

Eventually I overcame the sickness, and set to tending to my friends. I had exhausted my power, but I still had my kit and did what I could. I kept an uneasy eye on the portal above the altar. It had not closed and seemed to grow larger, and I could make out shapes in the shifting light inside. Hans walked slowly around the portal muttering “It is still empowered, I am sorry to say, but I cannot determine how”. My ministrations done, I joined Hans, and then gazed at the room hoping to discover something clever mage had missed, but I was not optimistic. “Wait, look Hans, the carvings on the walls, do they not resemble the workings on the machines from above?” “Jah” Hans replies, “Perhaps they do. Hmmm..let us assume so, and “ His voice trailed off slowly as he approached the wall and ran his fingers over its surface” “Mmmm, the most parsimonious explanation is that this entire room is a gigantic machine stabilizing this portal, but I cannot see how the energy is being conducted, or where it draws its power from. It is most vexing”
