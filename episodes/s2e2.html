Season 2, Episode 2: Forgiveness and Fire

We gathered for morning meal the next day and we were not halfway through when a young man in plain robes entered and somewhat timidly looked around. Seeing us, he approached and stumbled to a halt. All in one breath, he said, “Gentlemen, ma’am, and erm..others, I am from the Temple and have been asked by its leader to invite you to lunch at noon this day. They request your presence and hope you will attend. What may I tell them?” He finished with a little head bob, and was most pleased and more than a little relieved when we agreed.

Still struck by Brodert’s extreme cautiousness, we agreed it might be wise to determine if this boy was who he said, and Zunk confirmed this with B’ethena. So not a trap then, or at least, not an obvious one. Rule #2 (nothing is as it seems). We needed to head the Goblin to eat our avian breakfast, and then to Brodert’s to pick up the scroll Hans ordered.

Zunk made the hideous apology (he was quite sincere) while I stood by his side and smiled. Chesty seemed satisfied with Zunk’s peace offering, finally admitting he could not in truth stay mad at the little goblin How cute. Chesty looked at me briefly, probably in implied contrast. I smiled even more, the witless girl not realizing she was being scorned. I am having to learn there is more than one way to appeal to a man’s ego. It seemed to work.

That being done, we made for Brodert’s. He was quite surprised to see us and hissed “What are you doing here-I said lay low.”

“Ah good morning good Scholar” Hans said, quite loudly “I hope I am not too early to transact our business regarding the documents we discussed”. He could not resist winking, twice, but it surely would be visible only to us.

While Brodert spent some time producing the scroll he surely could have done more easily, we whispered about the Temple invite we had received, but all Brodert would say was it was best to keep it. He did not seem concerned about a ruse.

Our little one-act play was over, and given the Temple was on the other side of town, it was time to head to our appointment. We were met by the master of the temple, Abstalar Zantus, a most polite and friendly gentleman, and were lead into an inner courtyard of the temple. A stone table had been set with an impressive amount and variety of good, and seven menhirs to the major gods stood in a large circle around us. The temple, reconstructed after the dragon attack, was the grandest building I had ever seen!

Abstalar was a perfect host, asking us each in turn about ourselves and how we ended up in Sandpoint. I could not resist asking him if he knew the Heroes (slightly, he afirmed), and why would someone such as his grace be interested in us. It seemed we all were wondering the same.

“It is a matter of history” he replied “do you know of the early events in Sandpoint when the adventurers became part of our lore? Do you recall Nualia and the role she played in these events”? I did, sort of, but it was a long time since I had read the tales of The Pride and Abstalar had to fill in the gaps. But basically, Nualia was the adopted daughter of the last Master, and was both god touched and scorned by the town. Young and alone, she had a gift for healing that was most useful but also frightening to the townsfolk. They could not eschew her despite being fearful, which led to even more scorn and callousness. Behind her back, and sometimes to her face, they called her monster, and worse. None of this of course was understood by her “father”. Eventually the loneliness and anger (which she did not understand from the vantage point of a young girl) broke her. She turned to Lamashtu (was she not a monster already?) and torched the temple, fleeing to the tunnels beneath Thistletop that she had found in her wandering. There she followed Lamashtu’s path and convinced the goblins there to attack the village (they were even more monsters than she). This was the first test of The Pride; they helped thwart the invasion and eventually found their way in to Thistletop (at a steep cost, this was Javis’ first death as well as that of a local ranger) to defeat Nualia.

“And I have been thinking about this for the past 17 years” concluded Abstalar “and how we, the town, failed that poor young girl. We owe her restless spirit something better than eternal bitterness”

We all agreed this seemed so. Well, that is not entirely true. I did, Tom was sympathetic-he understood not having a place, Zunk agnostic and Hans concerned we were making excuses for Nualia’s exercise of freewill.

“A gesture of reconciliation is for the town as much as Nualia, my gnomeish friend. Her actions are her own, just as of course, were ours. And they were not without effect”.

“Jah, okay. This is an intellectually consistent argument at least. So what-build her stature in memorial? And what have we to do with this”?

“Well, an excellent question” replies Abstalar “We uh, need you to recover her bones. We needed a physical link to her, you see, to perform a cleansing ritual and when we raised her coffin we discovered what looks like a ghoul tunnel into the grave. They do that sometimes to find bones to gnaw on. It is a ghoul thing. Anyway, her bones are gone and I was erm, hoping you would investigate and hopefully recover them for us.”

You betcha. Although again it took a little discussion. “She was just a little girl” was my opinion “and this will give her and the town a way to go forward.” Tom was firmly in my corner, Zunk shrugged. “Jah, okay, well Brodert told us to keep our head down, and underground is definitely down” was how Hans considered it.

“So, priest” said Tom “what threats do ghouls represent? We might as well be prepared”

This set off a round of conversation that ended with Abstalar excusing himself to rummage around for some things to help. He returned with an assortment of potions, and a bow and a very lovely morningstar. “Healing draughts, he explained, “and some weapons that might be more effective than what you possess. You Tom, look well equipped, especially for a farmer. We keep them for our defense now” he added somewhat sadly “ so please return them when you return. And those tonics will help prevent disease that will turn you, so try not to get bit or scratched by the ghouls”

You betcha.

His Grace showed us to Nualia’s grave, alone at the corner of the yard. “It was the only place the townsfolk would accept” he offered. “Hopefully we will set this bad business aright, with your help. Godspeed” he added as he left.

A small shaft led from a corner of the grave sloping down. Zunk crept down, set the rope, and we descended into a cramped tunnel. Zunk ranged forward so he could scout without our light to reveal him, went some distance ahead, and then returned to bring us forward, and we repeated this several times. He was nearly out of my sight when I heard him whisper “shhhh” which was confusing as we knew to be silent, until I heard some high pitched voices, followed by Zunk saying something like “ your leader has returned” and more skittering words. This did not seem promising, and sure enough two figures appeared at the range of my vision to threaten Zunk.

I stung one with a beam of holy light, and closer now, I could see they looked much like Zunk, except very pale and scraggly. They stank of undeath. Goblin ghouls. Ghoublins, I guess. “ Don’t get bit” I murmured to myself.

Tom stepped up to trip the one I had burnt, and a bolt of electricity from Hans fried them. The ghoublins seemed fixed on Zunk, but he evaded their blows and ended the one on the ground, while Tom tripped the other one as I flailed about poorly. Bolts from Hans chunked into the one on the ground, and then it was encompassed with a dark energy from one we had not noticed; unhealing.

The one the ground made whole was joined by another. Zunk struck one of them hard, while Tom pushed it back and then tripped the second. I did much damage to the air, but the ghoublins couldn’t hit us either, so there it stood. The ghoublin in the corner, which we now could see, looked different the others, waved its hands and the one on the ground was unhealed. Another one joined the fray.

Emboldened by the energy it had received, the prone ghoublin arose, and Tom’s blade turned swift as a snake and stuck it down. “Fuck that guy” he muttered “I told him to lie still”.

Zunk reversed their dagger in a blink and stuck down, driving the blade into the ghoublins neck and then reversed their blade again as they slid towards the other, and drove the dagger up through the chin.

Another foe appeared (I swung and missed, naturally) as electricity from Hans flickered. Caster ghoublin steps back and unleashes a terrible painful sound so intense it did us harm. Tom is flanked so Zunk steps, makes to stab high but at the last second, takes advantage of the off balance ghoublin and guts it from below. I swung at the one behind Tom, hoping to distract it, but somehow it blundered into my arc and I stove its head in. I don’t love killing undead to be honest, but at least there is no blood and gore.

Only the strange one left, which hissed at Zunk “Imposter! Our leader will kill you for your lie” and made to strike. instead, I clubbed it pretty hard, and it is not clear who was more surprised. Its look of shock increased as Tom buried his blade in its chest and the milky eyes closed forever.

“Huh, those are ghouls, eh?” I say. “They were horrible little creatures. Ah, no offense, Zunk”.

“None taken, m’am” he replied courteously.

“Now Tom, let me take a look at that bite to make sure it’s not infected. You would be an awful goblin”
