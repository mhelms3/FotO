Season 1, Episode 4: The Island of Rust

We escorted the morose Vanda to the gate; she and Tom made for a big pile of depression! Vanda left us, and we found the pen and the sick ram and I gave it the medicine from Elsie. I must confess I almost forgot, but I was still seeing the horrid split faced man in my mind’s eye. Why would someone set that thing upon their brethren?

Vanda was at Elsie’s when we arrived and the acolytes and the blacksmith too. “There they are-the ones that saved us” one of them croaked, but at least this was a firmer voice than the near death whisper of before. They were recovering, Milani be praised!

There were other townies in the store as well, and at least they weren’t calling us pig diddlers, or whatever. Our arrival set off a jumble of everyone talking at once, asking why and what and how, and even some words of thanks and praise. It was nice to see what a little good could do.

“Ah..ahem?” says Hans, and then again, a little louder. “I know you have many questions and so I, we…, will try to provide answers. So it all started when “ and he explains how we snuck into the temple and the things that happened after.

“What do you mean they won’t be bothering us again” says one of the townies “Are you sure we don’t need to fear these rusted thugs attacking us in our homes?”

“Ah,, jah says Hans. “I, we, my friends and I that is, have, uhhm, handled them. It. Rest assured they shall not trouble you”

“And oh, Vanda was quite magnificent, we couldn’t have slain the split faced thing without her” I added, trying to be helpful. Vanda sat head down the whole time, so still and silent and wrapped in misery the whole room had forgotten her. Vanda’s head moved fractionally, so at least I know she heard. I hope she felt better. “Oh, and Elsie, we found your sick friend and he is feeling better. You should get him tomorrow-there is no one left there to take care of him”. Stupid! Why did you put it like that?

“Friends” I said, “I know this talk of monsters and rust is very upsetting, but we will try to go save our friends and defeat whatever vile plans this Metreimar has set in motion, but we need your help. I know we can do this together!”

Murmurs of agreement among the townies, and also from one “We are not fighters-so what do you expect”? I look at Vanda but she has become a statue.

“I promise we will not put you in harms way, friends. First we need to rest, but we also need some supplies. Something, anything, cold iron would be nice. We have some coin, not much-we are villagers like you, but we don’t want to take advantage. “

Elsie is the first to offer her support as I thought she might. “You have done so much already. Take what you need of any of my basic supplies, and I have some droughts and the like that might be of use. I cannot afford to give them to you, but can offer them for what it cost me”.

“Here”, says the smith whose sword Tom carries. He reaches into his belt and tosses me a lump of metal. “Cold iron, it is, not enough for a weapon but perhaps it will serve you?”

“Oh thank you sir, this will do fine. We are most grateful for all your help. It still may be a a mortal threat to certain creatures we might meet, if it is used properly. It is a great boon, thank you!”

The crowd wandered out, worried and anxious, which is sound. I am too.

We moved the still weak acolytes to a spare room and I checked to see they were on the mend. I gave a prayer to Milani to speed them to health, hoping it was not a violation of their code, but it seemed best to offer what little help I might. Then we rested.
