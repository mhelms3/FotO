Season 2, Episode 4: Hemlocks and Harbingers

It was a lot of mending, and we needed some time. Hans inspected the stones and had little to offer only that it was ancient and powerful magic that would the best scholars years to unravel. We talked about what to do with the thing beyond the barricade. The choices were release it, kill it or let it be. I was pretty much against the third, and undecided on the second. More information seemed necessary-we had never even seen the thing, so I offered to go back to the room of the goat-headed fire demon and talk to it through the crack in the wall-maybe we could learn something to help us decide.

After a time we felt ready and headed back to that room with the strange door. It seemed I had not dealt with the warding magic completely. Tom took a horrible spear to the thigh when he entered. I apologized to Tom for my mistake, several times, I think, and used my last spells from Vistaro’s staff to make it nearly right. “sorry”, I murmured, for possibly the 4th time. “It’s alright Jhys” he said, ever stoic “we probably wouldn’t be here if not for you.” Well, that seemed true, actually. I think Vistaro would have looked favorably on my efforts.

Clever Zunk suggested to offer the thing some water, holy water specifically, and that seemed a good way to sort out what they might be. I approached the crack in the room trying to get a look at who we had been talking to. “Hello” I called through the rent in the stone, “It’s us. We’re back. Can we talk a little bit? Come over here so we can chat. What’s you name-mine is Jhys, and Tom and Zunk and Hans are here too”.

The thing was quite worried about the return of the fire demon, but relaxed when we convinced them the threat was over. “I’m Zorph” they said as I got a look, and options 2 and 3 started seeming more likely. It was a goblin, at least at some point. Now it was a hacked up ghoublin, pale, with a huge wound over half their face. I caught a whiff of grave scent. “It’s a ghoublin” I whispered to my friends. Somehow, I was worried it would feel judged. “ I don’t suppose you want, erm, any water”? “Nahhh, but if ya got a couple of bones to gnaw on, that would hit the spot”. My friends shifted uneasily.

But Zorph was talkative now they were safe, even friendly, and we coaxed them to tell their tale. It was quite the revelation. It was little confusing, working backwards from where we found them, but the basics were this. Their ghoublin “friends” threw them in this room and shut them in to prevent the fire demon from killing everyone. They (being the ghoublins) accidently released goathead by breaching its prison wall. “Demons can blip through space” they explained “ so once it could see through this teensy crack we be talking through, out it popped into this room to roast a couple of us, so I hid and the others blocked the door, trapping me. Luckily the thing went back to wherever it was and left me alone”. Holy smokes, I didn’t know demons could do that. Well, I didn't know anything about them, to be honest, but this was way worse than I had imagined.

Zorph was in this position having recently been turned into a ghoublin by “some guy named,, er,,what was it, Goldnuggget or something” “Ripnugget?” I supplied helpfully? “Yeah…that’s it, Ripnugget. I had just come from up north and found a small group o’cousins, camping out in the shadow of some old fort named Thistletop when we got bushwacked by this Ripnugget and a horde of goblin-ghouls-they killed some and turned the rest of us so we could look down here for some artifact, and Nugget’s sword. Really pissed off about not having it, and well, we did what it told us. Didn’t have much choice, being turned by it and all, and well, they’re an evil eyed flaming skeleton to boot. Got a real thing going on for the town above-gonna raise a host, it said, to wipe ‘em all out. I’m a ghoul, and they scare the crap outta me. Figuratively speakin o’course. I don’t think I can shit anymore. But I’m just getting the hang of this ghoul thing. So you guys gonna let me out now? It’s a little cramped in here.”

“Uh, just, you know, sit tight a little longer. We’re going to come around to the barricade”. I felt a little queasy at saying this, knowing the likely outcome. But I was still hoping.

“Are we really going to kill them?” I whispered as we made our way back. “I mean, they trusted us and told us some really important stuff. Sorry about your Gran, Zunk, by the way. And all they’ve done so far is gnaw on some old bones. That’s not so bad, right?”

“Well, we can’t exactly let him out to do evil things, jah?” replied Hans. “It is clear they have sense of civic responsibility or social compact. Who knows what they would do.” The others didn’t say anything, but their body language suggested they agreed. And democracy and all.

We returned to the room beyond which Zorph was trapped, and Tom removed the barricade. Zorph stepped out and cautiously looked around, clearly presenting no threat. “Hi” I smiled hoping it did not look too awkward. I stepped a little forward, hoping it was not too obvious I was in the way of any potential blade work, but everyone seemed to be waiting.

“So” I said, hoping to lead the witness, as Da might say. “What are you going to do know, that, uh, your free? All the others are dead, so you can, well, decide to do anything.”

“Well, free” they said “is a sort of new concept since I been a, well, you see. Don’t reckon I like the idea of going back to Ripnugget. They gonna be mad we ain’t found nothing, and I don’t want to see them mad, that’s certain. Maybe I just, you know..” and they shrug.

“Jah, let me ask you a question, Zorph. Suppose you found a baby, and there was no one around, and you knew no one would punish you if well, ate the baby? What would you do?”

Zorph seemed a little confused by this hypothetical and strange circumstance. “Answer carefully” I advised.

“Well, I guess if I weren’t to be punished, then maybe I’d nibble a couple of fingers or so. Yeah, I think they’d be like them little cakes with stuff inside, what they call lady fingers.”

“That is all? Hans replied archly, leading the witness for sure.

“Well” stammered Zorph, definitely blundering in the wrong direction “I guess maybe if nobody would mind, I might eat the whole thing, not wanting to waste and all.” Hans lifted an eyebrow at the other two. Uh oh.

“But anyway” continued Zorph, oblivious to the turning wheels of fate that were about to crush it “I probably should be on my way, and you ought to head back up and warn the town there’s gonna be a whole lot o blood if they aren’t careful. The sooner the better or it’s gonna to be bad for them.”

I pounced, “Now why would you care about that town” I asked smiling at Hans “You’re a ghoublin-don’t you hate them? Who cares what Ripnugget has for them?”

“Ahhh, no, I don’t hate ‘em” he replied.” It’s kind of a mutually beneficial relationship, I reckon. Like a sym-bol-icness, they call it. We chew on their dead bones, but they gotta be alive first to keep on making them bones for us, and can you imagine if all them bones started piling up everywhere-they gonna run out o’room. We clean the place up in return. I gotta long unlife in front of me, so I ain’t gonna rock the boat none.”

I smiled even more broadly at Hans, who was clearly irritated and confused by this display of civic responsibility.

“You make no sense” he shouted. “You’d eat a baby but save the village? Like the parents of the baby you eat don’t matter. Evil I say.”

“But” replied Zorph “You said I aint were to be punished, so I took from that nobody would mind. You didn’t say nothing about parents and all.”

Jah, I said you weren’t to be punished; you wouldn’t get caught and would get away with a horrible deed. You inferred the rest”.

“Well yeah” replied Zorph, as if it were obvious. “You didn’t say one way or the other, so I inferred. Nought else I could do.”

The logic was beyond reproach, and everyone, even Hans, I think, relaxed a little bit as the wheel of fate rolled by Zorph, missing him by a whisker. It seemed the right decision, I hoped it was the right decision. Vistaro didn’t tell me it would be so complicated.

We agreed, I guess, to let Zorph go with the promise there would be no baby eating. And they further agreed to forget we had met, and we agreed, in turn, to forget as well and not mention we had met Zorph or their friend Woogie. Shrug. Something like that. We left them wandering away to search for Woogie and some dry bones to gnaw. I wonder what Zunk thought of all this. Meeting undead cousins and knowing it was the fault of their Gran, who further, seemed bent on the destruction of Sandport. Perhaps that was what they were considering as we returned through the tunnels and found Abstalar having his supper with an elven woman.

Priests ate well, I’ll give them that. He put down his fork and stared at us a moment. We were whole more or less (Tom still had a slight limp I had no strength to put aright), but the scorch marks and smell of smoke told the tale well enough.

“Friends, I am so glad to see you, but honestly, you look terrible! Did you find Nualia’s bones? What transpired with the…” and here Abstalar gestured vaguely to the blackened sooty stains we all were marked with.. “fire”? “Tell me all that has happened. And oh, this is Arlen-we were talking over the ritual for Nualia, but we can discuss this later. What happened down there”?

We told him pretty much everything, from the return of Ripnugget and the ghoublin host they are raising to murder the town, to the artifact and the sword, and the demons. Hans observed somewhat frostily how it was “virtually ineffably inconceivable” that a Thalassonian ruin was beneath church and nobody knew. “Its and old town” replied the priest, as if that explained everything. “Anyway” he continued “this is obviously a serious matter and the authorities need to hear this ill news. But these appear to be the bones of Nualia-see the elongated left forearm?” (I did, and was embarrassed to have failed to do so earlier) “That is nearly definitive-she had begun some sort of transformation that the heroes kept her from completing. We should perform the ritual soon. Arlen, perhaps you should take it from here.

Arlen, who seemed quite shy, quietly explained the requirements-a bright moon and cloudless sky were important for her patron. She was somewhat more relaxed once we had established I was a devotee of Milani, and we were sisters of a sort, or maybe distant cousins. “We have maybe two days that are favorable. Abstalar and I will invoke the gods and seek to call Nualia to her remains so we may converse. But the ritual can be jarring to those who have passed. She may still think she is in her death throes, or maybe 1000 years have gone by in bitterness and woe. There is no telling. Jhysilein, I would like it if you could..”

“Yes! Of course. I will help in whatever way I can”

“..and your friends? Would they be..”

“Uh, yes! Well I hope so anyway, I would feel better if they were there too”. I smiled either hopefully or desperately, I wasn’t sure.

“We got your back, Jhys. Says Zunk, with a toast. Tom added “We’re there for you.” Hans nodded. We’re a good team-I’m lucky to have them!. How strange is it that the people I know least I trust the most. Rule #2, I think.

So it was decided. We would rest and perform the ritual tomorrow. But before that we needed to tell our news to the Cpt of the Guard, Bearm Hemlock. Oh-so exciting-I knew him from A Kitty’s Tail. Promqeth thought he was a ponce, and stole his pen! (eventually giving it back, to be fair). They needed to post guards and get ready for Ripnugget. We would probably have to venture back down to the catacombs in search of the artifact. But first things first, so to Hemlock we went.

We arrived as the guard office began to close up. The men in front were reluctant to bother Hemlock who was “putting on his coat” which from the stress on the syllables was code for “wanting not to be bothered”. Zunk managed to convince the officers to call Hemlock by mentioning we were sent by Abstalar and one of them men opened the office door just in time for Hemlock to stride out, looking somewhat awkard.

“Hrmmm..what this, more adventurers? Last thing I need now” he grmphd as he shrugged into his coat. Hans and Zunk had skillfully positioned themselves in his way, so Hemlock was forced to stop and he glared down at them. There might have been pastry crumbs on his mustache.

Hans and Zunk were their charming and relentlessly gracious selves, explaining our deal with the priest, and all that happened since.

“So we, that being Abstalar and us, thought we should warn ya’ something bad is coming”, concluded Zunk.

“Well,” he sputtered. I had noticed a slight tic appear at the mention of Ripnugget. “Erm, potentially grave news yes, best considered after a good rest, I say. This could be Ripnugget back from the grave those, heroes, sent him to. Or it could be the ravings of a mad ghoul”. He extracted a time piece from his pocket and looked at it. “Please let us reconvene at 11 tomorrow, shall we?

Hans leapt in. “Is it not hasty, perhaps even a dereliction of your civic duty to not take this seriously? If you act and it is nothing, you are now the wiser and Sandpoint is safe. If you fail to act and it is something, you will have failed duty” That tic again. “And of course” continued Hans. Third, if you act and..” I think at this point I may have blurted out “You know the history of this town. Do you really want to make the same mistake twice?” (I mean, maybe that was a little unfair-he was not directly responsible for the Festival Day Massacre, but still. It was infuriating to be put off like this.)

Hemlock looked down at Hans and blinked. He jerked his head around to the guards and spat “Why was I not immediately informed of this? What were you thinking? Never mind, rouse the guard, post watches. I want boots out on the ground NOW”.

“There” Hemlock says to Hans “Never let it be said I neglect my civic responsibilities. I will go see Abstalar now and confer. Unless you have other, suggestions”? Tic. This seemed a bad way to end things.

“Oh, this is so exciting” I burbled. “You knew the heroes before they were even the heroes. You are in A Kitty’s Tail, aren’t you”? Hemlock did a reasonable imitation of pretending to think about it for a moment, before he allowed this was so-he might have been mentioned, a few times in fact. “Oh, I’ve read he books a whole bunch of times, I know all about you”. I was betting he hadn’t read the books recently. Luckily, I guessed right and he was moved to offer to sign my newly acquired book. We parted on considerably better terms.

Back at the Rusty Dragon, we quickly took to our rooms-we were still singed and stank of brimstone and smoke, which likely would have attracted attention. We ordered room service. Everyone passed on the hagfish stew in eel jelly, and the boiled giant squid eyes in “special sauce”. Zunk and Tom had braised manticore flank with fermented ramps, Hans and I mushroom risotto-I reckoned as perhaps did Hans-this was the safest choice.. The mushrooms glowed; it was like chewing a forest.

“Hrmm” I mumbled around the first bite, “The food might have gone down hill a bit” But everyone ate, and there was little conversation. We wandered off to sleep.

It was lovely the next day, and after we picked up my new rune-inscribed morningstar from the smith (this would be the most powerful weapon in Osprey, I’d bet. How strange), we went to Brodert’s for Hans. Much of the day remained, so I went to the beach and prepared myself for the ritual. The others decided to explore, and I heard later they found a potion shop, conveniently located next to a brothel. We ate a light meal and headed to the church right before moonrise.

Aurelio, the gangly one we had met before lead us to a large inner chamber, where Abstalar and Arlen had carefully set Nualia’s bones. We gathered in a rough circle around them, and the two priests began to chant words I did not recognize. They were deep in it after a couple of minutes, their eyes rolled back, and if they were aware of anything it certainly wasn’t us. Ghostly lights twinkled in the vaulted gloom, and after a couple minutes more, coalesced into two glowing figures of indeterminate race, age and sex, dancing stately in a circle around us.

Suddenly, Aurelio bounded in, stumbling on his words as well as his feet. “They’re breaking the door down. They got pitchforks and hammers, the townspeople. They’re not happy about the ritual”. There was a splintering sound and cracking and an angry jumble of voices. A mob poured into the room. I recognized the two Scarnazzi thugs from the Dragon.

I stayed with the two priests, whispering that interrupting the ritual would be dangerous, and trusted my friends to handle the mob. People were confused and angry, and had gotten the wrong idea-that we were summoning something to do evil. Or been told. One man, in particular seemed to be inciting the anger. He was better dressed and smooth talking, accusing us of conducting a fell ritual. Hans and Zunk explained the truth; to resolve the ill will with Nulia’s shade, to help cleanse the town of bad spirits that if lingered could do more harm. Hasn’t Abstalar always acted in the best interests of Sandpoint and it’s people, our friends asked.

Smooth-talker kept trying to steer things the other way; the two thugs acted as foil, but Zunk and Hans were more persuasive and the crowd, somewhat embarrassed, wandered away. “This.Is.Not.Over.” hissed Smooth-talker as he stomped away, trailed by the two thugs.

“Boy, the Cam Scarnazzi is a piece of work, right?” offered Aurelio, after they had gone.

We had little time to puzzle over why this crime family was so interested in thwarting us, because something had changed in the ritual. The dancers had grown more substantial during the time we dealt with the mob, and the dancing faster, almost frenzied. But erupting up from the floor in several places were skeletal arms attempting to clutch the dancers. Perhaps to drag them away, or cause them to stumble, but this was obviously not part of the ritual. Something was intruding.

I leant my prayers to the other two priests and was sucked into a vortex of energy that nearly blinded and deafened me. I felt the presence of the other priests, and this was the only thing that kept me from flying apart. Strangely, the dancers seemed more real to me now, and I could see the desperation and the panic on their faces as the black hands clawed and clutched at them, ankles barely out of reach. Zunk approached one of the arms with a vial of holy water, and it somehow popped away to reappear on the other side of the circle. I was straining, pouring all I could into the ritual. We were holding our own now, but Abstalar and Arlen had been at this for quite some time and I could feel them starting to flag. Just then, I felt the presence of another, and then another still as Hans and Tom joined their strength to the effort. The forces on me lessened as the dancers rose higher, out of reach of the clutching hands, and their steps quickened and became more assured. Three bright flicks of moonlight struck around us and another larger one, the circle’s center, blowing away the mind storm. I came fully to my senses to see three smoking holes around us, and a young woman in resplendent red armor floated in the air above us. Nualia had returned and she looked none too happy about it.

The two priests were still entranced, but I knew from my joining with them that they would not come out of it till Nualia was gone. Their voices were strong and confident, buoyed by the energy we had given them and they would be able to keep going for awhile now the veil between us and the spirit world had been pieced. But it was up to us to entreat with Nualia, but she was not like to be receptive.

“You” she hissed, fixing on Abstalar “why have you brought me back here? Have I not suffered enough at your hands? This place, these people have taken everything from me-there is nothing left to take, curse you”.

“They aren’t here to take from you” I said, “but to give. They offer their deep apologies for what has happened to you and their role in it. They have made a great effort to bring you here, and at some risk, and hope you will be able to forgive them, as they have forgiven you. You can all move forward if you allow it.”

“I am so sorry for what happened to you” I was crying. “you were just a girl”

“Bahh they made me into this” she raised her left arm, which was too long for her frame, with long fingers ending in wicked claws “and they send two priests and a handful of sell swords to make amends? They expect their debts to be so cheaply erased”? Still, it seemed the white hot rage in which she appeared was somewhat banked. She was at least negotiating.

“Jah you have suffered greatly, and that must be reckoned with, which is why you have been called. To know the townsfolk are ashamed, and afraid. They see they have created a stain here, and hope by acknowledging their guilt, you will forgive this abuse.”

“Abuse” she shrieked, white hot again. “You have no idea. Do you think I desired this? I was manipulated, provoked and humiliated, and forced to become what I was. They made me this terrible bed, is it not just they should lie in it”? But something had broken; her face, only angry before, showed flickers of sadness and regret and maybe, weariness.

“Look” offered Zunk “I do not know what happened to you here, but I do know humiliation. I’m a gnome, we live beneath the humans, in stature as well as location, mostly living by their neglect and their dross. Tolerated only if we are useful, and reminded of it always. But the way they really win is when we poison ourselves and become only bitterness and rage. The one thing they cannot take away, at least if you don’t let them, is yourself. Are you going to let them?

Her eyes narrowed and she peered at Zunk, seeking perhaps some subterfuge or guile. I wondered when the last time it was that someone offered her nothing but kind blunt counsel.

“Do what you want” Tom shrugged. “You have been given good advice to which nothing further can be added. But I will tell you this” he continued, voice quite firm “My friends here speak nothing but the truth, both in regards to the town, but also in their concern for you. Take it as you will.”

Hah” she sneered “It is good you do not care if I trust you for I trust nobody, and have not for a long time.” This was surprising-we were approaching normal conversation, except for the fact we were talking to a shade of a priestess of monsters.

“You have given me things to think about. You have been respectful, neither prevaricating nor fawning with false promises. So I will give you some things to think about as well. When I said I was manipulated, it was more true than you know. Chopper and I were the first of the dooms. There are seven. Not all who have plagued Sandpoint lie still, and new enemies work in the shadows. I do not wish these seven dooms upon Sandpoint, but one does, and that one hearkens on red wings. Unless you can delve the Pit in time, you will not escape his fires.”

“I leave you now” she glanced sharply (with a hint of something else) at Tom. “Take it as you will.” I noticed her left arm was no longer disfigured. Huh-wonder when that happened. And then she was gone, and Abstalar and Arlen collapsed to the ground.
