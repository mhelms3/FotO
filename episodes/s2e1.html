Season 2, Episode 1: Sandpoint Awaits

As Javis related, we are bound for Sandpoint, the place where the heroes also began our journey, to seek a man who might know something about the horn we prised from Metriemar. I was only half listening, instead thinking how fantastic this all was, to have met the members of the Pride. And I wondered what my Da would/will say to this, or even if he would believe me. I think he secretly loved those stories as much as I did; he admired particularly the stoic, wise Vistaro. I clutched the staff even tighter. But also, there was a lot of blood and death.

Hans has been busy writing and figuring. “I have been thinking; our group is a micro-society and we should have governing principles. For us to thrive as a community, we must balance individualism with the greater good. To this end, I propose that we vote on a group acquisitions tax rate. That is to say, let us choose some fraction of our gains be taxed for our community’s benefit. These funds will be used to ensure synergy and efficiency in our endeavors — the best allotment of which can be voted on democratically, or determined by an authoritarian Hans… erm handler... to keep the process orderly and expedient. While we may debate this tax rate, and it may have to be re-evaluated as we progress through our journey, I would like to pose a preliminary rate of 50%. The remaining funds will be distributed evenly between members.”

“I secondarily suggest that individual members of our society be allowed to propose an item be classified as a ’tool of the society’ — please, no jokes… this is not the time —.. “ he trailed off. I hope it was not because of my vacant look. I am sure these were totally worthwhile suggestions, but I was tired and my mind was elsewhere. Anywhere.

“Jah, let us perhaps take this up in the morning then”.

We put off discussions of coin. We could never tell exactly where Javis was, and it was not like we didn’t trust him, but well, I guess we didn’t. I couldn’t get over the undeath smell, myself so I tried to be where he wasn’t. I hoped that what he was often munching on was a bag of peanuts, and tried to ignore how wet they looked. Were boiled peanuts a thing? He said little, and his promised explanation consisting of telling us we must seek out a scholar named Brodert Quint and show him the horn. Oh, and that we should bring him some sausage rolls from Sandpoint Savories. I wondered again about the bag of “peanuts.”

It was a strange trip. The sea was made for brooding. But nothing could be stranger than what we already lived through, and that was somehow comforting. I thought of what Vistaro would say, something like, “and yet, you all have survived; sometimes that is the best you can do”.

And so, after 3 days, we saw Sandpoint by the cracking light of dawn and rowed ashore. The first person we saw was a grizzled seaman with a salt-and-pepper beard and a perpetual squint. We greeted him good morning and asked of a place we might get some food, and perhaps lodging. “Aye” he said, “Most of the adventurers seek out the Rusty Dragon. Up there a few streets and then right. Good day, travelers”. That was apparently as much as we rated and he turned his attention back to bundling his nets.

The Rusty Dragon looked old, but was well kept, and a small gnarled woman who named herself B’ethena greeted us as we entered the main room. They were setting up, but would see what might be available and she left us to it. Brusque, but not unkind; so different from the poorly hid hostility of the citizens in Iron Harbor (at least at first).

We started to discuss what must be done, but before this, Hans quite rightly pointed out we could resupply here and so should consider the splitting of our hard won coin and goods. He made many suggestions. Many. Some of which were quite good. Eventually we agreed on how it should be done, although I confess I did not pay so much attention once it became clear I could transfer the magic from Meitremar’s weapon to my own. Hans was a bit put out I did not just take the weapon and didn’t really seem to understand it was important to me to wield a Morningstar to honor Milani. No matter, this would make me less useless- no, Vistaro would say, “better able to protect my friends” so it was fine. But everyone was quite accepting of the idea that whatever made one of us stronger helped us all, and everyone was content. I was grateful nobody put their purse before the friends; it is very rare to have companions of such character.

Somehow, it was still very early after all the decision making and the acquiring of rooms. Zunk paid B’ethena a generous amount over the requested price and she instantly became less brusque. Zunk knows what they are doing. The innkeeper helpfully gave us the locations of a smithy, Quint’s hangouts, and the local caster society so we set about our business.

The smith was professional and courteous, happy to take our coin and assuring delivery in 4 days. It was hard to give over my precious Morningstar, but it was in good hands, and I still had my hammer. The Curious Goblin, a parlour and bookstore, was Quint’s favored ground so we headed there. The proprietor was named Chesty or Chestman or something; I immediately stopped paying much attention. He was the overgrown version of the fisherboys I knew; self-absorbed and blustery, sorting women into two classes-available and not.

“Ah welcome adventurers” he said. He was the friendliest townie we had encountered, but there was more than a little performance behind it, perhaps. “It is always a pleasure to meet another band of brave seekers. I used to be an adventurer myself, and had quite a reputation. Perhaps you have heard” He extended his had for mine, lips pursed. Oh, yuk. I smiled politely and introduced myself, leaving the hand questing. More sweetness. I am pretty sure I managed to avoid rolling my eyes.

Eventually I think he sorted me into the “unavailable” bin, or perhaps the “not yet available” bin and moved on, greeting each in turn. His best smile was reserved for Zunk, chortling how it was such an unexpected delight to have a goblin standing in his so-named establishment, and launched quite unbidden into how the Curious Goblin acquired its name. He was quite the talker, again managing to mention his adventuring past (eyeing me for any change in my reaction, of which there was none), while relaying the name came from a poem written by his grandfather. Quite the big deal in its day, apparently. Zunk bought a copy of collected works, which set off another round of, flirting, I think, ultimately concluding in a offer from Chesty to perform a reading. We of course, must attend, staring hard at Zunk and then me. I was bemused, and maybe a little irritated although I was not sure why.

I felt a little bad and wanted to not seem so standoffish, and so inquired about whether he had stories of The Pride, thinking this was perhaps the perfect place. I bought “The Complete and Authorized A Kitty’s Tale” which contained some stories new to me, and beamed with satisfaction, complementing him on his wonderful store. Chesty, now buoyed by the attention and coin responded in good cheer to our inquiries about Quint. He related that Quint was indeed a frequent customer, and baring other activities, generally showed up midmorning.

“Ahh, good good” says Hans “we have a most interesting ancient artifact we are most keen to show him” and Zunk chose that moment to have a coughing fit before Hans offered more. The somewhat awkward silence lingered for a bit till Hans recovered and exclaimed “I see across the way Sandpoint Savories, I suggest we visit this establishment and procure nibbles for our friend. Hopefully Brodert will show up in the meantime”

Hans bought a truly impressive amount of sausage rolls of various types. “jah, we should be thorough-we don’t know what he likes” and Zunk took this opportunity to probe the baker’s memories of Javis. Beyond recalling him slightly, an impressive feat given the time span, she had little to say. Hans took the sausages to a side table, the baker moving on to other customers, where he took out a small case and put them inside. It must have been something given to him by Javis to carry his treats. Just as I thought we would leave, Hans opened the satchel again and murmured “ Jah, well look at this” and reached inside. and he pulled out a small pouch, which upon opening, revealed an abundance of gold coin, maybe a hundred by my count! (you get good at this type of thing having to sort fish all day long). Oddly, his arm disappeared into the bag up to his elbows when he retrieved the coin.

Another strange gift from our friends.

Sadly Brodert had not appeared at the Curious Goblin by the time we returned, but Chesty helpfully told us where Quint lived; the last house before the ancient Thalassonian ruins. It would not be hard to find. “Why” says Hans “the local arcanist school is most conveniently located on the way. Nearly. Maybe a short detour, but it would be more efficient to go there on the way, hmmm”?

Maybe not the best argument he’d ever made, but he was clearly excited about the possibility, and it would be unkind to deny him this little joy. Things have been rough. Perhaps everyone else saw as I did, and we all agreed.

It was even better than I expected! The mage society shared the building with The School for Wayward Youth, established by Promqeth after The Pride defeated Kharzoug. I could not believe they were unaware of this, and of its great history as having produced some of the most skilled diplomats, storytellers and yes, rogues, of this age.

I was so distracted relating all of this that I barely noticed the somewhat dour figure behind a desk that fronted large ornate doors. “Adventurers” he intoned, dry and musty “I regret that we do not give tours or visitations, so please leave us to our business” He did not sound regretful at all, and the unasked for advice that maybe “the Rusty Dragon would be more amenable to, ah, your tastes” was not kindly spoken.

“But you see” says Hans “I understand what great things you pursue here as I am a fellow seeker of arcane skills. I wish perhaps, only a small visit to your library to study, and hope you will grant me some time there in the spirit of those, such as yourself, devoted to knowledge”.

“Bahh” came the reply. “How could one such as you even hope to comprehend the simplest contents of the tomes we have in our possession? Oh tell me, what grand knowledge do you possess? Only those scholars we judge worthy can join our society and have access. Remove yourselves at once. Don’t you have beer to quaff, or something? ”

“Hey” I shouted “You can’t talk to my friend like that. He may be small, but he is mighty. He has battled demons!”

The arched eyebrows of the deskwarden suggested dismissal.

“ah, perhaps” says Hans, “ I can convince you my background is meritorious. Have you head of Belmarious? Well of course you have, jah. I have met the red armored woman myself, recently in fact and..”

“Nonsense” interjected deskwarden “How dare you try such an infantile deception? There are a thousand likenesses of the rune lord you could have seen. And she has been absent for generations. The crudeness of your con affirms what an ignorant simpleton you are”

The blood left Hans’ face. “Fine, fine” he spat “ foolishly ignore what is in front of you. You will never know the nature of this artifact we..”

We hustled him out

“…have obtained and which I will not share with you”!

“Hans, mate” whispers Zunk “perhaps we should keep this to ourselves, just in case.”

Hans nodded glumly, still stung by the contemptable little flunky and his bad attitude. I knew exactly what he was feeling and laid my hand on his shoulder “Let us see Quint, perhaps he can offer more than just identifying the horn”.

Brodert was home when we arrived, greeting us at the door. “Sorry adventurers, no tours today” and turned to leave. Hans explained the nature of our visit which was met with skepticism. “An ancient artifact hmmm? If I had a copper piece for every adventurer with this claim, I would own the town. And you got this where”?

“Fish and Fiddlesticks” I yelled. We were told by the Pride, and I know you know who I mean because you have met them many times, to seek you out so you can help. Us. Do whatever it is we need to do.”

“Well, this is a new story” he replied “But quite fantastic. You expect me to believe you were recently in the company of the Heroes who have not been seen for nearly two decades?”

I whispered to Zunk that maybe the dagger he was gifted would supply some evidence; the goblin raid left a deep mark on the town, and Brodert was witness to them. He nodded, and withdrew the blade, carefully with two fingers and said to Quint “Hey friend, look at this. The dagger was given to me by the great rogue his orangey self. He said it was from here, or at least that seemed to be part of what he was meaning. Does this not prove our tale. Do ya think anyone could actually have stolen something from him? Certainly not I, he’d carve me up like a fish. Bet he could do it with a teaspoon before I could blink”

Brodert rubbed his chin, and looked at least. Baby steps. “Well, I’ll grant you small one that it is the work of the Thistletop clan, and could be Ripnugget’s own, but still this does not prove anything. You’d be surprised at the things I have found in a trader’s bargain bin. If you had something more personal to prove your claim, but no. I’ll not be dragged into this by way of an old knife.

Tom stepped up “Look old man-I did not get this in a bargain bin” and held up the amulet he had been given. “What, please” says Quint, “this dissembling does not become you and…” and then his eyes grew wide as saucers. “Viktor’s amulet” he choked out, somewhere between a whisper and a croak. “How did you get this, son”?

“By the gods” I said. Maybe shouted “Why do all you men not listen? I told you! From the heroes. “Promqeth gave that dagger to Zunk, and Tom got that from his father! Look. I mean really look. Can you not see the family resemblance?

Tom helpfully put his hands to his ears, covering the points that bespoke of his mothers’ lineage.

The eyes got even bigger, which hardly seemed possible, and he grabbed Tom by the waistcoat and dragged him in, us following. “No talking until I say. None” and put his finger to his lips. We followed him down hallways and several flights of stairs, clearly going underground some distance from the house; the walls at some point went from wood to stone and came to a stone door. “Inside” he hissed, and herded us in. The door was thick and the inner side carved with runes. In fact, the whole walls were covered and I could almost hear the little gears in Hans’ head as he tried to work them out.

Brodert gestured at the walls “These make it safe to talk here” and drew a breath. “I am sorry my friends, but many weird folk have come to me with fantastic tales. It is the ruins, I believe. Let us start anew. I am Brodert, and you <gesturing at Tom> are Thomas Fidelus. We have met a few times before although you probably were too young recall. Please introduce me to your friends, and then tell me everything, from the beginning”.

Brodert was clearly troubled by the end of our story. Not actually about blood or beheadings and the like, but of Belmarius and the horn. “I do not understand much more than you now, but I will do some digging. But the appearance of a rune lord is not a good thing. I do not think we can destroy this thing <gesturing at the horn>; I doubt even Stan could do it, but we can least understand who seeks it and why. It is good you have come to me. This will attract a lot of interest that is best to avoid. Of course, you have told nobody about this, correct”?

“Well, jah” says Hans “We might have mentioned it to a couple of times, once to your friend the bookseller and the other at the academy, but” he added somewhat peevishly “their reactions were the same as yours. They think us either insane or con men”.

Brodert put his hands to his face. “Okay, okay” So probably we can handle that. Look, stay out of sight. Maybe even leave town for a couple of days. I’ll keep the horn-it will be safely hidden here. And say nothing”!

Brodert’s level of paranoia was impressive. He insisted on giving us a tour of the ruins (I couldn’t believe it!) and instructed us to be obvious about payment once we reached the surface. We were paying customers of the standard sort. When Zunk presented, with a flourish and a “My good man”, 4 coppers, anyone observing the scene would see the face of a merchant about to get bilked. It was a lovely ruse, and quite amusing to Zunk, who winked and produced a much more appropriate payment. The tour had been a grand one, and it was into the late afternoon before we returned to The Dragon. It already contained a small crowd and were led to a side table. Zunk alternated between conversing with us and chatting with the innkeeper behind the bar. At one point he returned, telling us to keep an eye and ear out for Annika, or Aelric or Annabel or something. I was a little too stressed to pay attention. There were so many people, strangers, and it was getting quite loud.

Naturally, I spilled my water, and when I bent to retrieve my glass I caught sight of a table of 3 hooded men paying us too much attention. I whispered as much to Zunk, and Hans said “ Ah, they are probably taken by your ah, feminine wiles” I shot him a look “Hans, that is the stupidest thing I think I will ever hear you say!” and glared. I was still annoyed by Chesty. “I think you underestimate Hans” murmured Tom, which did in fact make me giggle. “Anyway” I said, trying to make up for my sharpness “I hardly think they are interested in me. Like that, I mean”.

I was not at all taking Hans’ suggestion seriously; he had 2 drinks and he is a small fellow. Zunk made another trip to the bar after I suggested knowing more about those guys was wise. While he was there, a woman stomped in, looked around and made a bee line to the empty seat next to him. They obviously exchanged words, or rather she said something, and then she turned, shot me a cold look and departed clomping as she came. When Zunk joined us, he reported the 3 were men in the Scarlatti gang, a powerful, old and not entirely above board clan. “They’re probably just checking out the new arrivals. It’s fine. Keep yer wits though and don’t be wandering around alone”. You betcha.

“What was the other thing about, you know, the woman-she seemed rather rude?”

“Aye well, she was a friend of the Curious Goblin. We missed the reading and the bookseller was quite put out. We may have to eat some crow”

Ah, yes. In all that happened I had forgot! Maybe being involved in some world ending plot has benefits. I lingered awhile longer and then retired. It was an interior room lacking windows, so the only way in was that door. Amazingly, I was not scared; I knew my friends would have my back and expected no visitations, although I kept my hammer within reach.
