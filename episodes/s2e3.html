Season 2, Episode 3: Flame On!

After a time, we explored our surroundings and spied a tunnel leading to a small alcove containing an altar, something shrouded atop it. Bones it turned out, not gnawed, of a human. Nualia’s? Even if not, this was an ill place for a final rest so I wrapped them carefully in the shroud and placed them in the strange satchel Hans had obtained from Javis. They fit easily-the thing was somehow bigger on the inside. Thank you Javis.

We followed Zunk through a chamber where a pile of wood leaned against one of the walls, a crude barricade it seemed. Tom shushed us “I hear something on the other side”. He called out, and a weak voice croaked in return “ Quiet” it pleaded “don’t wake the fire monster. If it finds us it will burn us to a crisp a second”.

It was hard to understand the voice between its panic, and the muffling from the barrier. All we could make out was it was scared and trapped by some awful creature born of fire that killed his comrades. He thought its den was northwest of him. It would be well against Milani’s wishes for me to leave him there, and fortunately we all seemed of the same mind, although Zunk muttered “Aye, there’s something scyvey about that one. I don’t trust him” We promised we would try to find this creature and come back for him.

It seemed our best hope was to go north from the room with the ghoublins, so that’s what we did in the same manner as before with Zunk leading. The corridor looped north and then south, I think. Not obviously in the wrong direction.

Zunk led us through a chamber that had been breached by a recent tunnel in one wall, and into another half taken up by a pile of rubble that blocked the far door. ZunK studied the scene for a moment then stepped back and looked. Pointing up, he said, “Now that’s what you call a trap door.” I was impressed he managed it with a straight face, and shocked I could find this funny. He continued “They must have tripped it trying to go through that door. I’m beginning to wonder a little bit more about our friend back there”.

Yeah, maybe Rule #2 again. But still, we couldn’t leave him there.

We cleared the rubble and there was a body just as Zunk predicted. It was too crushed to be readily recognizable beyond something having two arms and two legs. We carefully set it aside and then opened the door. We followed hallway around until eventually we found a door in a blind alcove. And not just any door, but one scrawled with terrible runes. They were fascinating in a horrible way, of evil intent but hard to look away-like an accident that you know is coming. I could not read them precisely, but I could tell their form and the direction of their intent.

“Uhh, this may sound crazy” I stammered, ‘but I think I can thwart it with Milani’s grace. But I have never channeled in this way. Anyone, uh, have other ideas”? They did not. But Hans used his wand to protect me from fire “Just in case, jah, to be on the safe side. Okey dokey then.

I knelt and began to sing. Milani’s hymns have no words as such, but syllables and melody and rhythm, up and down and meandering, like through a dreamy moon lit meadow bathed by the light that helps the innocent and heals the infirm. These songs were not designed to summon her, for she is always here, but to free the mind and open one up to her ever-present grace so that it might be directed, with of course her consent.

As I sang, a soft light gathered around the door and resisting this, the runes began first to glow, in vile sharp colors, and then squirm as the two energies contested. I increased my voice and focused and the colors became brighter and more threatening, and so on until I could not sing any louder or with more conviction. Please Milani I wispered, there are innocents at stake. The pressure was nearly unbearable, I was at my end. A bright flicker, almost overtipping, and then the glow slowly faded and the runes quieted, looking somehow lifeless even though they were just..runes. I guess.

I took a long breath. “Thank you Milani, I whispered I think it is safe now”. I shall open the door.”

And the horned figure with the flaming sword who was behind the door conjured a fire ball and threw it into our midst, scorching us badly. “Run” Zunk yelled, and we did.

Well, the others did, around the corner. I lumbered into the hallway and thinking my friends were around the far corner of the hallway, I stopped in an alcove and drank a healing draught-there was no sense in grouping up, and I was the most fire proof. I guess I hoped it would come for me, which it did, blazing around the corridor enwreathed in flame. It was ugly and violent looking, goat horned and hooves clacking, flames licking the greatsword it carried and trailing around its arm like a deadly garland. Tom moved in (I had forgotten the room to our north, where he must have sheltered and hopefully healed himself with a potion), and tripped it, allowing me a slight advantage so I thunked it.

It spat some kind of smoke that expanded greatly and was lost to my sight. I felt sick and heard someone choking, probably Tom. But my previous fight with the demon in Iron Harbour had not been lost on me. I had researched rituals on the boat, finding one that cleansed the air of poison, and so I channeled power and clapped my hands. The smoke around me dissipated and a ring of clear air travelled away from me like the ripples made by a skipping stone slung into water. The cloud faded away from Milani’s grace.

The demon spit a stream of fire striking Tom full in the chest and he went down, and it thunked me with its fiery sword for good measure. Zunk appeared behind it, probably hidden by the smoke I had just cleansed, and stuck it with their blade, unleashing a gout of burning blood that smoked Zunk’s arm. Hans emerges from around the corner down the hall and taps his spell book releasing a ghostly page that seemed to flow into him. He cast some spell and I saw the demon smile. “oh funny, little tasty mage” it growled “I was already enjoying this-but you have added to my good humor. I shall save you for last as a token of my appreciation.” I was pretty sure this was not what Hans was expecting,

I channeled more of Milani’s light into a shield, as it struck at me and missing, withdrew. I step back and see Tom in the room above, looking very grave. If he feels like me, he can’t stand to drink a healing draught, so use my power and make him nearly whole. He proceeds to vomit out black smoke and rasps at me to do it too, which I do and am able to drink a potion myself. Too little too late-the thing spits fire at me and the next thing I know, Zunk is reviving me. “Ouch-that hurt” was all I could say as I stand up. Magic darts from Hans strike goat head as Tom trips it. Nobody other than me is hurt. I think one of the things Vistro was trying to tell me was that among my many duties, I must include myself. I can’t protect them if I am dead, so I grant myself Milan’s boon.

The thing stands on its cloven hooves, allowing Tom to land a massive blow, but still it rises and lands an equally deadly strike on Tom, cutting and burning. And then stomps him to the ground with cloven hoof. But I am ready.

Zunk closes and slices it, while Hans steps around the corner again, and looking at the bloody scene, blanches. Still, he casts his bolts of cold iron, muttering “Suck on this you bastard”. Two strike demon eyes, and two more plunge into the snarling screaming mouth and its head snaps back. “You were funnier before” it choked and then keels over. Cold iron runs from the eyes and ears, and mouth, pooling on the floor and then congeals into a chunk that rolls back to Hans. “Jah, well” he says, looking at the guttering flames flickering out on goathead’s corpse “that just shows you demons have a terrible sense of humor”

We took some time while I attended wounds and burns and were finally able to look around. The room previously occupied by the demon was lit by a flame on the ceiling, framed by a sihedron and by the somewhat baleful light we examined a small altar holding a bowl with a knife resting on its rim. Tom and I conferred and agreed (well I took his word for it), that the altar was dedicated to Abraxsis who presided over torture and obsession and related woes. Not a pleasant one to have as a patron. There didn’t seem to be anything actively malevolent, but the evil was perceptable. I suggested we cleanse the altar if we could, and so Zunk poured some holy water into the bowl and then onto the altar. There was no obvious effect, but I still felt better. There was not much else of interest, although we found a cowl that granted some spiritual insight. I had the symbol of Abraxsis woven into it, and I was against putting it on until Tom explained it would reflect the wearer’s faith. Sure enough, when I touched it, the runes on it disappeared and were replaced with the symbol of Milani. “My dad told me about these” he said by way of explanation.

We came into another room with a cold blue light emitted by another seven pointed star, and it again seemed only for decoration. Some weapons, old with the normal kind of rust sat on a shelf, and Zunk grabbed a couple of things they found interesting. The far door opened into a long corridor covered in ash. Perhaps some bones lying on the floor, pale ends sticking out. I decided not to look so closely. It led into a large empty room, and it was hard not to notice the scorch marks on floor where the corridor ended. Zunk gazed carefully around and pointed to a portion of the far wall that on inspection seemed, scorchier. Not a door, exactly, but regular and door shaped. Tom shrugs, puts his shoulder into this and it disintegrates in a cloud of ash as he stumbles into the room beyond.

I caught a glimpse of red stones in a largeish room, and perhaps a red stone altar, and then all could see was fire. Three manshapes of flame, like a burning scarecrow without the straw, rushed Tom, who swiped at one and stepped back as Zunk stepped forward to meet them.

I was some distance and even then, the heat was almost too much to bear; it must be like being in a great furnace for Tom and Zunk. Two of the flamemen closed with my friends and let off a blast of heat that blistered their skin. Tom is flaying about but the flames must be affecting him as his blade finds nothing but air. I smelled burning wood and sulfur, with overtones of undeath.

Tom is getting shredded by the two foes in front of him, and each terrible swipe left a burning gash. Hans makes a funny wavy motion with his hands, and a gout of water rushed forth dousing one. It screamed in some weird way, the roar of a great fire throttled up in pitch, as a third flameman runs into the room letting loose another burst of burning heat. Tom, perhaps seeing the effects of Hans’ water spell, pulls out a bottle of holy water and throws it at the flameman in the middle, I guess hoping to splash them all. The effect was minimal.

Zunk and Tom are holding, but just barely. Between the burning wounds and the terrible heat, they are in very dire straits. Going down in the heat and flame would be deadly for either and both are nearly there, so I do the only thing I can, praying for Milani’s guidance. I have not used power like this before and I do not know if it is enough, or even if I have it in me. But I have to try.

I channel power, squeezing it hard and casting it into the middle of Tom, Zunk and the three foes, hoping Milina’s light will heal both Tom and Zunk, while injuring the flamemen. It worked well enough, as Tom and Zunk stand taller and the flamemen scream their weird screams. It bought us some time at least.

Hans reaches out and makes the wavy motions with his hands as he incants a mystic verse. The air ripples before him and water burbles spill upon the ground in fits like an old man trying to pee. Hans quickly incants again to the same effect. His hands fall to his side as he looks glumly at the enemy in front of him and the water gushing down his trouser legs, splashing out from beneath his robes. “Jah” he mutters “that would be hilarious, were this a comedy act and not an omen of my imminent demise." Poor Hans-he is having a really bad day.

Zunk’s clever blade swipes through the flame and then again and one of the burning enemies explodes into a cloud of ash to be swept away in the updraft of its kin. Despite this small victory, we are losing. Between the terrific heat and the burning wounds, Zunk is about to die and Tom is about to die too. Tom lands a powerful blow and for a moment I think we might be all right, but the flamething only flickers but still stands. Zunk is overcome by the heat and fire, while Tom evades the strikes of the flamemen that surely would kill him, and I channel Milani’s power to make Tom nearly whole. But Zunk is still gravely injured. They are in reach at least, and I take another gamble, trusting in Milani to see me through. I have no spells to heal the little thief, but I can stitch his wounds, and I eschew caution to go for speed and maximal effect. It is harder-a single mistake will undo everything, but my hands are steady and true. I staunch the worst of their wounds. Lightning arcs from Hans and turns the wounded flameman into a cloud of black ash. We have the last one surrounded. I desperately mend Zunk again-I have to keep everyone up until we can end this last one and its killing heat. I have little left but the power in my staff, so it had better be quick, or we are finished. Tom lands a flurry of blows that greatly wounds the flameman as I use my staff and heal Zunk again. It’s now or never. As if they heard my thoughts, Zunk kills it with a blow to the chest and the layer of ash on the floor is a little thicker.

I am beyond tired, but there are things needing doing. “Oh my friends” I cry “thank Milani you all are alive. I am so sorry-those burns look awful. Sit here and rest while I mend you.
