Season 2, Episode 9: The Rise of Ripnugget

So, I guess it was back to the pit. But we thought Abstalar should at least know what we found there on our excusrsion, and that maybe he had sensed the energy too and might know something. His feelings on the devil cult were the same as both Hemlock’s and Brodert’s, which was both unhelpful and not surprising. There might be a threat there but it didn’t seem connected to horn. How could a little town like Sandpoint have so much afflicting it?

In desperation, I asked Abstalar if he knew of any relics of Xar Azmak nearby, which elicited a sharp look from Hans. “Who or what is Xar-Azmak? I have no idea what you are talking about” replied Abstalar. “Never mind” I muttered “Stupid idea. Have you noticed anything unusual in the air, maybe?”

The priest sucked in his breath when I asked if he sensed anything unusual in the air, and said “Maybe. I had a strange and disturbing dream last night about Nualia. It was not entirely natural I think”.

Oh-was this a sign? It felt absolutely urgent to visit Nualia’s grave, and I set off for it once Abstalar gave me directions, not even looking back to see if the others were following. Which I knew they were.

“Jhys”, Hans began “I know you have little use for tact, but did you really have to mention, you know who? Brodert told us to…”

I rounded on him. I was overwhelmed by all the threats, or maybe threats and threats within threats. I needed to do something, but I didn’t know what and even if I did I was scared I couldn’t do it. “Whatever Hans! Fine!” I shouted. “What did it matter anyway? He thought I was an idiot anyway. Which I probably am. If you have a better idea then let me know.”

“Well,jah, Jhys, you didn’t mention the horn, which was good,. Well done, jah?. But it’s the horn of Xar-Azmak, so there’s the inferred meaning, which might..”

I’m afraid I might have blacked out for a moment, and then I was standing at Nualia’s grave. I took a breath and calm now, asked for peace to focus.

There was something wrong here, but the flavor was different than the energy directed at the horn. A sense of woe and destruction and fate. Diffuse. No source-from everywhere and nowhere, infusing the grave or reflecting into the grave from the grave itself, which didn’t make any sense but that’s what I felt. Still, it was different from the horn. Great-another woe to carry. As if we didn’t have enough.

“So” says Hans, when I flutter my eyes open. “Did you discover anything useful worth the risk”?

It was too much. “Useful? Useful? Well yeah. There’s something wrong here; something bad has happened or will happen or is happening now and I don’t know where it is or what it is, but it feels awful and it’s not the FUCKING horn. Is that useful enough? I don’t know. Who cares”?

Hans sighed “Jhys, I’m not judging you, I’m just trying to understand”

I sighed. “Yes, Hans, I know, I’m sorry. It’s just I don’t know what I am doing here. I mean, Tom’s the son of a hero, Zunk is connected to Ripnugget, you’re the son of a magician. My Da was a cabinet maker.”

“Actually, Jhys, my father did not use magic. He was a scientist using magically enabled techniques to manipulate arcane energies through the use of technology. It’s not really the same at all.”

It was a measure of how defeated I felt that I let the Hansplaining slide right by. “Okay Hans, but that’s not really my point. It’s that you all belong in this. Here. But, if the Fates picked me for whatever this is, they made a really big mistake. I don’t belong here-I’ll just screw everything up.”

Hans to his credit, did not immediately reply. Tom drew in a breath, and here it comes. The “Jhys is being silly” argument.

That’s not at all what he said, which to be fair, I should have expected. “I hear you. Believe me I understand” were his first words, and then “We all feel like that sometimes, Jhys. I mean, Zunk just put down his grandfather. Pretty sure he’s been wondering why that fell to him. And yes, my dad is a great man. But take it from me, that doesn’t really help. Do you think I don’t worry that I will not be as strong as Viktor, or that I don’t fear disappointing him? We all worry we’re in the wrong place. But you belong here every bit as much as we do. Or you know, if this isn’t some cursed divine plan, we’re glad you have our backs. Our lives are in your hands every time we battle, and well, we’re still kicking”.

I hadn’t considered that everyone else could find reasons to doubt themselves too, and if someone like Tom could feel these things, maybe what I was feeling was not so unusual. I owed to my friends to honor their trust in me and not let the strangeness of this all get in the way.

I nodded. “Okay. Thanks Thomas. I’m fine, or at least good enough for now. So, I guess we go back the pit”?

“Seems the best bet, so yes. But after a little shopping; some healing draughts might be wise, just in case.”h

We headed to the shop where Tom bought his rope belt (I still haven’t asked him about this) and Vauxhall Voss welcomed us into his shop. “I’ve been hoping you’d come by. Wait here, I have something to show you” and he went into a back room to return with a little wooden box. He opened it up to real a small metal bird body; wings and chest but lacking the other bits to which a body would normally be connected. “Somes such as yourselves sold this to me a while back. One of the few who challenged the pit and made it out alive. It’s a mechanical thing of some sort, but it’s missing pieces. I hear you’ve been to the pit, and just in case you are crazy enough to go back, I’d pay you 20 sovereigns for each part you find. Now-what was it you wanted?”

Even with the stop, we made it to the pit well before sunset, avoiding a climb up the difficult path in the dark. We descended the ladder, and stopped just outside the tunnel to the goblin’s trap room and Zunk whistled as he was taught. Nothing. After awhile and another whistle, Zunk crept forward to scout. Something was most definitely wrong and it was best to take it slow. Zunk carefully opened the door and I heard an indrawn breath.

“Oiy, this don’t look good at all. There’s a couple of goblin corpses here all burnt and slashed, and dog corpses hanging from the roof. It was a massacre. Come through while I look inside. Don’t want to take it on faith there ain’t more traps here,”

We crowded into the tunnel before the door as Zunk gingerly walked in, considering carefully his every step. “Look here-this is a slippery patch, and don’t be touching whatever this thing is-gotta be some sort of trip cord”. Zunk was pointing out a leathery-ropey thing stretched across the room around 3 inches off the floor, attached to the walls on each side.

Zunk couldn’t make out the mechanism, but nobody trusted it and the sneaky slippery spot and so Tom offered to use his pole arm to cut the cord from afar, hoping we’d be well out of the way of whatever happened. Which was nothing. The two ends fell to the floor was all.

It seemed silly to be halted by two leather cords on the ground (well, dog skin as it turned out, ick) so Zunk carefully avoided the slippery floor and stepped over. Immediately the cords snapped back from the wall towards the center, but got caught up in the free cut ends, leaving a tangled mess on the floor. Likely it would have ensnared, or worse, anyone who stepped over, had it been whole.

Zunk nodded, as if appreciating some clever artistry, and moved, still cautiously, around the scarecrow horse (somewhat burnt) to one of the dogs. “Looks like they skinned it and stuffed it, like with these spiders. And I can hear something ticking inside, which I don’t like at all, so I’m going to come back”.

We talked it over and agreed distance was our friend and settled on Hans using his lightning. The ticking suggested a clockwork thing, and those did not like lightning at all. Sure enough, they exploded when Hans zapped them, leaving globs of smoking green goo on the floor. Clearly not safe to walk through so Tom used his pole arm to, well, skewer the dead goblins and use them to swab the floor to create a path.

Zunk moved around to one side of the horse and Tom the other. I saw Zunk frown and shiver and he pointed “Don’t step there” he said so I didn’t and went around to Tom’s side, stepping only in places where Tom had. Bad energy came from the spider dolls and I was sure being any closer to them would have been dangerous. It took us awhile but we assembled behind the horse on each side of the door to the cavern where we had left Tunch. Tom pushed the door open.

Tunch was there, but chained and more than half dead, his demented pet snarling nearby also badly beaten. Goblin bodies were piled into low barricades here and there, with others lying about amid more dog corpses. It was carnage. At the far end of the room stood Ripnugget, the bastard we had killed, looking no worse for wear, along with his lizard. What the flock? How was this even possible?

“Greetings kinslayer Grandson. Time for me to return the favor you gave me. Attack!” he yelled “Kill them all” Some bitty winged fey we hadn’t noticed chattered the teeth and hissed.

Zunk wasted no time, running in to free Tunch, who could barely stand. The weasel lashed about wildly, clearly out of it’s mind and landed a blow. I tried to club it and flailed (of course) as Tom stepped up and ended weasel, while Hans sent a wall of water that knocked a pile of corpses back into Ripnugget and the lizard. Zunk slashed one of the fey, killing it.

Then Ripnugget spoke some words and several of the dead goblins began to rise.

Fish and Fiddlesticks!

In the end, it was more irritating than anything else. (Should I be worried mortal combat now only rises to “irritating”? Maybe, but I had other things to worry about, like figuring out what Ripnugget had planned, and why Nulia could not rest, and what the cult was up to, and whether farmers were in danger, and the fate of the baker woman’s daughter. Yeah, stuff like that).

I channeled Milani’s gift into a bright burst, healing my friends (and the fey and the wild weasel, but no matter), and also harming the zombies. One of them let loose a terrible moan that shook us but affected its friends as well. It tried to rise, so Tom struck it down. The good news was that the screaming stopped, the bad news was the zombie screamer exploded peppering us with bony splinters, but again, also affecting our foes. Tunch got his pet under control while Hans conjured a burst of wind that kills one of them near me and it explodes, but Milani protected me and the shards whiz by, although my foes are not so lucky.

As long as they don’t blow up at once I feel confident I can keep everyone safe. Hans ‘conjured wind is harrying the zombies and slowing them down, while Zunk sends arrows zipping toward Ripnugget, who conjures his lizard mount.

Nhay Nhay Nhay some of the fey taunt at the weasel, and amazingly, this enrages the stupid feral beast sending it out of control again, while the others stick their tongue out and poke Hans with little daggers.

I kill another zombie (it seems they are made mainly to explode), and another grabbed the last remaining member of the Whistletop tribe, letting one of the treacherous fey kill him with its dagger to end the inglorious and brief reign of Tunch. Hans killed one of the zombies and I got hammered with body parts, which also killed weasel. The damage from the meat bombs was starting to add up, and then one of them clawed me hard and I was grabbed. I was pretty bloody by this point, more than “pretty” if I was being honest, but I was alright. Probably. Unless someone killed the zombie. “Don’t worry, I’m fine” I yelled, sort of optimistically. I noticed Ripnugget fleeing on the back of his lizard without a second look, laughing like a maniac.

Zunk stabbed down the fey that killed Tunch as I called Milani’s light down upon me and unprised myself from the potential explosion. Moving away from the zombie I said “Thomas-please feel free to fuck that..erm..guy up” Tom speared the zombie from afar and it blew up harmlessly. The last fey squeaked “Fuck me” and flew away.

What is going on here? Did Ripnugget come back just to kill the Whistleteeth? He must have other plans. I looked around the room hoping to discover, well anything. It was hard to look past the carnage but nothing presented itself. There was a charred hole I did not recall from earlier, and it radiated bad intent, but I could tell whatever happened was fading, the energy used up. Huh.

We debated for a moment. Hans was of the mind we should just head for the exit and catch Ripnugget escaping, but we did not know that’s where he was going and the risk of not getting more information was too great. We still didn’t know what triggered the horn. Unless. Huh.

In the end we decided to follow (although not without some residual grumping from Hans) and set off in the direction RIpnugget had went. The path led to a sunken cavern and Zunk snuck down in case it was an ambush. But Ripnugget was gone, But it was still sort of an ambush. After creeping forward out of sight, Zunk came hurriedly back. “Big lizard thing back there. It’s sort of in a bad way, but it’s still pretty uh..see what I mean?” Zunk quickly climbed back up to us, and yes, we could see what he meant as the beast rounded the corner into view.

It was a rather large and fierce looking lizard, easily bull sized, with a long snout full of sharp teeth, a spikey back, and a nasty looking barbed tail. As scary as it was, it was indeed in bad shape; scored everywhere with cuts and slashes and punctures, one eye milked over and scales dull. I remembered it was called a hodag, and it was not a brute-supposed to be at least as smart as a horse. I felt sad for it and thought it must be really angry at whomever abused it, likely the goblins.

I put on my most cheerful expression (I hoped-not sure what would be cheerful to a hodag) and my best voice. “Uh, hi. We’re not here to hurt you. We just need to go in that direction” as I pointed to some barely visible stairs in the back of the chamber. Its head swiveled towards me, favoring the side with the clear eye. “Uhh, are you hungry?” Zunk and Tom had conspired together to drag dead weasel to the edge of the pit where Tom hoisted the corpse up and threw it in. “Look, food. Eat, we won’t bother you”. Keeping its eye on me the whole time, the hodag sniffed around the dead animal and chuffed a couple of times, and sat down on its haunches in front of the carcass warily. It was smart enough to not get distracted, so maybe it would realize we would not hurt it.

“I’m going to come down” I said loudly (as if that would help) “so don’t get all worked up. Easy now, OK? and I climbed down slowly. It took some time. Even after I healed it twice with my wand, I did not feel comfortable moving, and I had sat down. I pointed towards the stairs. “I’m going to go that way. Uhmm,,stay. I won’t hurt you” I hoped it was smart enough to realize I had healed it. It stayed put as I gingerly moved past until I was able to see the stairs seemed safe, and going back as carefully as I came, whispered to the others to come down. Everyone was careful and calm, and while the hodag’s back spikes rose, and the tail lashed, it remained in place. Zunk managed to creep past out of sight, which probably was for the best.

“Thanks for not killing the hodag,” I whispered to the others “I think it was just scared, the poor thing.”

“Jah, well, this is a waste of time anyway, so why make it worse?”.

We followed the stairs to a doorway leading to a chamber so big we could not see the walls, especially as thick spider web strands were strewn everywhere. Except that is, for the trail burnt through the webs by Ripnugget. We had gone maybe 30 feet when a big spidery shape appeared out of the obscuring webs, 4 unblinking eyes regarding us.

“Whhhhat are you pink fleshhhies doing heeeeere? Issssssss not the burning scaly thing enoughhhhhhhhhhh?”

Aye, well about that” says Zunk “that’s no friend of ours and we’d be happy to get rid of him for both our sakes if ya let us pass”

“Perhapsssss I will not. I am wondering why I sssssssssssshould not eat you? We are hungry and need flesssssh”

Why is it always about bodies here? Zunk and Hans negotiated safe passage in return for the corpses in the carnage room, and even got our hosts to reveal the location of a door to that room that bypassed the hodag. Trapped of course, according to Zunk, bur we retrieved the corpse of Tunch and the fey and the spiders were satisfied. We left to the sounds of crunching, sucking and gurgling. Two of the spiders were alarmingly large. Another battle we were fortunate to avoid, but for different reasons than the hodag.

The burnt path through the webs finally ended in a tunnel that arced around for some distance, emerging in another cave with rubble piles on the floor, and strewn with bent spoons. A series of small tunnels and one larger one lead out; the little ones (only Zunk and Hans could hope to pass through) looked recently dug, which seemed to explain the spoons. A shiny sword was stuck upright in the largest rubble pile; of magical nature and rather valuable. I guessed Ripnugget was in too much of a hurry to grab it, and so it was ours.

I could barely pick out a set of tracks to one of the small tunnels and Zunk and Hans cautiously went through. It was the worst news possible! I could not imagine a more horrific outcome! Hans was right; the tunnels lead outside to the pit walls. Ripnugget was gone.

Hans sauntered back in, saying “Well, Ripnugget escaped out of the pit-who would have predicted he’d do this, jah”?

Sigh. Better to get it over with. “Yes, Hans, you were right. We should have done what you said.”

“Jah, well, of course, but that wasn’t really my point, which was…”

“Just. Take the win, Hans.”

“Okay, jah, but it wasn’t a total waste of time, that sword is pretty valuable.”

Having established our chase was futile, we headed up the large northern passage that returned us to the spider’s lair, where Zunk found the door leading back to the carnage room without alerting the spiders, which was just as well.

We trudged back to Sandpoint in the dark, having accomplished nothing. Hans being right continued to eat at me. It wasn’t Hans’ fault-he was not ungracious about it, it was just me. Maybe I was feeling guilty that we had nothing to show for our efforts. But then I thought that even if we had gone as Hans had suggested, the tunnel we found was nowhere near where we would have come out, and we probably would have just watched helplessly as Ripnugget’s lizard scaled the pit walls out of our reach. And then we wouldn’t have healed the hodag and found the sword. So it was worth it? Hans would say it was crazy (Tom would have shrugged) and he was probably right, but I still felt better.

At least nothing had gotten worse when we got back to town. Even though it was late we went to Brodert’s to check on the horn, and he reported it had quieted down, and the threat was over. I thought of the charred hole in the floor of the carnage room, and it seemed too coincidental even though I had no idea what the connection was, how to stop it and whether it would happen again. Great. That only left the cult, the Sandport devil, Nualia’s restless spirit, and the baker’s daughter to worry about.

Just peachy. Time to rest. Things would make more sense in the morning. Probably.
