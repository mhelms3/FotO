Season 2, Episode 8: The Rust Awakens

Zunk was quieter than normal on the way back, and Hans finally got him to talk. “Well, I guess killing my Gran was kind of strange. Not that I feel bad for what I done-he was a right bastard, but it’s just weird. I mean, the whole thing with the Runelord or whatever she was, and being caught up in what feels really big and beyond me. I’m wondering about it all and well, what’s really going on here?”

“Ah Hans” said “ Who can figure out anything of the gods, or whatever plans they might or might not have? Suppose you didn’t know if he was your grandfather, it would have probably happened the same way. Jah? And then you wouldn’t even be thinking about any of this. You just need a good breakfast-how about we hit Sandpoint Savories”

“Maybe. I mean yes for food, maybe on the other. I just got to get my head around this, you know?” Yes, I was pretty sure I did as I was thinking some of the same thoughts.

Alana was behind the counter as usual and greeted us warmly as “my favorite customers”; seems she had moved past my blunder about her daughter. She also was quite concerned something bad would happen to us (well, it did, but we didn’t offer details) and surprisingly, brought up her daughter. She was plainly worried, having not seen her for some time after she returned from the pit, and didn’t think too kindly about the company she kept. She didn’t trust “those adventurers from Magnamar”, We asked what we could do, and she just wanted us to keep an eye out. Of course, we said, although there didn’t seem to be any place around here she might be and wasn’t clear what we could do without at least going to Magnamar ourselves. Still, offering to help at least made her feel better, which was a start. We got a bunch of goodies for Hemlock and his crew, went to the Sherrif’s office.

The guardsmen knew enough at this point (Hemlock thought we were useful, we had treats) to let us through, and we asked Hemlock whether the rangers had reported back.

“Yep, came in around dawn, with not much to show other than some farmers whining about weird stuff. Country folk, you know? Everything’s always pointing to this devil or that beast and none of it real. Some bumpkin found a funny spot on their roof, and they got the idea it was made by a burning hoof and how the Sandpoint Devil’s back looking to set fire to everything. “ He rolled his eyes and grumbled. “Not like I ain’t got enough to do, especially with your lot stirring up trouble and this whole Ripnugget thing”

“Aye well” says Zunk “ You don’t need to worry about that anymore.”

“What?” Hemlock managed to get out around a mouthful of pastry “Tell me”

We did, or rather Zunk and Hans did, while I nibbled on a mushroom hand pie and Tom looked around. “Well, excellent!” says the sheriff “now I can at least cross this off my list. Ripnugget dead. Check. Don’t suppose you want to go to a meeting of the zoning board, do you.?” Even Hans didn’t think that seemed attractive.

“Excuse me Sherrif” says Tom “but I couldn’t help notice your men are quite busy with their preparations-is there anything going on? Are they getting ready for something.”

“Nahh” offered Hemlock “If anything it’s the opposite. I mean, even with this Ripnugget thing it’s been pretty quiet and I think the boys got lax. The spit and polish is missing and it seems they have to be reminded of their duties. So I got ‘em spitting and polishing is all. Anyway. I have lunch, uhm..lunch meeting. With the Mayor. Soon. So unless there’s anything else you can tell me that will shorten my list some more I gotta run. Nice seeing you.”

We took the hint and made for the door. Tom popped in the guardroom where the men had laid out their weapons and armor and were tending to them vigorously. “Looking good men” offered Tom “Hemlock got you on your paces, huh? Well, he’s right; take care that gear and it’ll take care of you.”

One of the men looked up. “Well, the Sherriff ain’t wrong, sir, but the thing is, I do take care of my kit. We all do. And we ain’t been doing anything but walking the walls and the docks and whatever. I gave everything the once over a couple days ago and it ain’t supposed to be looking this bad after so short a time. So we didn’t even notice. I dunno, maybe some bad gear oil, or the air more salty or shit. Whatever it was we all got a tongue lashing for sure.”

“Uh, you missed a spot there” I observed, trying to be helpful.

We looked at each other as we walked out, and I was pretty sure we all were thinking the same thing. Rust-not good. Maybe Brodert had some idea what was going on and we needed to give him the scrolls and books we found in the pit anyway.

Brodert didn’t answer the door, which struck me as unusual, and his windows had all been papered up, which seemed even stranger. Why would he do this? Zunk thought he smelled something like pickles? Fortunately we had an expert in our midst. “That’s the acid smell from the vinegar” Phryllis said “it’s where the tang comes from, but the real secret is the spices. I bet you know all about spicey pickles”. One eye on Zunk, the other on Tom.

“Well, ahem” cut in Hans, trying to redirect “maybe Brodert is at the Goblin, or the Rune Keepers, perhaps we should try there.”

I shook my head “This is weird Hans, I don’t like it and am worried Brodert is in trouble. Zunk, pick the lock. If it’s nothing, we’ll apologize. Brodert will understand”

Zunk nodded and moved to the door getting out his tool. “Aye, have a look at this” he murmured. We all peered at the lock, which was as rusty as a piece of naked iron after months on the sea. Definitely not good.

Zunk picked the lock, but basically it was so rusty he broke it, and Tom opened the door. He had to set his shoulder to it and when it opened, the scent of vinegar was so strong it made our eyes water. We heard chunks and hisses and thunks down the hall. The reeking cloud was diluted enough by the fresh air that we could enter without choking and I strode down the hall towards the sound to see Brodert in heavy work clothes and wearing strange head gear that covered his nose and mouth. He was hunched over a great beast of metal and glass and tubes and dials, furiously adjusting. Pipes lead down the hall into the room holding the horn. Yikes. “Brodert” I yelled “What is going on here?”

“You” he yelled back as he tore off his head gear “Thank the gods you are here. Yes, zunk, close that door and make sure you press the wax seal back in place. It’s the best I could think of. Don’t worry, it may sting but it’s not harmful. We just need to get the pressure feedback right and tighten the coupling, but the kinetic capacitance is off and the planar resonance tuning is all fucked. Gods be damned, I didn’t have time or the right equipment-it’s a mess but it’s all I have” he wailed.

“You’re absolutely right-it’s a mess” Phryllis hissed “These are going to be the worst pickles of all time”.

“Pickles?” shot back Brodert. “Is that what you think is ..? No, it’s the Horn you scaly pea brain. Something activated it and I’m trying to dampen its effect before it rusts all the metal in the sodding town. The distillate here is being pumped into the chamber but the absorbance spectrum is wrong and the oxidative fluxons are increasing. I don’t know how long it will hold. I don’t even know why it woke up, but it’s been emanating since last night. Started a few hours before midnight”

Something activated the horn last evening? Uh-oh-that can’t be a coincidence.

“Hans grab a wrench and the sealing tape there and patch up the leaks-gotta get the pressure up. That bimorphic copper might help if you smear it on the tape. ”

Well, my fumbly fingers would not be helpful, but I wondered if I could figure out what set the horn off, so I went to the chamber door and focused. I could feel a rising and a falling energy, like the crashing of waves or the beating of a heart. No, maybe two rhythms-one projecting from somewhere and the other, lesser one centered inside the chamber. Call and response. But I couldn’t tell where the call was emanating from, Maybe if I could find another object that was affected, I could narrow things down.

But even if I didn’t know where the energy came from, I thought I might be able to dampen the effects like I did the door in the catacombs. It might buy Brodert some time. “Guys, I’m going to try something but it might take some time. Please don’t interrupt”

“Understood” says Tom “I remember something my Dad told me about how to counteract rust, so Zunk and I are going shopping. We’ll be back soon-do what you can”

I nodded and focused again on the door to have an anchor and reached out carefully with my power. Ahh-there-I have you. I began to chant blanking my mind to all but the energy affecting the horn, and used my own energy as a counter, like a breakwater diffusing a wave, deflecting it inward leaving the beach beside, calm. Slowly slowly I could feel the counter rhythm of the horn still. I wasn’t sure how long it had been, but I heard Zunk murmer “Easy girl, take a beat, Tom is going to try something”

I gradually ended my chant, becoming aware of my surroundings bit by bit. My hair was plastered to my forehead and my eyes were gritty. I realized I was looking at Tom adding ingredients to a giant glass vessel filled with a straw colored liquid, likely vinegar. Phrylls was perched on the table, staring intensely with nostrils flareing. I had no idea what she was doing. “No!” she suddenly barked “Tom, that’s enough peatmoss-let it combine, don’t jiggle so hard-its like a baby. See the blue tinge-good, good”

As I looked, the blue streamers appeared and spread with the gentle swirling from Tom, until the entire contents of the flask became a brilliant transparent saphire. Tom and Phryllis nodded at each other and Tom brought the flask over to Brodert, who opening up a port on the big copper chamber, carefully poured it in. “Good, good” he murmured “the temperature damping is not bad. Okay..here it goes”. Blue tinged vapor shot up the glass coils and into the copper tubing leading to the chamber. Brodert ran to the other side of the room, picking up something with spikes and a dial, staring hard. “oh..oh…the fluxons are realigning and it looks like the horn has stabilized. From the decay rate I think it will hold like this for a few days. Thank the gods you came when you did-I don’t know what would have happened without you, And, uhm, sorry about calling you a pea brain Phryllis-I was a little anxious about the entire town turning into a rust pile.”

There didn’t seem much else we could do about the horn at the moment, so Hans brought Brodert up to date on what had transpired in the pit. The scholar was dismissive of the devil, but offered that cults surrounding that legend have popped up from time to time to cause trouble. It seemed unlikely that whatever cult was in the pit was connected to what was awakening the horn, leaving us nowhere.

I said that, if we could find another target of the energy, I might be able to locate the source and we agreed the Hellstorm stones in the catacombs were an object of power that might also be affected. We decided to take a look, and ventured into the catacombs again. The guards knew enough to let us pass. The Rune plonkers studying the Hellstorm flume machinery had not noticed any activity, and sure enough, I could detect nothing. Dead end.
