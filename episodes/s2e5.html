Season 2, Episode 5: Pickles Pits and Hellstorm Flumes

We made sure the two priests got escorted, half carried, to their chambers. They had enough energy to ask us what happened, and we gave them the short version covering the mob, its dissolution in desultory shame and embarrassment and confusion, the role of Scarnazzi, the exchange with Nualia and her restored left arm. They seemed relieved and satisfied, but maybe it was because they still were alive. We told them we probably were going to venture down to the catacombs, and not to worry; we would check back later.

We were just leaving when we heard a terrible shriek and a hiss; weapons at the ready we looked around for whatever ghastly new threat this was. It came from out of a darkened corner and it was…just a lizard. A striking one, to be sure, with shiny green scales and a bright red ruff, but just a lizard, if a largish one. We watched, relieved and amused by our jumpiness as it scampered back and forth, sped in circles as if trying to catch it’s tail, and did a little dance, raising each foreleg and tilting its head, first left then right. How cute. Suddenly it stopped and seemed to look at us. Then it happened.

It shrieked again, but this time there were..words. “What has happened? Where is my beautiful hair and my soft skin? The last thing I recall is opening up the shop, and then fleeing from the dragon-fire all around. The pickle barrels exploded and the window shattered, and... Am I dead? Why am I a ..LIZARD?”

We looked at each other, speechless, all except Hans, that is. Naturally.

“Ah, are you by chance, Phryllis Diller, of Diller’s Dill Delights and Pickles”?

Head bob, Could a lizard cry?

“Well, jah, the good news you are not dead! But erm, the bad news is yes, you are a lizard.”

“You have a beautiful ruff” I said helpfully and Tom nodded.

“You think so, tall dark and handsome” ? It was odd to hear feminine wheedling from a lizard. Phryllis batted her big green eyes at Tom. I think she was having trouble getting her mind around her present state. Holy smokes, what is going on?

“I think, Frau Diller, you have been somewhat unfortunately swept up into a ritual intended to call someone else from beyond the veil. I, well, many memories passed through my head when I was entranced in the working, and I might have recalled your shop and the delicious sweet sour early pickles, with the onions.”

“Those were my favorite too..”

“Well, jah, but the thing is Frau Diller, erm, may I call you Phryllis? Jah, well Phryllis, I think the memory triggered something in the mind storm and it being so powerful, transubstantiated you, erm, into this..body, Jah? Probably you went in to whatever was around, jah. Good thing there was something other than roaches, jah?”. I think he was trying to be helpful. “So, I am sorry but this, well, it could be worse, jah?” he finished, gamely.

Phryllis stomped up to Hans and peered up, ruff flaring. It was cutely menacing. “This is YOUR fault! Yes, my pickles are the taste of heaven, and that was a good batch if I recall, but this came from YOUR head. What am I to do now, like this? Well, I will tell you! You will take care of me, and fix this. Me. “

Hans sighed. “Yes, we will have some agreement, but it is not what you are suggesting. We are bonded in some way now, and our fates entwined. I might be able to reverse this in time, but not now. In the meantime, we must help each other. Come, let us discuss this.”

“Fine. Not like I have a choice” hissed Phryllis, as she scampered up Han’s robes and perched on his shoulder. “Bye tall dark and handsome” she said to Tom.

Hans sighed and turned to leave. “What do you eat anyway?” he asked as he headed back to the Dragon. “I might have some mushroom risotto from the other night.”

Well, that’s one way to get a girlfriend.

We headed to Brodert’s in the morning, after an enjoyable meal of egg battered toast with maple syrup; it was nice to eat something normal. We could barely keep up with striding Tom, who wanted to see the blacksmith so was eager to get at it. Nobody said anything about Phryllis being perched on Han’s shoulder. She seemed less talkative but clearly the two had come to some sort of arrangement. She felt well enough to make suggestive comments to Tom, who took them well, all things considered.

Brodert took one look at us and said “Let me offer you some tea” and led us inside. There was little he could tell us of The Pit or why there was an ancient Thassalonian complex beneath the streets. “It’s an old town” was all he said; I was getting tired of hearing that, to be honest. I thought the pit might be the jumbled ruins just a stones throw from his house, but Brodert thought it unlikely. I have never heard it referred to as thar before-only the light house. “But I cannot recall half the things I have read these days”, he said “ so you are welcome to check my library. Perhaps there is something in the town’s history about that, and the catacombs as well. “

“About Scarnazzi, well, all I can say is they are one of the original families of Sandport and have always been powerful here. I wonder if they really cared about the ritual, more likely they were trying to make trouble for others. There seems to be some disagreement about continuing an old pact. The elders have long leased this land from the original claimants-it is the cost of doing business some of which they pass to us, but the agreement is expiring soon, some of the families are against paying.”

“That sounds typical; powerful Scarnazzi family shaking down the workers” I mutter.

“Oh no-just the opposite” replied Brodert “the Scarnazzi are definitely against it, but I’m not sure where the others lie”

Well, that was unexpected. Anyway, Hans and I stayed at Brodert’s to raid the library, while Tom and Zunk went to the blacksmith to transact some business.

We dug into the books, Phryllis occasionally scampering off Han’s shoulder to pick up a volume and bring it to him. “Ah, look” he said “something about the pit. From this entry, It appears to be a mysterious open shaft in the heart of the rocky escarpment known as Devil’s Platter; reachable by trail about 2 miles to the southeast of Sandpoint.”

“That’s great Hans, I didn’t find anything about the catacombs exactly, but I did find out something about the lighthouse. Here, look at this:” For many years, the ruined tower known as the Old Light was thought to have been an ancient collapsed lighthouse, but now that more has been learned about ancient Thassilon, the Old Light has been revealed to have once been known as the Hellstorm Flume, a powerful weapon used by Runelord Alaznist to protect her nation’s eastern border with that of Runelord Karzoug’s nation of Shalast. Despite its name, the Hellstorm Flume had no direct association with Hell itself—it was so called for its ability to emit a flume of searing fire at extraordinary range to blast approaching armies with a devastating inferno. “Cool huh? But I don’t know how this helps”

“Jah, well, unravelling the mysteries of the ancients does not come overnight. Haven’t you told me yourself it took Stan, well, a long time to bring the others back? We must be as diligent! Perhaps we will learn more in the underneath. Let us rejoin the others”

We met the others back at the church and prepared to enter the catacombs. Tom had purchased a scroll he thought might be useful, and reported that the events of last night were in the rumor mill, but not tied to us, at least at the moment. Tom’s purchase reminded me a scroll to resist fire might be handy as well, and I was able to get one from the temple, for a small and reasonable donation. Then, in we went.

We started to examine some of the rooms in the corridor that lead to the place with the stones, finding one that contained a desk with scrolls and books. They were ancient and crumbled to dust at the lightest touch. But there was a map on the wall I could actually read so I tried to make some sense out of it as Hans and the others were trying to salvage the scrolls and books. The map was very old with no landmarks I knew (not that I knew much) but still looked familiar.

“Hey Hans, look at this. I think it might be a map of this areas a long time ago. See the coast? It sort of looks like what’s west of Sandpoint, which should be..right around here? I can’t read this writing though-I think its Thassalonian”

“Jah, Jhys, I think you are right. It does look like this area. The writing says HF complex”

We looked at each other, and then at the same time said Hellstorm Flume complex! So that’s what this is-something that was connected to that ancient weapon. “See Jhys, patience and persistence does the trick” From Hans’ shoulder, the lizard said “And Phryllis-she does tricks too” while looking directly at Tom.

“Jah, well anyway, if HF is Sandpoint, then right down there ought to be the Pit. The map showed a red circle very near the spot Hans had referred to, with a note along the margins. “Captured spies whisper of a sizable Shalast cohort stationed here, led by someone named Liralarue—must secure authorization from Mistress for a raid.” Well, okey dokey then. Going to an old hideout of a bunch of Kharzoug’s troops to look for some terrible plot started 1000 years ago. What could go wrong?Anyway, that’s a problem for another time.

The map was made of better material than the scrolls, so we carefully rolled it up and stashed it. Maybe Hans could use it to impress the rude plonkers at the mage academy.

A door in the corner led to a storeroom, where we found a parchment and a key ring. The parchment described some strange trap with doors and baskets that needed to be filled with gems of a certain value. The writer took great pains to reveal that some insolent soldiers were being led to their deaths by the wrong advice, although they also noted the correct placement of gems. Not that it mattered. How could we possibly encounter this little piece of nasty work?

Well, down the hall, as it turned out. Tom opened the next door down not noticing the basket and entered a room with doors on the north wall. They also they had baskets, and quickly noting the lack of any gems he returned to us and shrugged.

Well, now how were to find these gems, or beads as they were referred to in those wicked plans?

“Jah” starts Hans “I….OW! Phryllis would you be so kind as to not be digging in with your claws so much? Thank you. Now, what was it I was going to say? Ach. Nevermind,” Hans sighed.

We went to the last door in the corridor and opend it and were promptly set upon by a stony winged thing with a long snout, horns and a rather big ax. I grant my companions the blessing of Milani as long-snout gets blasted by magic from Hans. Tom trips it, and Zunk flips their knife downward, first gashing a large wound down the thigh. It twisted and Zunk’s second strike punched through the thing’s now exposed chest. I looked at its lifeless horned head and reptilian snout-another demon perhaps. It worried me I should have been more worried.

“Hey Zunk: I said as we looked around the otherwise empty room “does that look like a door to you”? and pointed. “Aye it does girl” as he approached and peered at it closely “good one!” He carefully revealed the outlines.

Zunk fiddled with it for a moment, pronounced it safe and invited Tom to open it. A small long room on the other side, with what looked like a green stone…egg..resting on the floor. Large egg, bigger long than Hans or Zunk was tall. (Not that I am judging). Stoney tentacles emerged from its side and curled up the walls. A bloodless sea creature in its underwater death trap? A stoney spider about to come to life? I did not like it at all, and refused to go in.

I heard Hans and Zunk muttering and then a rolling sliding noise and I peaked back inside. Somehow, they had managed to open the egg, the sides now expanded. It was not an egg; it was a coffin. Inside were the bones of some humanoid form, curled in fetal position. I could see a flash of green in the hollow chest. Some stoney hearted monster ready to be woken? That was not a thing that should have been opened, or further played with, but naturally the boys were scheming how to get the green thing out in a safe way. As if. But they settled on Hans using a spell to carefully remove the object without disturbing anything, and he extracted a small delicately carved gemstone snake. I could sense some sort of magic, but not the type that would be likely to cause us harm. Hans inspected it further and pronounced it to be able to transubstantiate into a real snake of useful size, ferocity and loyalty. It was decided Zunk would take it.

There was no place to go expect the room with the baskets on the doors. The obvious trap was worrisome but Tom pointed out he had been in the room before and nothing had happened; whatever nastiness there was no longer functioning after all this time. That made perfect sense; it was also completely wrong.

Tom entered the room again, this time inspecting it more carefully for some scrap of knowledge, but there was none. Only a fiery explosion that torched the others, Tom somewhat badly. I tended their burns in the hallway as we talked it over.

“Oh, I recall what I was going to say”! cried Hans. “I really should have realized this earlier, but I was, distracted, by some unfortunate… Ow! Anyway, please show me the gems we got from the room, if you would not mind, Zunk? That hand looks healed. They were with the knife and the bowl. Ah yes-it is good we did not sell these, jah? The values match the requirements of these baskets, although we lack the most valuable mentioned for the statue, but progress! Better late than never.” Mhmmmm, I thought as I tended Tom.

Hans arranged the gems in piles as I worked. “This one in the first, these in the second and so on, should neuter the trap. When of course, we all are ready”

After a time we were and did as Hans instructed. Nothing happened, which we took to mean Hans had puzzled it out. So yes, progress. We opened one of the doors on the north wall and looked at a small room with a statue inside of it of a splendid woman, holding a real and deadly looking pole arm. Her other hand was open, as if she expected something to be placed in it. There was nothing else in the room but a door to the right of us-the one we did not open.

“Ah, well, it looks like we need the last gem to complete things” said Hans, quite reasonably. He was also completely wrong. The statue moved.

I immediately drew on Milani’s power to ward my armor. We were more or less standing right in front of it, and hopefully it would attack me. I took a swing at it to give it a reason but missed. Fortunately, all of the others were more successful, and they harassed it with magic bolts and steel. It wounded Tom badly, but I was ready and made him whole almost immediately. Zunk killed it with gutting strike before Tom had a chance to take his revenge.

Okey dokey then. We couldn’t find anything else, although the gleaming pole arm was valuable, so we left with it and the gems too. I was unsatisfied, even though we had cleansed the place of some very not nice things.

“Well, this is all well and good, but I am not sure what we have accomplished”. I said “I mean, Hans might be able to get in good with the Rune readers or whatever they are called, but we still don’t have the artifact”

You keep talking about an artifact, Jhys, but what is that exactly?” asked Zunk “I mean, how do we know what we are looking for? Like the Horn or something?”

“Ah well” began the Hansplaining. “It is quite simple, really. An artifact is an object of ancient and great power. To be an artifact is to be something legendary and mysterious. Like the knife Promqeth wielded that came from the well, jah? But of course and on the other hand, an artifact needn’t be so conveniently packaged, it could be a..”

Hans had stopped. “Zunk” he cried “that was a most perceptive question to which I think I might have an answer. I believe we have already found what we are looking for-the red stone room. Those stones are the ancient workings of the Hellstorm Flume-here, in the Hellstorm Flume complex!

Fish and fiddlesticks! That seemed exactly right-according to the map this place was connected to the Flume, and those stones were clearly mysterious and ancient. It did not take much to imagine mucking with them might be dangerous-clearly the Rune lords built things to last. I thought of the basket room.

“That was so brilliant Hans! I cried “Those stuffed shirt mages have to take you seriously now. They will look so stupid when you tell them all this was right underneath them and you figured it out.”

We had reached the cave at the beginning, and Zorph had appeared out of somewhere to stand before us. “Oh..hi again. Its us, we were back a bit but we’re leaving now”

It seemed his friend Woogie wanted to meet us, which was sweet. But Zorph was nervous-he held Woogie in high regard so apparently did the host of ghoublins under his leadership. He was most insistent we should be cool, and calm. Woogie just wanted to talk, he kept saying.

“You betcha” I affirmed “I mean you have been fair and helped us a great deal. No baby eating, right”?

There was none, Zorph agreed as he took us into a passage we hadn’t noticed before. I ghoublin stood a score of paces in front of us, with a pack behind him. Not ghoublins as it were, fullsized goblins. Woggie was..distinguished for someone not alive, with delicate if chewed up features. His manner seemed almost..academic (?) and he wore glasses just like Hans, which seemed a weird affectation. Did ghoublins suffer from poor vision?

“I am Woogie, leader of this group, and you are the ones who helped my new friend Zorph here, with no guile. So I would like to talk so that there are no hostilities between us and the town.”

Well, that would be good.

“We wish our current situation to remain unchanged. The town is not aware of us, and we will we will continue to stay out of their way, and not eat babies, or anything else living for that matter.” Zorph nodded. It was a good start.

“So I have taken the liberty of drawing up a contract, as civilized people do, wherein you will agree not to divulge our existence to the town and intercede with them on our behalf if something happens to reveal us. In turn, we shall continue as we have agreed. We intend on leaving this place soon, so your obligation will be brief. “

Well, that spared us the awkward conversation about the town invading this place because of the artifact, which was a good thing.

“Well, I am impressed with your observance of proper procedure” says Hans, “but I wish to add a condition that you and your group also don’t aid Ripnugget in his plot to murder the town. But if you agree, I cannot see any barrier.”

“Absolutely” I affirmed, “you guys are more civil than the folks at Iron Harbor, that’s for sure”. The others nodded.

“We can agree to that” replied Woogie. We have no use for Ripnugget. He is no longer on the board anyway, as they say. My spies tell me his band was attacked by a group of goblin alchemists and there was a great slaughter. And then the alchemists were massacred as well by some unknown actor. Bad business all the way around-we will go in a different direction and keep to ourselves.”

“Where do I sign? I said.

Woogie produced the contract, written in dark rust colored “ink” on something too yellowy and wrinkled to be parchment. I did not look at it closely.

Hans, ever careful, read the agreement to make sure we were not signing away our soul or some such, but all was as it should be. We all signed. I made sure not to touch the thing and used my own quill.

“Well” Woggie said “You are most civil yourselves, for living things anyway. We shall leave you to your business then. Farewell”

Zorph walked us out through the door we agreed we did not see, and we headed to the surface.
