Season 1, Episode 7: Dero in the Dark

Milani doesn’t say, so okey dokey, I decide to ask my companions.

Hans thinks Ragnulf has not done his duty and Zunk that he has made a stupid decision and will regret it. Tom shrugs “Who cares? Fuck that guy”. Well, 2 of 3, anyway! And it was not exactly the dwarf’s decision to get clawed to death so I take out my things and tend him. Ragnulf is curiously resigned when he recovers, muttering only “I knew you would not let me rest” and we continue on.

Down the hallway we find a large room with several forges attended by two rat-pig faced men. They grunt at us nastily and raise their hammers, which look like they will work on flesh as well as metal.

Tom and Zunk step up and wound one of the rat-pigs nearly before it can blink, and it lools rightly afraid. I am somewhat in the back and this time I decide to use my head instead of my brawn (that there is) and bestow Milan’s gift on my allies, granting them a truer strike. They glow a moonish soft silver for an instant; I am pleased to see it worked (I had never tried this before now).

Tom has taken a good blow from rat-pig, and Ragnulf’s hammer misses the thing and clangs off the anvil. Milani can only do so much it seems. Tom is occupied with the first one, and fells it with a clever backhand as I run at the other before it can threaten Zunk.

It is an evil thing and it recognizes my goodness and makes right to me all asnarl. My god grants me strength and I shrug off feeble blows. While I don’t hit it, I occupy it for Ragnulf who lands a solid strike. Electricity crackles on it from Hans. Little Zunk sees and opening low and takes it, while Tom swings high, the big blade cleaving into the thick neck. It slumps across the anvil, leaking blood onto the floor.

The way out is a long hallway with a padlocked door on one wall. Zunk breaks his pick on the stubborn thing, leaving me to bash it off, which I do but, clumsily. I am sure Vanda would have crushed it with one blow. It took me 5. Inside was a pile of rusted metal and a silver ingot, which we take. These people do not deserve nice things! They would only use it for bad ends.

Zunk takes the lead and heads south down a hallway to a half open door. We are well back to not have our light interfere with the sneaking, but not so far as we cannot hear him say softly “Ooops”. So we rush forward.

Tom is in the fore and I see him draw his blade and round the door. When I get there I see a blue beastie sort of thing with a nasty mouth and what looks like a boggard only bluish and less friendly. It smells of undeath. Tom has wounded the beastie and Zunk dives behind Tom to pop up behind the zombie boggard and sever its spine, while I rush the beastie and swing as hard as I can. I brain it good and just as I think it’s going down, it savages me with teeth and claws. Holy smokes that hurt. Like really hurt. But then I am in bright light and it’s beautiful and there oh! There is Milani smiling at me and..and..

And I am on bloody ground with a vial to my lips and Zunk’s face before me.

“Hey Mum” he slurs, or maybe it is my hearing “you had me worried. Can’t loose my carryall, eh?” The blue beastie is dead, still smoking slightly from Han’s lightning, head stove in. Oh, right.

“uhh, yesh. Thank you, friend,” I slur. “I’m uhh, fine. Just gonna sit here and tend my wounds for a minute.” I still wasn’t sure entirely what happened, but I think I was gone there for a time.

Eventually we move on. I feel better, Zunk having tended to me as well, but things were a little blurry and muzzy. We find a door and inside nothing but rusted metal. But also a clever trap of the sort we found in the Temple entrance, but this time the blades would spring forth from the ceiling. Some time passed as Hans repaired Zunk’s broken pick and then Zunk disarmed the trap. I was glad for the rest, to be honest.

In the room were a couple of acolytes, Tom killed the first and Ragnulf took out his fury on the second, which I was abstractly glad about. I really wasn’t paying much attention.

We decide to return to the bogards to tell them we have removed the threat, human and non. Zunk takes a small trinket worn by the zombie boggard to explain to the others that we released their kin from undeath and head back.

The Queen was there when we arrived and listened to our tale. I was not entirely sure she believed us until we got to the part with the blue beast (an akata, according to Hans, from a different plane, whatever that means). The boggards had run into that thing and fled, with the unfortunate zombie we set down being the slowest of the bunch and so taken. The trinkets confirmed our story, and she became grateful as the truth of our actions was confirmed.

While most appreciative, she actually knew little of help. Only the location of a secret door that lead eventually to the same room with stairs down, where we fought the pigs, or the splitfaced men, or maybe the akata. I honestly don’t recall but in any case, we already knew how to get there. She did tell us that was the way to an underground lake the boggards cavort in, but also there is a big scary door they leave alone because of what the humans are doing behind it. Obviously. She gives us some little bone trinkets that will signal to any other boggards we might meet, that we are friends of the Black Lake clan, which is sweet. She really is trying to help and drags out some items from what the clan has salvaged. Among vials of nasty looking stuff there is a wand I recognize (I saw it once in the hands of a traveling medic)-a very useful wand of healing! Queen agrees to trade it to us for a more than fair price, and we give her some of the things we’ve found along the way. Hans (obviously) does the accounting.

We debate what to do next, go up or down. Hans is of the mind that sacrifices are coming from above, even while admitting there could be many other sources of the magical energy being directed below. “Look, ja, I have made a chart and on balance I think going up is a better choice and I feel like some fresh air would be good for us, ja?”

*Damned foreshadowing gave us tunnel vision.

**Good for him. Less good for our appetite.

But my instincts say to go down and when I say this Hans offers. “Well ja, but the possibility the sacrifice being above means we should of course consider this, ja. So maybe we should go up”? And I say I still think we should go down. And it goes like that for a while until I shout in frustration “Well fine! Up! Lets go up if that’s what you want”. (“Easy Mum” whispers Zunk “Take a breath. You just got your bell rung pretty hard, not good to get the blood up so”).

And then Hans does something I have seen before, generally from men. Talking to women. He denies suggesting what everyone knew he was suggesting and rather, that he was just trying to point out that blah blah. I was breathing, and when the Hansplaining was done, I slowly and distinctly said. “Down. I think we should go down. I would like to go down, and so can we please just head down?”

Ragnulf looked up from cleaning the blood off his hammer. “I would listen to her, lads. Sounds like Vanda when she is done discussing” Really? Holy smokes. I tried not to smile but was only partially successful.

There was no further commentary, so we headed back to the room with the stairs and followed them down into a large opening. Naturally, I lost my step and clanged down like rickety potsmith’s wagon, alerting the two splitfaced beings below. Stupid girl!

Zunk slides towards one of the horrors and a red streak opens on one of the monster’s legs, while Tom swings and opens another gash. I swing and feel a crunch of bone and flesh, and and claes rakes my shoulder. I see Hans take out his wand and point, and the ground beneath the two splitfaces takes on a oily sheen. The things lose their balance and fall, clawing for purchase on the suddenly slippery ground. Zunk reverses his daggers and plunges them into splitface and is splattered by a gout of blood as the monster takes a last rattling breath. The other one has managed to stand, avoiding Tom’s blade and strikes at me with claws and teeth, but by Milani’s grace I deflect the blows. Tom’s blade goes through a shoulder and I swing up, the spikey ball connecting with the splitface’s chin and tearing the slavering head from its body. Luckily the blood misses me entirely. Holy smokes, did I just do that?

We take a breath and look around. We’re in a chamber brightly lit by flickering flames inset into small silver-lined nooks, with a grand ceiling and paintings on the wall visible by the soft and even light. Finely crafted depictions of a horned and hooved giant in rusted full plate accomplishing great tasks of utter destruction, with the last in the series showing some sort of ritual. Ugh..why is there always some nasty ceremony? Part of the wall in the southeast corner has caved in somewhat, but the hallways exiting the chamber to the north and south are still intact. To the west, a massive fifteen-foot-wide iron door seals the passage beyond; sharp spikes and blades of rusted metal cover the entire surface of the door.

Hans says “Ja, that does not look very inviting. I suggest we make a chart about the pros and cons here, ja? Con-what is beyond door is very likely to kill us. Pros anyone?”

While this is going on, Zunk slides carefully through the rockfall, picking his way into deeper darkness. We all slide a little in that direction, now quiet, but not wanting to go further for fear of alerting anything. Or maybe that was just me.

He slips back, silent as a mouse, a moment later.

“uh well” he says softly. “Somekinda funny fellow there, among a pile of dross, muttering to himself and picking up things and breaking ‘em. Not makin any sense at all, breakin is all he seems to want to do. I don’t think he’s dangerous, but I don’t much like the looks of him, either”

We look at the rockstrewn tunnel but no threat emerges. “Okey dokey. Maybe if we just head the other way maybe it’ll leave us alone?”. There seems to be the unspoken agreement to leave the spikey door for now so we go north, finding a door that leads to a well appointed room, recently slept in, with a chest and a rapier leaning on the desk.

“Aye my little box, what have ye to offer Zunk?” as he takes out his tools and begins to work. A little click, an “oops” from Zunk, and oh, what’s this? Little metal hissing snakes appear suddenly and make towards us with little metal teeth that are quite sharp; Zunk is bleeding from multiple bites.

I flail uselessly at the squirming things, while Tom flattens one. It explodes a moment later spraying metal bits everywhere, bloodying Tom and little Zunk, who is not looking so good. Hans fires off his lightning, and the things squirm and twitch violently. Another spell might take them out given the damage done by his first, so I mash one (just to be on the safe side) and step back. Ragnulf and the other are in the far corner and I hope the dwarf is thinking along with me, but he keeps hammering at the metal serpent at his feet. Another jolt from Hans and the things convulse and are still. I quickly grab Zunk and run into the hallway before they detonate. I feel bad leaving Ragnulf but there’s naught I can do and I don’t think Zunk can take another hit.

“Aye, like wot I said, Mum” says Zunk as metal bits shred the door “what would I do without my carryall, eh”?

The chest is now safe to open and its contents are unsurprising for a merchant. The rapier, while well crafted is nothing unusual. On the desk there is a key and a book written in Thalassonian, which Hans wants badly to look at. But there is one more door at the end of the hallway that we need to check first.

It is locked, but the key we found on the desk slides in easy and the door open.

Ordwi rises from the small bed and says “I knew you would find me. Where is Meitremar? And what is he doing here?
